The most common beginner complaint is not knowing where a "good spot" even is, and having nobody to ask. Florida makes that easier than most states. FWC runs nearly 80 Fish Management Areas (FMAs), and many are small lakes and ponds in city parks near Jacksonville, Orlando, Tampa and Miami. FWC stocks them with channel catfish and sunshine bass, sinks brush or PVC fish attractors marked with buoys, and puts in fish feeders that draw catfish and sunfish to the bank.

Add Florida's public piers, where a pier license can cover your saltwater fishing, and you can catch fish within half an hour of most Floridians' front doors.

## Before you go: three rules beginners miss

1. **Under 16 fishes free.** Everyone 16 and older needs a license unless exempt. Florida residents 65 and older are exempt with proof of age.
2. **FMAs need a license even with a cane pole.** Florida residents normally get a cane-pole exemption in their home county, but not on Fish Management Areas, where residents 16 to 64 and non-residents 16 and up need a license by any method.
3. **The fish decides the license.** Pond or lake fish need a freshwater license; anything from a pier on the coast needs a saltwater license, unless the pier's license covers you. Prices and exemptions are on the [Florida fishing license page](/fishing/florida/fishing-license).

## Jacksonville and Northeast Florida

### 1. Hanna Park ponds (Duval County)

FWC-managed ponds in Hanna Park, a Jacksonville city park on the ocean. Open from 30 minutes before sunrise to 30 minutes after sunset. No gas motors, no cast nets, no alcohol. Limits: 20 panfish and 6 channel catfish a day. Good for bream and catfish from the bank.

### 2. Jacksonville Beach Pier

Reopened in July 2022 after six years of hurricane repairs. As of WJCT's last report, fishing cost $5 a day ($4 for Duval County residents and hotel guests) and the pier was open 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. Ask at the gate whether the fee includes pier license coverage for your saltwater fishing.

### 3. Ronnie Vanzant Park (Clay County)

A family-only FMA southwest of Jacksonville: **no one 16 or older may fish unless accompanied by an angler under 16.** Hook and line only, no boats, no swimming. Panfish 20, channel catfish 6. Exactly the kind of place to take a kid for a first fish without competing with serious anglers.

## Orlando and Central Florida

FWC's Fish Orlando program has run since 1998, managing city lakes for quality fishing in an urban setting.

### 4. Lake Ivanhoe (Orlando)

The best bank lake in Orlando. FWC says its narrow band of shoreline vegetation means bass holding 8 to 15 feet deep next to submerged grass are within a 20- to 50-foot cast from the bank. Cast nets prohibited; panfish 20, channel catfish 6.

### 5. Turkey Lake (Orlando)

Boat access is limited by deed restrictions, but FWC, the City of Orlando and Bass Pro Shops Orlando run a **boat loaner program** here. Bass are catch-and-release only. No gas motors except for sanctioned events; panfish 20, channel catfish 6.

### 6. Lake Lawne (Orlando)

An FMA that holds a Florida record: the 9.44-pound Florida gar, caught in 2001. Cast nets prohibited, channel catfish 6. Gar, bream and catfish from the bank.

### 7. Harris Chain piers (Leesburg)

FWC lists public fishing piers at **Venetian Gardens** and **Singletary Park** on Lake Harris, **Herlong Park** on Lake Griffin in Leesburg, and **Lake Griffin State Park** in Fruitland Park. In winter these are speck spots; Lake Griffin crappie must be 10 inches to keep. The chain is covered in detail on the [best lakes page](/fishing/florida/best-lakes).

## Tampa Bay

### 8. Al Lopez Park Lake (Tampa)

A small FMA lake in a big city park. Cast nets and minnow seines prohibited; panfish 20, channel catfish 6; no swimming. Bream on worms around the edges, catfish on the bottom.

### 9. Walsingham Park Lake (Pinellas County)

A Pinellas County park lake with no gas motors. Limits: 20 bluegill and redear combined, 4 sunshine bass (10-inch minimum), 6 channel catfish. Sunshine bass are a hard-pulling surprise for a first-timer on a small spinning rod.

### 10. Skyway Fishing Pier State Park (St. Petersburg)

The remains of the old Sunshine Skyway bridge, now a state park pier on Tampa Bay, **open 24 hours a day, 365 days a year**. Florida State Parks lists a $4 per vehicle fee plus a $4 per person fishing fee, and says **the fishing fee covers your fishing license for 24 hours.** The park also requires a short online course, renewed annually, to fish the pier. Check current fees and the course link with the park before you drive.

## South Florida

### 11. Tropical Park Lake (Miami-Dade)

The northernmost lake in Tropical Park is an FMA. Panfish 20, channel catfish 6, and bluegill and redear under 8 inches go back. Miami-Dade and Broward canals nearby hold butterfly peacock bass, which bite only in daylight.

### 12. Plantation Heritage Park Lake (Broward)

Same rules as Tropical Park: panfish 20, channel catfish 6, bream under 8 inches released.

### 13. Lake Okeeheelee and Juno Beach Pier (Palm Beach County)

**Lake Okeeheelee** in West Palm Beach is an FMA with no gas motors; panfish 20, catfish 6, bream under 8 inches released. For salt water, the **Juno Beach Pier** runs about 990 feet into the Atlantic and is managed by Loggerhead Marinelife Center. The county reported its T-section closed for repairs after high surf, so call 561-855-6185 before going.

## Tallahassee and the Panhandle

### 14. Lake Piney Z (Tallahassee) and Navarre Beach Fishing Pier

**Lake Piney Z** is a City of Tallahassee FMA: panfish 20, channel catfish 6, no swimming, no cast nets, and no access between sunset and sunrise. For the Gulf, the **Navarre Beach Fishing Pier** is 1,545 feet long, the longest fishing pier in Florida, rebuilt in concrete in 2010 after Hurricane Ivan destroyed the old wooden one. Ask at the pier house whether its fee includes pier license coverage.

## Free fishing days

On license-free days anyone can fish without a license; all other rules apply.

- **Saltwater:** Saturday, **November 28, 2026** (the Saturday after Thanksgiving), then the first weekend in June 2027.
- **Freshwater:** **April 3–4, 2027** and **June 12–13, 2027**.

A free day is the perfect test before you buy gear and a license.

## Simple rigs that catch Florida fish

**Bream (bluegill, redear):** a light spinning rod, 6-pound line, a small float, a split shot and a size 8 or 10 hook. Bait with crickets or a piece of worm and set it 2 to 4 feet under the float near weeds, docks or the fish feeder. FWC says redear bite best on worms around the March and April full moons.

**Catfish in urban ponds:** a sliding sinker above a swivel, 18 inches of leader and a size 2 circle hook with cut bait or shrimp on the bottom. Feeders draw catfish; fish within casting distance of them, not on top.

**Bass from the bank:** a 7-inch plastic worm, weightless or with a light bullet weight, cast along grass edges at dawn and dusk. Florida bass have no minimum size, but only one over 16 inches per day may be kept, and several urban lakes are catch-and-release.

**Pier fishing:** live or frozen shrimp on a 1/4-ounce jig head, or on a bottom rig with a pyramid sinker. Ask the bait shop what's biting that week; they know.

<p class="callout"><strong>Tip:</strong> Summer afternoons in Florida mean heat and lightning. Fish at sunrise, bring more water than you think, and leave the pier or bank when you hear thunder.</p>

## Family tips

- **Pick a pond with a feeder.** Constant bites keep kids interested. Big fish can wait.
- **Learn to release fish well.** Wet your hands, support the fish and get it back fast; see [how to release a fish](/blog/how-to-release-a-fish).
- **Know your fish.** Bream, bass and catfish from a pond; drum, sheepshead and snook from a pier, each with its own saltwater limits. Our [bluegill](/species/bluegill) and [channel catfish](/species/channel-catfish) guides help with ID.
- **Look into FWC Fish Camps.** FWC's youth conservation network runs Fish Camps around the state with casting, fish ID and boating safety lessons. Email FYCCN@MyFWC.com to find one.
