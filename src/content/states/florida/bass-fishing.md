Ask a Florida bass angler what's different here and you'll hear about grass, shiners and giants. FWC's data adds detail: when personal bests actually happen, which counties produce them, and what they're caught on. This page puts that data to work, month by month.

## The bass you'll catch in Florida

| Species | Where | Rules | State record |
|---|---|---|---|
| Florida bass | Native statewide; pure south of the Suwannee River | No minimum length | 17.27 lb (with largemouth) |
| Largemouth bass (northern) | Intergrades in north Florida and the western Panhandle | Same as Florida bass | 17.27 lb (combined record) |
| Suwannee bass | Mainly the Suwannee and Santa Fe rivers | 12" minimum; release on the Wakulla River | 3.89 lb |
| Shoal bass | Apalachicola River system, rocky streams | 12" minimum; release on the Chipola River | 5.95 lb |
| Spotted and Choctaw bass | Panhandle rivers | 12" minimum | 3.93 lb |
| Butterfly peacock bass | South Florida canals (Miami-Dade, Broward) | 2 per day, 1 at 17"+ | 9.11 lb |

**Florida bass vs largemouth.** In 2023 the American Fisheries Society split the former largemouth into two species, based largely on a 2022 genetics study. FWC now uses *Florida bass* (*Micropterus salmoides*) for the native fish across most of the state, and *largemouth bass* (*Micropterus nigricans*) for the northern fish. You can't tell them apart by looking; only a genetic test can. Regulations, TrophyCatch and the state record treat them as one.

What the split changes for Florida: stocking northern largemouth is prohibited. They're on FWC's conditional nonnative species list, and only genetically authenticated pure Florida bass may be stocked or brought into the state. The fish that grows to 15 pounds in Florida grass is protected on purpose.

**The little river bass.** Suwannee, shoal, spotted and Choctaw bass rarely top 4 or 5 pounds, but they're Florida specialties and fun on light tackle. The newest record is a 3.93-pound spotted bass caught by Joe Durden from a backwater slough off the Apalachicola River during a June 2025 tournament, beating a 40-year-old mark.

**Peacocks.** Butterfly peacock bass are nonnative but managed as a game fish. FWC says they're mainly in Broward and Miami-Dade canals, have spread to Palm Beach County in mild winters, and bite in daylight only. They hit hard and fight harder than a Florida bass of the same size.

## Month by month: the Florida bass calendar

Florida bass spawn early, and the spawn moves north with the calendar. FWC says spawning can begin in January in extreme South Florida and run into May in the Panhandle, with March and April the peak. In Central Florida lakes, FWC's Tenoroc page puts the start in February or March, when water reaches roughly 58 to 65°F.

| Month | South and Central Florida | North Florida and Panhandle |
|---|---|---|
| January | First beds in South Florida; big pre-spawn females in the grass | Cold-front lull; fish slow on deep edges |
| February | Spawn waves on warm, stable days around the full moon | Pre-spawn staging on points and creek mouths |
| March | Peak spawn and post-spawn; PB month | Spawn starting as water climbs past 60°F |
| April | Late spawners, post-spawn and bream-bed feeding | Peak spawn on Talquin and the rivers |
| May | Post-spawn; topwater early, offshore grass by midday | Last spawners in the Panhandle |
| June–August | Heat. Dawn topwater, then offshore hydrilla, eelgrass and mats | Same, plus river current and shade |
| September | Hottest water of the year; night and early fishing | Early signs of fall feeding |
| October | Shad and baitfish feeding, schooling fish | Fall transition on the rivers |
| November | Pre-spawn fish move to the outer grass edges | Good fall bite until fronts arrive |
| December | Heavy pre-spawn females, especially on Okeechobee and Kissimmee | Fronts dictate everything |

FWC's TrophyCatch angler survey sums it up: March and April produced more than 40% of personal bests, and almost 10 times as many as late-summer August or September. If you can only take one Florida bass trip, take it in March.

Weather matters more than the date. A strong front can shut down a shallow Florida lake for two days; read [fishing after rain, wind and cold fronts](/blog/fishing-after-rain-wind-cold-front) and [barometric pressure and fishing](/blog/barometric-pressure-and-fishing) before you plan around one.

## Lures and baits that work in Florida

FWC's personal-best survey settles an old argument. Live golden shiners have the reputation, but **78% of PBs were caught on artificial lures and 22% on live bait**. Plastic worms led, followed by other soft plastics, topwater lures and swimbaits.

What that looks like in a Florida rod locker:

- **Plastic worm, Texas-rigged.** A 7- to 10-inch ribbon-tail or straight-tail worm on a 3/16- to 1/4-ounce weight, worked along grass edges and in pockets. Dark colors like junebug in stained water, green pumpkin in clear.
- **Punching rig.** A compact creature bait under a 1- to 1.5-ounce weight, punched through hydrilla and hyacinth mats in summer and on cold-front days when fish bury up.
- **Hollow-body frog and buzzbait.** Pads, mats and early mornings from May through October.
- **Lipless crankbait and vibrating jig.** FWC names lipless cranks, speed worms and vibrating jigs for Toho's offshore hydrilla. Rip a 1/2-ounce lipless free of the grass in winter and early spring.
- **Swimbait and swim jig.** Along the outer edge of eelgrass and bulrush on Okeechobee and Kissimmee, especially when shad are around.
- **Wild shiners.** Free-lined or under a float along grass lines in winter. Several lakes (Orange, Lochloosa, Fellsmere) require circle hooks with natural bait longer than 3 inches, and it's good practice everywhere you plan to release fish.

## Florida bass regulations

Statewide, from FWC's 2026–2027 rules:

- **5 black bass per day** (all six species combined), **only one 16 inches or longer**. No minimum length for Florida or largemouth bass.
- Suwannee, shoal, spotted and Choctaw bass under 12 inches may not be kept. Shoal bass on the Chipola River and Suwannee bass on the Wakulla River must be released.
- Keep bass whole (no filleting, no removing head or tail) until you're done fishing for the day.
- Black bass and peacock bass may not be used as bait.
- Possession limit is two days' bag.

**Catch-and-release waters** include Stick Marsh/Farm 13, the Fellsmere Water Management Area, Kenansville Lake (S.N. Knight Tract), Wildcat Lake, and several Fish Management Areas such as Turkey Lake and Lake Underhill in Orlando and the Tenoroc and Mosaic FMAs. **Orange and Lochloosa lakes** require releasing bass 20 inches and longer. **Lake Seminole** and the **St. Marys River** allow 10 bass, each at least 12 inches.

**Trophy exceptions:** a TrophyCatch angler may temporarily hold one Florida or largemouth bass of 8 pounds or more over the limits to photograph it, then must release it alive in the same water right away. An angler with a possible state record bass of 16.5 pounds or more may keep it over the limit while contacting FWC for certification.

## TrophyCatch: FWC's trophy bass program

TrophyCatch is a citizen-science program that rewards anglers for documenting and releasing big bass.

| Tier | Weight |
|---|---|
| Lunker Club | 8 to 9.9 lb |
| Trophy Club | 10 to 12.9 lb |
| Hall of Fame | 13 lb and up |

**How to submit:** a photo or video of the whole fish on a scale, with the weight readable, taken before release. You need a current license or exemption. Catches from public or private water in Florida count. Hall of Fame fish earn a bonus $50 Bass Pro Shops e-gift card if you add a length photo. Season 15 runs October 1, 2026 to September 30, 2027.

**By the numbers:** FWC's 2026–2027 guide counts more than 17,000 documented and released bass, 176 of them in the Hall of Fame. Since 2011, FWC biologists have also tagged 8-pound-plus bass with $100 reward tags. As of late October 2026, the Season 15 leader was a 10-pound, 14-ounce bass from the Withlacoochee River.

<p class="callout"><strong>Tip:</strong> No scale? FWC's formula is length squared times girth, divided by 1,200, in inches. A 22-inch bass with a 15-inch girth is about 6.1 pounds. Our <a href="/tools/fish-weight-calculator">fish weight calculator</a> does the math, but TrophyCatch still needs the scale photo.</p>

## Handle big bass the way FWC's research says

FWC and the University of Florida tested how trophy bass recover from different holds. A two-handed, fully supported horizontal hold had fish righting themselves in under 10 seconds on average. A vertical hold with a grip device took about 33 seconds. A one-handed, tilted jaw hold averaged about 12 seconds but often left fish shaking their heads and adjusting their jaws before they settled. None caused permanent damage, but the supported horizontal hold was clearly easiest on the fish. Get the photo fast, support the belly, and see [how to release a fish](/blog/how-to-release-a-fish).

A TrophyCatch submission and a personal record need the same thing: a clear photo on a scale or board. PB Fishing keeps that photo with the length, weight, water and conditions for every catch and tracks your personal best per species. Its Florida leaderboard shows angler name, state, length and weight, never the lake, so a big fish can rank without giving away where it lives.

## Eating bass in Florida

The Florida Department of Health's basic guideline for untested fresh waters: **do not eat Florida or largemouth bass longer than 16 inches**, and limit smaller bass to one meal a month. Bowfin and gar are on the avoid list too. Search the DOH advisory database for your lake before you keep a limit. The statewide one-over-16-inches rule and the health advice point the same way: keep a couple of small ones if you want, release the big ones.
