"When should I fish in Florida?" has two answers. The calendar answer is below, built from FWC's seasonal calendar, its species forecasts and the 2026 saltwater rules. The real answer is "on the stable days within the right month," because a cold front in February or a heat wave in May will rewrite the bite overnight. Use the table to pick the month and the [best time to fish](/blog/best-time-to-fish) guide to pick the day.

A note on Florida's climate: South Florida runs about a month ahead of the Panhandle. A spawn that starts in January around Okeechobee may not start until April on Lake Talquin.

## Florida fishing calendar, month by month

| Month | Fresh water: what bites, where | Salt water | Closures to know |
|---|---|---|---|
| January | Specks peak on Okeechobee; South Florida bass start bedding; stripers below Jackson Bluff Dam | Redfish and trout on warm-water flats | Snook closed statewide |
| February | Bass pre-spawn and spawn in Central Florida; specks on Lochloosa around the full moon; shad runs on the St. Johns | Snook open on the Atlantic side | Spotted seatrout closed in the Panhandle |
| March | Peak bass spawn; redear spawn on the full moon; Talquin specks | Snook open statewide; sheepshead | Sheepshead 50-per-vessel limit in March and April |
| April | Bass spawn moves north; redear and early bluegill beds; big channel cats start | Snook open statewide | |
| May | Post-spawn bass on topwater at dawn; bluegill beds; channel cats | Snook open on the Atlantic only | Snook closed on the Gulf side |
| June | Bluegill beds; big channel cats; bass offshore by mid-morning | Redfish, trout; snook catch-and-release | Snook closed statewide |
| July | Early and late only; bream and catfish | Same | Snook closed statewide |
| August | Hottest month for anglers; night and dawn fishing | Same | Snook closed statewide |
| September | Bass begin feeding as days shorten | Snook reopens (except Charlotte Harbor and Southwest) | |
| October | Stripers leave summer refuges on the St. Johns, gather at bridges and jetties | Snook open statewide | Flounder closed from Oct. 15 |
| November | Specks pick up on Okeechobee; pre-spawn bass on outer grass | Snook open statewide | Flounder closed; seatrout closed in the Indian River Lagoon |
| December | Specks and heavy pre-spawn bass; stripers | Snook open on the Atlantic until Dec. 14 | Flounder reopens Dec. 1; IRL seatrout closed; snook closed on the Gulf |

The freshwater calendar is the same for residents and visitors. If you're crossing between fresh and salt, remember your license has to match the fish ([how Florida licenses work](/fishing/florida/fishing-license)).

## Spawning periods at a glance

FWC's temperature guide lists approximate spawning water temperatures. These are what turn the calendar on:

| Species | Spawning water temperature | Florida timing |
|---|---|---|
| Florida bass | 62–75°F (starts around 58–65°F in Central Florida) | January (south) to May (Panhandle); peak March–April |
| Black crappie | 62–65°F | Late winter, January to March in most lakes |
| Redear sunfish | 70–80°F | Full moons of March and April |
| Bluegill | 75–85°F | All summer |
| Channel catfish | 72–82°F | Late spring into summer |

For the physiology behind those numbers, see [water temperature and fish activity](/blog/water-temperature-and-fish-activity).

## Freshwater species

### Florida bass

Best months: **December through April**, peak **March and April**. FWC's survey of TrophyCatch anglers found those two months produced more than 40% of personal-best bass, and almost 10 times more than August or September. Summer bass are still catchable on offshore hydrilla and mats, early. Limit: 5 per day, only one 16 inches or longer. Everything else is on the [Florida bass page](/fishing/florida/bass-fishing).

### Black crappie (specks)

Best months: **November through April**. On Lake Okeechobee, FWC says the bite picks up in November and December and January gives the best catch of keeper-size specks. On Lake Talquin it's January through April. On Lochloosa, FWC says the full moons of February and March "usually produce memorable fishing trips." In cool months, drift or troll minnows and jigs over offshore vegetation; as water warms, fish move shallow to spawn in bulrush, cattails and lily pads. Limit: 25 per day, with a 10-inch minimum on Okeechobee, Talquin and Lake Griffin. More on the species in our [crappie guide](/species/crappie).

### Redear sunfish (shellcracker)

Best months: **March through May**, peak around the **full moons of March and April**, per FWC, when spawning activity peaks. Earthworms on the bottom near beds are the classic bait. At Deer Point Lake in the Panhandle, FWC says shellcracker are best April through June. Merritts Mill Pond produced the 4.86-pound state record in March 1986.

### Bluegill

Best months: **April through September**. Bluegill spawn all summer in colonies, and FWC notes you can find 30 to 40 nest holes scooped out in one shallow area. Crickets and worms under a small float. Panfish limit is 50 per day combined statewide, 20 in many Fish Management Areas. See the [bluegill guide](/species/bluegill).

### Channel catfish

Best months: **April through June** for big channel cats statewide, per FWC's seasonal calendar. Urban Fish Management Area ponds are stocked with channel catfish and fish feeders draw them in. No statewide bag limit, but most FMAs cap channel catfish at 6 per day. See the [channel catfish guide](/species/channel-catfish).

### Blue and flathead catfish

Best months: **April through November** in the Northwest region rivers, per FWC. Both are nonnative, both now hold records over 69 pounds (blue 73.60 lb, flathead 69.90 lb), and both came from Panhandle and Big Bend rivers.

### Striped bass and sunshine bass

Best months: **October through April**. FWC says the best striper fishing is fall through spring and that stripers can't tolerate water over 75°F for long, so in summer they hide in cool springs and deep holes. On the lower St. Johns they leave those thermal refuges in mid-to-late October and gather around bridge pilings, jetties and channel markers. Below Jackson Bluff Dam on the Ochlockonee the run is late fall through spring. Limit: 20 striped, white and sunshine bass combined, only 6 at 24 inches or longer, with tighter rules on the Suwannee, the lower Ochlockonee and Lake Seminole.

### Butterfly peacock bass

Best months: **warm months, daylight only**. FWC's calendar notes peacocks only bite in daylight. They live mainly in Broward and Miami-Dade canals and have turned up as far north as Palm Beach County after mild winters. Limit: 2 per day, only one 17 inches or longer.

### American shad

Best months: **winter**, during the upstream spawning run on the St. Johns. Both tied state records (5.19 lb) were caught in February and March. Limit: 10 shad and herring combined, hook and line only.

## Saltwater species

All saltwater rules below are FWC's 2026 rules. FWC's 2026 guide flags changes to the seatrout over-slot rule for 2026–2027, so check MyFWC.com before you keep fish.

### Snook

Slot: **28–33 inches** (Gulf regions) or **28–32 inches** (Southeast, Indian River Lagoon, Northeast). Bag: 1. Snook permit required with a saltwater license.

| Region | Open months |
|---|---|
| Panhandle, Big Bend, Tampa Bay, Sarasota Bay | March–April, September–November |
| Charlotte Harbor, Southwest | March–April, October–November |
| Southeast, Indian River Lagoon, Northeast | February–May, September 1–December 14 |

June through August is closed everywhere, so summer snook go back.

### Red drum (redfish)

Open year-round. Slot **18–27 inches**, 1 per angler. Vessel limit 4 in the Panhandle, Big Bend and Northeast; 2 in Tampa Bay, Sarasota Bay, Charlotte Harbor, Southwest and Southeast. **The Indian River Lagoon is catch-and-release only.** Harvest in federal waters is prohibited.

### Spotted seatrout

Slot **15–19 inches**. Bag 5 in the Big Bend, 2 in the Indian River Lagoon, 3 elsewhere. **Closed in the Panhandle in February** and **in the Indian River Lagoon from November 1 to December 31.**

### Flounder, sheepshead and the rest

- **Flounder:** 14-inch minimum, 5 per angler, **closed October 15 through November 30**.
- **Sheepshead:** 12-inch minimum, 8 per angler, with a 50-fish vessel limit during March and April.
- **Black drum:** 14–24 inch slot, 5 per angler, one over 24 inches allowed.
- **Pompano:** 11-inch fork-length minimum, 6 per angler.
- **Mullet:** 50 per angler; vessel limit 100 from February through August, 50 from September through January.

## Always closed or protected

- **Shoal bass** on the Chipola River and **Suwannee bass** on the Wakulla River (headsprings to the St. Marks): release immediately.
- **Sturgeon** (Atlantic, Gulf, shortnose) and **alligator gar**: release immediately; possession is illegal without a permit.
- **Catch-and-release bass waters** such as Stick Marsh/Farm 13 and Fellsmere, year-round.
- **No fishing** within 50 yards of Silver Glen and Salt springs, and from the Rainbow Springs headwaters to a mile downstream in Marion County.

## Putting it together

If you have one week a year to fish Florida, make it the second half of March: Florida bass on beds or just off them, redear on the full moon, snook open on both coasts, and specks still biting in North Florida. If you live here, keep a log; within a season you'll know which months and conditions your own waters actually fish best, which is worth more than any calendar.
