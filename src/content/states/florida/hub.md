Florida sells itself as the "Fishing Capital of the World," and FWC's own regulations guide uses that line without irony. It has earned it. Few states let you throw a frog for a 10-pound bass in the morning and soak shrimp for redfish off a pier the same evening. But that mix is also what trips people up: different seasons, different limits, and two different licenses depending on what bites.

This hub is the short version. Each section links to a deeper Florida page.

## What makes Florida fishing different

**The bass are a different fish.** In 2023 the American Fisheries Society split the old largemouth bass into two species. Across most of Florida the native fish is now the **Florida bass** (*Micropterus salmoides*), while the fish most of the country calls largemouth is *Micropterus nigricans*. FWC says pure Florida bass live south of the Suwannee River, with crossbred intergrades in the north and the western Panhandle. You can't tell them apart by eye, regulations are identical, and both count for the state record. What matters to you: Florida bass are the strain that grows giants, and they're why people fly here to chase a double-digit fish. More on that in the [Florida bass fishing guide](/fishing/florida/bass-fishing).

**The lakes are shallow and weedy.** Florida's famous bass lakes are mostly flat-bottomed, vegetated natural lakes rather than deep canyon reservoirs. You fish hydrilla edges, eelgrass, bulrush, lily pads and floating mats. Depth finders matter less than reading grass.

**There's no off-season, just a different season.** Bass spawn as early as January in extreme South Florida and as late as May in the Panhandle, according to FWC. Specks (black crappie) bite best in winter. Summer is for early mornings, bream beds and catfish.

**Licenses follow the fish.** FWC's rule is that licensing "follows the species of fish you are fishing for, regardless of where you are fishing." Fish for bass in a tidal river with only a freshwater license and hook a redfish, and you have to let the redfish go. The [Florida fishing license guide](/fishing/florida/fishing-license) covers the combinations.

## Top species in Florida

| Species | Where | Best time | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Florida bass | Statewide lakes, rivers, canals | Jan–Apr spawn; fall pre-spawn | 5 per day, only 1 at 16" or longer |
| Black crappie (specks) | Okeechobee, Talquin, Istokpoga, Harris Chain | Nov–Apr | 25 per day statewide; 10" minimum on Okeechobee and Talquin |

| Snook, redfish, seatrout | Coast, bays, piers | Region-specific seasons | Saltwater license plus snook permit |

## Best regions at a glance

- **South Florida:** Lake Okeechobee, roughly 730 square miles and, per FWC, the second largest body of fresh water in the contiguous US. Bass in the grass, specks in winter. Peacock bass in the Miami-Dade and Broward canals.
- **Central Florida:** the Kissimmee Chain (Lake Kissimmee at 34,948 acres, Lake Toho at 18,810), the Harris Chain around Leesburg, and the Orlando urban lakes. FWC data from TrophyCatch puts Lake, Osceola and Polk counties among the top five for personal-best bass.
- **Northeast and North Central:** Rodman Reservoir, Lake George and the St. Johns River, Orange and Lochloosa lakes. Putnam County, home to Rodman, is another top-five PB county.
- **Panhandle:** Lake Talquin for specks and stripers, Lake Seminole on the Georgia line, and rivers like the Apalachicola and Chipola for shoal bass, spotted bass and big catfish.

The ranked list with sizes, access and regulations is on the [best lakes in Florida page](/fishing/florida/best-lakes).

## Seasons at a glance

- **December to April:** specks and pre-spawn and spawning bass. This is trophy season. FWC's survey of TrophyCatch anglers found March and April produced more than 40% of personal-best bass, almost 10 times more than August or September.
- **May to September:** heat. Fish early and late, work offshore grass for bass, bed bream around the spring full moons, chase big channel catfish.
- **October to November:** stripers move to bridges and jetties on the St. Johns, bass feed up ahead of the spawn, Lake Okeechobee's speck bite starts.

If you want the "why" behind the bite, read [water temperature and fish activity](/blog/water-temperature-and-fish-activity) and [the best time to fish](/blog/best-time-to-fish).

## The license in two lines

Residents: **$17** a year freshwater, **$17** saltwater, **$32.50** for both; the saltwater shoreline license is **free** for residents who fish only from land. Non-residents: **$47** a year, or **$17** for 3 days and **$30** for 7 days, per license type. Everything else, including exemptions, free fishing days and penalties, is on the [license page](/fishing/florida/fishing-license).

## Records and trophy programs

The Florida bass state record is **17.27 pounds**, caught by Billy O'Berry in Polk County in 1986, and it still stands. FWC's TrophyCatch program rewards anglers who photograph and release bass of 8 pounds or more; the 2026–2027 regulations guide counts 17,000+ documented bass, 176 of them over 13 pounds. The full list of freshwater records, and how to get one certified, is on the [Florida state record fish page](/fishing/florida/state-record-fish).

## New to fishing in Florida?

Start small. FWC manages nearly 80 Fish Management Areas, many of them stocked urban ponds with fish feeders and fishing piers near big cities. Kids under 16 fish free. The [beginner fishing spots page](/fishing/florida/beginner-fishing-spots) lists easy places near Jacksonville, Orlando, Tampa, Miami and Tallahassee, plus simple rigs. Our general [fishing for beginners](/blog/fishing-for-beginners) guide covers gear.

Not sure whether the fish you want is a freshwater or saltwater species? Try the [freshwater or saltwater quiz](/quiz/freshwater-or-saltwater) before you buy a license.

## Safety and advisories

The Florida Department of Health's basic guideline for untested fresh waters is not to eat Florida or largemouth bass longer than 16 inches, and to avoid bowfin and gar. Check the DOH fish consumption advisory search for your specific lake before you keep fish to eat.
