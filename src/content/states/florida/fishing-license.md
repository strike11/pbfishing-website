"Do I need a freshwater or saltwater license?" is the most common Florida fishing question, and FWC's own FAQ admits that where both kinds of fish swim, "the issue becomes less clear." Here's the straight answer, with every price from FWC's 2026–2027 license tables. Fees are set by the Legislature; license agents add an issuance fee and FWC adds a handling fee to online and phone sales. All sales are final.

## Freshwater or saltwater: the rule that matters most

Florida doesn't draw a line on a map. FWC's rule is that **license requirements follow the species of fish**, wherever you catch it.

- Fishing a lake or river where only freshwater fish live: freshwater license.
- Fishing the ocean, the Gulf, a bay or a pier: saltwater license.
- Fishing tidal rivers, spring runs or canals where both live: you need the license that matches the fish you're targeting, and you must **immediately release** any fish you aren't licensed for. FWC's own example is a bass angler on a river with only a freshwater license who hooks a red drum: the redfish goes back.
- One exception: you can keep mullet from fresh water with only a freshwater license.

If you fish both regularly, the resident **Freshwater/Saltwater Combo** at $32.50 is the simple fix. Not sure which side a species falls on? The [freshwater or saltwater quiz](/quiz/freshwater-or-saltwater) is a quick check.

There is no non-resident combo, five-year or lifetime license, and non-residents can't use the free shoreline license. If you're visiting and want to fish a lake and the beach, you buy one of each.

A note on buying online: from November 7, 2025, FWC stopped selling non-resident 3-day and 7-day licenses online, and visitors had to buy them in person. After pushback from guides and lawmakers, online sales came back on **May 21, 2026**. If an older article tells you short-term licenses are in-person only, it's out of date. Also new since late 2025: a non-resident annual saltwater license automatically adds the snook and spiny lobster permits.

## The free saltwater shoreline license

Residents who only saltwater fish from land, or from a structure attached to land like a bridge, jetty or seawall, can get the **Resident Saltwater Shoreline Fishing License for $0**. It does not cover fishing from a boat, from a shore you reached by boat, or while swimming or diving. It's resident-only; the Hillsborough County Tax Collector's guidance says non-residents need a non-resident saltwater license instead. It's free, but you still have to get it and carry it.

## Who doesn't need a Florida fishing license

**Freshwater exemptions** (from FWC's 2026–2027 guide):

- Anyone **under 16**.
- Florida residents **65 or older** with proof of age and residency (a Florida driver license works, or the free Resident 65+ certificate).
- Residents fishing with live or natural bait on a **cane pole** (no reel) in their **home county**. This does not apply in Fish Management Areas.
- Fishing on your own **homestead** (or your spouse's or parent's) in your county of residence.
- Fishing a **private pond of 20 acres or less** that's entirely on the owner's property with no surface connection to public water. Bigger ponds need a $3-per-acre pond license from the owner.
- Fishing the **St. Marys River or Lake Seminole** (not the Florida tributaries) with a valid Georgia license.
- Florida residents certified **totally and permanently disabled** with the disabled-person certificate; clients of the Agency for Persons with Disabilities.
- Florida-resident **military** home on leave for 30 days or less, with orders.

**Saltwater exemptions** add a few:

- Anyone fishing from a **licensed pier**, a **for-hire charter or guide boat**, or a private boat holding a recreational vessel license.
- Florida residents fishing from land or a fixed structure who receive SNAP, temporary cash assistance or Medicaid, with ID and benefit card.

<p class="callout warn"><strong>Check first:</strong> Fish Management Areas have their own rule. A license is required there for residents 16 to 64 and non-residents 16 and up, by any method, cane pole included.</p>

## License-free fishing days

On these days nobody needs a license, residents or visitors, though all bag, size and season rules still apply.

| Type | Rule | Next dates |
|---|---|---|
| Saltwater | First weekend in June, first Saturday in September, Saturday after Thanksgiving | **Nov. 28, 2026**; June 2027 weekend |
| Freshwater | First weekend in April, second weekend in June | **April 3–4, 2027** and **June 12–13, 2027** |

WUWF's June 2026 report notes the snook and spiny lobster permits are also waived on the saltwater days.

## How to buy

- **Online:** GoOutdoorsFlorida.com, 24 hours a day, usable immediately.
- **Phone:** 888-FISH-FLORIDA (888-347-4356).
- **App:** FWC's Fish|Hunt FL app for iPhone and Android. FWC says a license stored in the app counts as having it in your possession.
- **In person:** county tax collector offices and license agents, including Walmart and 400+ other locations. The $20 Military Gold Sportsman's is sold at tax collector offices.

Residency means Florida is your only state of residence, shown by a Florida driver license or ID. A voter card, declaration of domicile, homestead exemption or Florida military orders also work at an agent.

<p class="callout"><strong>Tip:</strong> Agency apps and cell signal don't always cooperate on the water, and App Store reviewers of Fish|Hunt FL have asked for an offline mode. Open your license in the app while you still have signal, and keep the confirmation email or a printout as a backup.</p>

## Penalties for fishing without a license

Under Florida Statute 379.401, fishing without a required license is a Level One, noncriminal infraction:

- **First offense:** $50 civil penalty plus the cost of the license.
- **Same violation again within 36 months:** $250 plus the cost of the license.
- If you contest it in county court, the judge can set a penalty of at least $50 for a first violation and up to $500 for repeats.

Ticket math: a visitor caught on the bank without a license pays more than the $47 annual license would have cost.

## Key rules once you're licensed

The license is only half of it. Freshwater bass are **5 per day, only one 16 inches or longer**; crappie 25; panfish 50. Some waters are catch-and-release for bass.
