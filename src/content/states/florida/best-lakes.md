Florida has thousands of lakes, and FWC publishes more useful data about them than most anglers ever read: angler-survey catch rates, how many 8-pound-plus bass each lake has produced in the TrophyCatch program, and lake-by-lake rules. We used that data to pick and order these 12 waters. Where FWC's own pages disagree on acreage (they sometimes do), we give the figure from the lake's site page.

A quick decoder for the TrophyCatch numbers below: **Lunker Club** is 8 to 9.9 pounds, **Trophy Club** 10 to 12.9, **Hall of Fame** 13 pounds and up. All are photographed on a scale and, almost always, released.

## 1. Lake Okeechobee

**Near:** Okeechobee (north shore) and Clewiston. **Size:** roughly 730 square miles. FWC calls it Florida's largest lake and the second largest body of fresh water in the contiguous United States.

Okeechobee is the big, grassy, wind-blown lake that put Florida bass fishing on the map, and the data still backs it: 502 Lunker Club and 70 Trophy Club bass in TrophyCatch, and FWC lists it among the top five waters for qualifying submissions over the life of the program.

**Bass:** FWC's forecast says fall brings good-sized pre-spawn Florida bass, with the best fishing around eelgrass along the outer edge of the emergent vegetation. Think swim jigs, vibrating jigs and weightless soft plastics worked through the edge, then a Texas rig into the holes.

**Specks:** the crappie bite picks up in November and December as fish prepare to spawn, and January usually produces the best catch of keeper specks. Fish the edges of vegetation stands early and late in the day. FWC's creel survey put the 2025–2026 speck harvest rate at 1.4 fish per hour, down from the year before.

**Access and rules:** this is boat water, and in low-water years it's tricky boat water; FWC recommends going with a guide under current low-water conditions. Crappie under 10 inches must be released immediately anywhere inside the lake's defined boundary, which includes listed canals like Harney Pond Canal and Taylor Creek.

## 2. Lake Kissimmee

**Near:** 40 miles south of Orlando, 18 miles east of Lake Wales. **Size:** 34,948 acres.

The southern anchor of the Kissimmee Chain and, by FWC's account, the lake where its biologists tag more bass over 8 pounds each year than any nearby waterbody. TrophyCatch shows 835 Lunker Club, 174 Trophy Club and 2 Hall of Fame bass. Its home county, Osceola, ranked in the top five for personal-best bass in FWC's angler survey.

**Where:** FWC's fall forecast points to the stretch between Shady Oaks Fish Camp and the Rocks at the south end near State Road 60, plus Osceola Slough, Philadelphia Point and the Pig Trail. Fish open pockets in hydrilla and the edges of bulrush, cattails and Illinois pondweed.

**Also good for:** bream and specks in the same vegetation. Channel catfish have no bag limit here, and nongame fish can be taken with cast nets, trotlines and traps under FWC's rules.

## 3. Lake Tohopekaliga (Toho)

**Near:** southeast of the city of Kissimmee. **Size:** 18,810 acres. East Lake Toho, next door, is 11,968 acres.

Toho is the most consistent big-bass lake in Central Florida. FWC's 2025 angler survey found bass anglers catching 1.03 bass per hour, well above the statewide average of about 0.6, and TrophyCatch shows 519 Lunker Club and 89 Trophy Club fish. Bassmaster put Toho and the Kissimmee Chain on its Top 10 Best Bass Lakes of 2026.

**Where and how:** offshore hydrilla edges and deeper holes inside the hydrilla, with lipless crankbaits, speed worms and vibrating jigs. FWC also names Little Grassy Island, Goblets Cove and North Steer Beach, and says golden shiners, jigs, crankbaits, spinnerbaits and plastic worms all produce. After a rain, run to moving water: the mouth of Shingle Creek, the St. Cloud canal and Partins Ditch.

**Access:** good ramps and fish camps around Kissimmee and St. Cloud. Statewide bass limits apply.

## 4. Rodman Reservoir

**Near:** south of Palatka, off U.S. 19. **Size:** 9,500 acres, about 15 miles long.

Rodman was created in 1968 when an earthen dam was built across the Ocklawaha River, and its old river channel and the Cross Florida Barge Canal give big bass deep water to live in. Its TrophyCatch line is the best on this list: **1,176 Lunker Club, 261 Trophy Club and 10 Hall of Fame bass.** Putnam County, where most of it sits, is a top-five county for personal-best bass.

**When and where:** in winter FWC says the larger bass relate to deeper structure near the old river channel and the barge canal, especially from Kenwood to Kirkpatrick Dam. Spring brings them onto flats and stumps to spawn.

**Rules:** Rodman periodically gets drawn down, and FWC has issued temporary no-harvest orders for black bass during those periods (most recently EO 25-29; the catch-and-release rule ran through March 15, 2026 and has expired). Check FWC's executive orders before you keep a fish.

## 5. Harris Chain of Lakes

**Near:** Leesburg, Tavares, Mount Dora. **Size:** about 75,000 acres across eight main lakes: Apopka, Harris, Griffin, Eustis, Dora, Beauclair, Carlton and Yale. Lake Apopka alone is about 30,000 acres.

A chain of connected lakes with over 30 public ramps, which makes it the easiest big-water trip in Central Florida. TrophyCatch shows 192 Lunker Club and 31 Trophy Club bass, and Lake County ranked among the top five counties in FWC's personal-best survey.

**Bank access:** better than most. FWC lists fishing piers at Singletary Park and Venetian Gardens in Leesburg on Lake Harris, and Herlong Park in Leesburg and Lake Griffin State Park in Fruitland Park on Lake Griffin.

**Specks and hybrids:** Lake Griffin is a long-time crappie lake with a 10-inch minimum on crappie. FWC has stocked sunshine bass in some chain lakes, which adds a schooling fish to the mix. Trotline rules on Griffin are strict, with weekend and daytime restrictions.

## 6. Lake George

**Near:** Astor and the Ocala National Forest, on the St. Johns River. **Size:** 46,000 acres, a natural impoundment of the St. Johns River.

George is the place in this list to catch a striper in Florida. FWC's striper hotspots are Nine Mile Point, the jetties at the south end (the Cowpens), and the spring runs, from fall through spring. When the river is flowing hard, FWC says live shad and grass shrimp around the jetties take both Florida bass and sunshine bass. Bass numbers are solid too: 159 Lunker Club, 30 Trophy Club, 1 Hall of Fame. FWC revegetated more than 90 acres of lake bottom in 2025.

**Rule to know:** fishing is prohibited in and within 50 yards of Silver Glen Springs and Salt Springs, two popular spring runs off the lake.

## 7. Orange Lake

**Near:** southeast of Gainesville, Alachua County. **Size:** about 12,550 acres (FWC pages range up to 12,700).

If you want the best odds at a 13-pounder in Florida, FWC's own numbers point here. TrophyCatch shows 272 Lunker Club, 184 Trophy Club and **21 Hall of Fame** bass, and FWC's bream forecast says more than 16 bass of 13 pounds or heavier were approved from Orange Lake in the past five years, the most in the state.

**Rules:** since April 2024 Orange and neighboring Lochloosa Lake are part of FWC's Florida Trophy Bass Project. Bass **20 inches or longer must be released immediately**, and circle hooks are required with natural bait longer than 3 inches. FWC stocks forage there, including bluegill, threadfin shad, lake chubsucker and rainbow trout, aiming for bass of 15 pounds and up.

**Caveat:** drought has left Orange Lake low, and FWC warns access may be limited. Call a local fish camp first.

## 8. Lake Istokpoga

**Near:** 5 miles northeast of Lake Placid and a few miles southeast of Sebring, Highlands County. **Size:** 27,692 acres.

A big, shallow lake that FWC says has one of the highest largemouth bass catch rates in the state and quality black crappie fishing. For specks, FWC names "Big Jones" creek at the north end and Turtle Creek at the south end. It's a good alternative when Okeechobee is low or blown out. Channel catfish have no bag limit, and nongame fish can be taken with cast nets, trotlines and wire traps.

## 9. Stick Marsh / Farm 13

**Near:** Fellsmere, Indian River and Brevard counties. **Size:** 6,500-acre impoundment, 4 to 8 feet deep.

Officially the St. Johns Water Management Area, and a catch-and-release trophy bass water. **All black bass must be released immediately.** It's a numbers-and-quality lake where the whole point is a photo and a release, which makes it a natural fit for anglers chasing a personal best rather than a fish fry.

## 10. Fellsmere Water Management Area (Headwaters Lake)

**Near:** Fellsmere, Indian River County. **Size:** 10,000 acres, also called Headwaters Lake or Lake Egan.

The newest name on this list: it opened to the public in August 2020. FWC's 2026 forecast says it has the **highest bass catch rate of any surveyed lake in Florida, over 1.60 bass per hour against a statewide average of about 0.60**, and a good day often means more than 25 bass with a real chance at one over 8 pounds.

**Rules:** catch-and-release for black bass, and circle hooks required with natural bait longer than 3 inches. If you've never caught a Florida bass, this is the lake that most improves your odds.

## 11. Lake Talquin

**Near:** just west of Tallahassee, Leon and Gadsden counties. **Size:** about 8,800 acres.

The Panhandle's speck factory. FWC says the best black crappie fishing is January through April, during the pre-spawn and spawn, and the 3.83-pound [state record crappie](/fishing/florida/state-record-fish) came from here in 1992. Talquin also holds the chain pickerel record. Bass spawn later than in South Florida, usually late March or April.

**Stripers:** FWC describes a wintertime striped bass fishery in Lake Talquin and below Jackson Bluff Dam on the lower Ochlockonee River, where the special limit is 3 stripers, only one 24 inches or longer.

**Rule to know:** crappie under 10 inches must be released, including in listed connecting creeks.

## 12. Lake Seminole

**Near:** Chattahoochee, where Florida, Georgia and Alabama meet. **Size:** 37,500 acres, but about 80% of it lies in Georgia; Florida's rules cover a defined southwest portion.

Formed by Jim Woodruff Dam at the junction of the Chattahoochee and Flint rivers, Seminole is stocked every year with striped bass and sunshine bass. The state record sunshine bass, 16.31 pounds, came from here in 1985.

**Rules:** Seminole has its own limits: **10 black bass, each at least 12 inches**; 15 striped, white and sunshine bass combined, no more than 2 at 22 inches or longer; 30 crappie; 50 panfish; 15 pickerel; 50 fish total in possession. A valid Georgia fishing license is accepted on the lake itself.

## Honorable mentions

- **Withlacoochee River and Lake Rousseau:** on Bassmaster's 2026 Top 10 list, per FWC, and the current Season 15 TrophyCatch leader came from the Withlacoochee River in October 2026.
- **Merritt's Mill Pond (Jackson County):** home of the 4.86-pound state record redear sunfish. It's a Fish Management Area with a 10-fish redear limit and a 10-inch minimum for redear.
- **Lake Jackson (Leon County):** FWC calls spring through early summer, February through May, its best bass season.

## How to choose

If you have one day and want a bass, go to Fellsmere or Toho. If you want a trophy and have patience, Rodman, Kissimmee or Orange Lake. If you want fish to eat in winter, Okeechobee or Talquin specks. If you're bank-bound, start with the Harris Chain piers or the urban lakes on our [Florida beginner spots page](/fishing/florida/beginner-fishing-spots).

Then match the bite to the season with the [Florida species calendar](/fishing/florida/fish-species-seasons) and the [Florida bass guide](/fishing/florida/bass-fishing), and read [how to find fishing spots](/blog/how-to-find-fishing-spots) for the map work. Florida weather swings bites fast; [fishing after rain, wind and cold fronts](/blog/fishing-after-rain-wind-cold-front) covers what a front does to shallow-water bass. Before you go, make sure your [license matches the fish](/fishing/florida/fishing-license).
