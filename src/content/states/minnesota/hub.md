Minnesota fishing runs on two calendars: the open-water season that starts with the walleye opener in May, and the hardwater season when towns of fish houses appear on the big lakes. Between them sit a few thousand fishable lakes, two river systems shared with Wisconsin, and the North Shore of Lake Superior. The DNR says its 10 largest inland lakes alone cover more than 825,000 acres and account for about 40% of the statewide walleye harvest, and that Minnesotans spend $2.4 billion a year on fishing. This hub gives you the lay of the land and points you to the detail pages.

## What makes Minnesota fishing different

**Walleye is the center of gravity.** The opener is a cultural event, not just a date, and most of the famous water (Mille Lacs, Leech, Winnibigoshish, Lake of the Woods, Upper Red, Vermilion) is famous for walleye first. Expect slot limits: on Lake Vermilion every walleye from 20 to 26 inches goes back, on Winnibigoshish it's 18 to 23 inches, on Lake of the Woods it's 19.5 to 28 inches.

**The ice season is long and serious.** On Lake of the Woods the DNR describes ice fishing from early December to late March. Shelters need a license once they're left unattended overnight or you walk more than 200 feet away, and they have to be off the ice by fixed dates (March 15, 2027 north of the Highway 10/34/200/2 line, March 1, 2027 south of it).

**Rules change by zone and by lake.** Northern pike have three zones with different limits. Bass have their own zones and a catch-and-release season that now covers every month of the year. Hundreds of lakes carry special regulations printed in the back of the booklet and posted at the access.

**Two big lakes are managed under treaties as well as DNR rules.** Mille Lacs and Upper Red are "intensive management lakes": walleye rules are set to stay within harvest targets and can change during the year. Lower Red Lake and the western part of Upper Red are open only to Red Lake Nation members.

## Regions, north to south

- **Northwest big water.** Lake of the Woods and the Rainy River for walleye, sauger, sturgeon and pike, plus the public 48,000 acres of Upper Red Lake. Border-water rules apply on Lake of the Woods and the Rainy River.
- **North-central lake country.** Leech (112,000 acres), Winnibigoshish (56,544 acres), the Cass Lake chain (more than 25,000 acres of connected water). Walleye, perch, pike and muskie.
- **The Arrowhead and Voyageurs.** Lake Vermilion, Rainy Lake (only 30% of it is in Minnesota), Kabetogama, and Lake Superior's North Shore for lake trout, salmon and steelhead. A trout/salmon stamp is required on Superior for anglers 18 to 64.
- **Central.** Mille Lacs, 132,516 acres and 1.5 to 2 hours from the Twin Cities. Walleye, trophy smallmouth, muskie and pike.
- **West-central lakes.** Otter Tail County is full of bass and panfish lakes with protected slots, like North Turtle, Norway and the Ten Mile lakes (bass 14 to 20 inches released).
- **Twin Cities metro.** Lake Minnetonka, the Mississippi below St. Anthony Falls (2 lines allowed in open water), the St. Croix, and about 70 small Fishing in the Neighborhood lakes.
- **Southeast.** Trout streams with their own season, and Lake Pepin on the Mississippi, where walleye, sauger and bass seasons are continuous.

## Licence in two lines

Residents 16 through 89 need a license ($25 annual); nonresidents 16 and older need one ($51 annual, or $14 for 24 hours). Residents may fish without a license from shore or on water entirely inside a state park, except for trout. Everything else, including stamps, family licenses and lifetime prices, is on the [Minnesota fishing license page](/fishing/minnesota/fishing-license). Yes, you need a license even if you release everything; see [catch and release and licenses](/blog/do-you-need-a-fishing-license-for-catch-and-release).

<p class="callout warn"><strong>Ice safety:</strong> the DNR does not measure ice on any lake. Its guidance is to stay off new clear ice under 4 inches and to double the numbers on white ice. Check the <a href="https://www.dnr.state.mn.us/safety/ice/thickness.html">DNR ice thickness guidelines</a> before you go.</p>

## Bass, records and beginners

Minnesota is walleye country, but the bass fishing is underrated: the DNR points to trophy smallmouth on Mille Lacs, and the Mississippi pools and Lake Pepin have a continuous bass season with a 14-inch minimum. The [Minnesota bass fishing guide](/fishing/minnesota/bass-fishing) covers zones, monthly patterns and lakes with protected slots.

Records tell you what's possible. The certified largemouth record is 8 lb 15 oz from Auburn Lake (2005), the certified muskie is 55 lb 14 oz from Mille Lacs (2021), and the walleye record still on the books is a 17 lb 8 oz fish from the Seagull River in 1979, listed as non-certified. Two new certified panfish records were added in 2026. All of it is on the [Minnesota state record fish page](/fishing/minnesota/state-record-fish).

If you're new, or fishing with kids, start in the metro: the DNR's Fishing in the Neighborhood program stocks more than 10,000 fish a year into small lakes with piers and parking. Our list of [beginner fishing spots in Minnesota](/fishing/minnesota/beginner-fishing-spots) names them, along with what bites and simple rigs. The general [fishing for beginners](/blog/fishing-for-beginners) guide covers gear.

The best Minnesota anglers keep notes: water temperature at the opener, which reef held fish on the second week of ice, what the pressure did before the bite died.
