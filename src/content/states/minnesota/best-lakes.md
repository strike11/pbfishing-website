Ask ten Minnesotans for the best lake and you'll get eleven answers, most of them walleye lakes. This list sticks to waters with public access and a track record you can check: the DNR's ten largest inland lakes (which together cover more than 825,000 acres and produce about 40% of the state's walleye harvest), plus the rivers and metro lakes people actually fish on a weeknight. Sizes come from the DNR where it publishes them; regulations come from the **2026 Minnesota Fishing Regulations**, effective March 1, 2026 through February 28, 2027.

## Lake of the Woods and the Rainy River

Minnesota owns about 300,000 acres of Lake of the Woods, and the DNR's own summary of the lake is a calendar: ice from early December to late March, the Rainy River in spring and fall, the main lake for the summer bite. Walleye, sauger, pike, sturgeon and perch are the targets. It's the default answer to "best ice fishing lake in MN" because the hardwater season is long and the lake is big enough to spread out. If you don't own a shelter, ask local resorts about rentals before you tow one up.

**Rules that trip people up.** This is a Canada border water, so the walleye season runs May 9, 2026 to April 14, 2027 instead of ending in February. You can keep 6 walleye and sauger combined, no more than 4 walleye, with every walleye 19.5 to 28 inches released and one over 28 allowed. Pike: 3, with everything 30 to 40 inches released. Four Mile Bay and the Rainy River below the International Falls dam are catch-and-release for walleye from March 1 to April 14, which covers the early part of the river's spring fishing. Lake sturgeon can be kept April 24 to May 7 and July 1 to September 30, one per calendar year, 45 to 50 inches or over 75, with a $5 sturgeon tag. The current catch-and-release lake sturgeon record, 80 inches, came out of the Rainy River in April 2026.

**Access:** many public ramps and resorts; ice roads are plowed privately, so ask at the resort.

## Upper Red Lake

Only the eastern 48,000 acres of Upper Red are open to the public. The remaining 72,000 acres and all of Lower Red Lake are reserved for members of the Red Lake Nation, and the boundary is a longitude line, so know where you are. Walleye are managed under a cooperative plan with the Red Lake Nation, with harvest targets tied to the mature female population, which means walleye rules are posted at accesses and can change in season. Northern pike have a permanent rule: all from 30 to 40 inches released, possession 3, only 1 over 40.

Plan around the posted walleye rules, which can change after the season starts. If you want a realistic shot at a big pike through the ice, this is one of the better public bets.

## Mille Lacs

At 132,516 acres (207 square miles), Mille Lacs is Minnesota's second-largest inland lake and only 1.5 to 2 hours from the Twin Cities. The DNR describes it as best known for walleye catch rates, with a fast winter walleye bite, tullibee and trophy pike through the ice, and trophy smallmouth, muskie and pike in summer. It has produced records: the certified state muskie (55 lb 14 oz, November 22, 2021) and the certified rock bass (2 lb 4 oz, 2024) both came from here, as did the catch-and-release muskie record (58¼ inches, 2022).

**Rules:** Mille Lacs is managed under treaties as well as DNR regulations. Its walleye, pike, smallmouth and tullibee rules are published separately on the DNR's Mille Lacs page, with one set for the winter season starting December 1 and another for open water starting in May. Culling is not allowed on Mille Lacs, except for bass. Check the current rules before every trip; they have changed often.

## Leech Lake

The DNR calls Leech's 112,000 acres "a tremendous multi-species fishery," and it earns that: walleye, yellow perch, northern pike, muskellunge, largemouth bass, sunfish, burbot (eelpout) and black crappie. Its many bays mean you can usually find a wind-protected shoreline, which matters on a lake this big. The certified state pumpkinseed record (1 lb 5 oz, 1999) is a Leech fish.

**Rules:** walleye possession limit 4, only 1 over 20 inches; crappie and sunfish daily limit 5 each. Walker Bay and the Walker area are the usual starting point.

## Lake Winnibigoshish

"Winnie" is a 56,544-acre Mississippi headwaters reservoir 15 miles northwest of Deer River, shallow (mean depth 15.1 feet, max 70) and wind-swept with sandy shoals. The DNR lists walleye, yellow perch and northern pike as the main draws. It's a perch destination through the ice, and a good early-season walleye lake when fish are on the shoals.

**Rules:** all walleye from 18 to 23 inches released, only 1 over 23 allowed; sunfish daily limit 5. The rule covers connected waters including Cut Foot Sioux, Little Cut Foot Sioux and the Mississippi River to the Knutson Dam.

## Cass Lake chain

Cass Lake itself is nearly 16,000 acres, and the chain adds up to more than 25,000 acres of connected water, with shallow flats, deeper basins and plenty of structure. Walleye, perch and muskellunge are what people come for. It sits between Leech and Winnibigoshish, so a long weekend can cover three big lakes. The chain (including Wolf, Andrusia, Pike Bay and Kitchi) has a sunfish daily limit of 5; otherwise statewide rules apply.

## Lake Vermilion

Vermilion is a rocky, island-studded lake between Tower and Cook: 39,271 acres, more than 365 islands and a maximum depth of 76 feet. The DNR lists walleye, muskellunge, northern pike, smallmouth bass, bluegill, crappie and largemouth bass, which makes it one of the best all-round lakes in the state. Shallow weedy bays hold bass and panfish; the main basins hold walleye and muskie.

**Rules:** every walleye from 20 to 26 inches must be released, possession limit 4, only 1 over 26.

## Rainy Lake

Only 30% of Rainy Lake is in Minnesota, but that part borders Voyageurs National Park, with boat-in campsites and access to Namakan, Sand Point and Crane lakes. The DNR lists walleye, sauger, northern pike, perch, smallmouth bass and black crappie. It's a classic smallmouth lake: rocky points, reefs and clear water.

**Rules:** Rainy Lake is a Canada border water. Walleye and sauger possession is 8 combined, no more than 4 walleye, all walleye from 18 to 26 inches released, 1 over 26. On Canada border waters, you may use only 1 line except when ice fishing (2).

## Lake Kabetogama

Kabetogama lies just south of Rainy Lake inside the Voyageurs area. Its Ojibwe name translates roughly as "the lake that lies parallel or double with another lake." The DNR lists walleye, sauger, smallmouth bass, northern pike and perch. It's smaller and more sheltered than Rainy, which makes it the better choice on a windy day.

**Rules:** unlike Rainy, Kabetogama is an inland water with a special regulation: all walleye from 18 to 26 inches released, 1 over 26, and walleye and sauger possession 6 combined with no more than 4 walleye. The rule covers Sullivan Bay and the Ash River up to Ash River Falls. Voyageurs National Park adds its own rules on interior lakes (artificial bait only, no private boats), but those don't apply on Rainy, Kabetogama, Namakan, Crane or Sand Point.

## Lake Superior and the North Shore

Superior is a different sport: trolling for lake trout and salmon from Duluth up the shore, and casting for steelhead and coho at river mouths. The records show what it can produce: a 16 lb 12 oz brown trout (1989), a 33 lb 4 oz chinook (two tied records, 1989), a 12 lb 5 oz coho (2024), and a 45.5-inch catch-and-release lake trout caught in May 2026.

**Rules:** you need a Minnesota license and a trout/salmon stamp (exempt under 18 and 65 and older). Up to 2 lines, but only 1 within 100 yards of a stream mouth. Lake trout season runs December 1, 2025 to October 4, 2026 and December 1, 2026 to October 3, 2027, limit 3. Salmon: 5 combined, 10-inch minimum. Unclipped steelhead must be released. Big water kills: check the marine forecast, not just the weather.

## Lake Pepin and the Mississippi River pools

Lake Pepin is a natural widening of the Mississippi, 2 miles across and 21 miles long, and the DNR lists walleye, black crappie, sauger, smallmouth and largemouth bass, perch, bluegill and lake sturgeon. The pools near Red Wing produced the certified sauger record (6 lb 2 oz, 1988) and saugeye record (9 lb 13 oz, 1999).

**Rules:** this is a Wisconsin border water, so either state's license works shore to shore. New for 2026, Pools 3 through 8 including Pepin share one rule set: walleye and sauger are continuous, 4 combined, 15-inch minimum on walleye, only 1 walleye or sauger over 20 inches. Bass are continuous too: 5, 14-inch minimum. Pike 3, only 1 over 30. You may use 2 lines. No fishing within 300 feet below Lock and Dam 3 (near Red Wing) or Lock and Dam 4 (Alma, Wisconsin) from March 1 to April 30.

## St. Croix River

The St. Croix, the Wisconsin border east of the Twin Cities, is managed for walleye, sauger, bass, pike and muskie, and it grows big flathead catfish. The catch-and-release flathead record (52½ inches, 2017) came from here, and the old non-certified flathead record (70 lb, 1970) did too.

**Rules:** walleye season opened May 2, 2026. Downstream of the Taylors Falls dam to the Highway 10 bridge: walleye and sauger 4 combined, 15-inch minimum on walleye, 1 over 20; bass May 23, 2026 to March 1, 2027, 5 with a 14-inch minimum. Upstream of the dam, bass harvest closes September 13 and it's catch-and-release after that. The stretch from the Taylors Falls dam down to the Highway 8 bridge is closed to all fishing from March 2 through June 15.

## Lake Minnetonka

Minnetonka sits about 16 miles west-southwest of Minneapolis: 14,528 acres of interconnected bays, a 113-foot maximum depth in Crystal Bay and roughly 125 miles of shoreline. Largemouth and smallmouth bass, walleye, muskellunge, northern pike, black crappie and bluegill all live here. It's busy with pleasure boats on summer weekends, so fish early, late, or on weekdays. The 2026 booklet lists no special regulations for Minnetonka, so statewide rules apply: bass harvest from May 23, muskie 54-inch minimum, 6 walleye.

## Otter Tail Lake

Otter Tail Lake is 13,725 acres with a 120-foot maximum depth, next to the town of Ottertail in west-central Minnesota. Walleye and northern pike are the most sought-after species. It's the anchor of a county with dozens of fishable lakes, and many nearby lakes carry their own rules, for example North Turtle, Norway, Sewell and the Ten Mile lakes, where bass from 14 to 20 inches must be released, and Big Pine, where walleye 18 to 26 inches are protected. The main lake itself has no special listing in the 2026 booklet.

## Mississippi River through the Twin Cities

You don't need to drive north to catch fish. Below St. Anthony Falls the Mississippi is one of the few Minnesota waters where you may use 2 lines in open water (the Minnesota River below Granite Falls is the other). It's catfish water: the old non-certified channel catfish record, 38 lb, came from the Mississippi in Hennepin County in February 1975, and channel cats are open all year with a limit of 5, only 1 over 24 inches. Inland rules apply until you reach the Wisconsin border pools at Prescott.

## How to choose

- **First trip, want walleye:** Mille Lacs or Leech in summer, Lake of the Woods in winter.
- **Want smallmouth:** Mille Lacs, Vermilion, Rainy or Kabetogama from June through September. See the [Minnesota bass fishing guide](/fishing/minnesota/bass-fishing).
- **Want a big muskie:** Mille Lacs, Vermilion, Leech or Cass, June 6 to November 30, and bring a big net.
- **Have kids or no boat:** the metro [beginner spots](/fishing/minnesota/beginner-fishing-spots) beat any of the big lakes.

Before you go, check the season dates on the [Minnesota species and seasons calendar](/fishing/minnesota/fish-species-seasons) and buy the right [Minnesota fishing license](/fishing/minnesota/fishing-license). On a new lake, the general advice in [how to find fishing spots](/blog/how-to-find-fishing-spots) applies: start with the DNR depth map in LakeFinder and fish the first breaks off the shoals.

<p class="callout warn"><strong>Ice:</strong> the DNR does not measure ice thickness on any lake. Its guidelines start at 4 inches of new clear ice for walking and 5 to 7 inches for a snowmobile; double them for white ice. Resort ice roads are not a guarantee. See the <a href="https://www.dnr.state.mn.us/safety/ice/thickness.html">DNR ice guidelines</a>.</p>
