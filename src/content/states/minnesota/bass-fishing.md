For most of the year in Minnesota, the bass you catch go back in the water by law, not just by choice. That shapes everything: the best bass fishing for size often happens in the catch-and-release window before harvest opens, the biggest smallmouth live in lakes most people visit for walleye, and the state's records are surprisingly modest because the season is short and the water is cold. Here's how to fish Minnesota bass legally and well, with every rule from the **2026 Minnesota Fishing Regulations**.

## Bass species in Minnesota

| Species | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Largemouth bass | Weedy lakes statewide, especially central and southern Minnesota and the metro | Certified record 8 lb 15 oz (2005) |
| Smallmouth bass | Rocky northern lakes, Mille Lacs, border waters, larger rivers | Certified record open; non-certified 8 lb 0 oz (1948) |
| Rock bass | Statewide, rocky shorelines | Separate limit of 30; record 2 lb 4 oz from Mille Lacs (2024) |
| White bass and yellow bass | Rivers, southern lakes | Limit 30 combined; not "black bass" under the bass rules |

There are no spotted bass or Florida-strain largemouth here. Only largemouth and smallmouth count toward the bass limit of 6 combined.

## 2026-27 bass seasons by zone

Minnesota splits bass rules into zones. The northeast zone is north and east of U.S. Highway 53 from Duluth to International Falls (Pelican and Ash lakes in St. Louis County count as northeast for bass). Everything else is the southern and north-central zones.

| Zone | Species | Catch-and-release only | Harvest (6 combined) | Catch-and-release again |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Southern and North-central | Largemouth | March 1 to May 22 | May 23, 2026 to Feb. 28, 2027 | (none) |
| Southern and North-central | Smallmouth | March 1 to May 22 | May 23 to Sept. 13 | Sept. 14, 2026 to Feb. 28, 2027 |
| Northeast | Largemouth and smallmouth | March 1 to May 8 | May 9, 2026 to Feb. 28, 2027 | (none) |
| Mississippi pools 3-8, Lake Pepin | Both | (none) | Continuous, 5 bass, 14" minimum | (none) |
| St. Croix below Taylors Falls dam | Both | (none) | May 23, 2026 to March 1, 2027, 5 bass, 14" minimum | (none) |
| St. Croix above Taylors Falls dam | Both | (none) | May 23 to Sept. 13, 5 bass, 14" minimum | Sept. 14, 2026 to March 1, 2027 |
| Canada border waters | Both | (none) | Continuous, 6 combined | (none) |

The headline change for 2026: the catch-and-release season now covers every part of the calendar, so there's no longer a closed window where targeting bass is illegal on inland waters. "Catch-and-release" in Minnesota means the fish is immediately released, held only long enough to unhook, measure and photograph.

## Special bass regulations worth knowing

Some lakes are tighter than the statewide rule, and the booklet lists them by lake:

- **Protected slots (14 to 20 inches released, 1 over 20 allowed):** North Turtle, Norway, Sewell and the North and South Ten Mile lakes in Otter Tail County, and Thirteen Lake in Cass County. Clitherall Lake (Otter Tail) has the same slot for smallmouth only.
- **12 to 20-inch slot:** Agnes Lake (Douglas) and Mink-Somers Lake (Wright, largemouth).
- **Largemouth catch-and-release only:** Ann, Minnewashta and Steiger (Carver), Crooked (Anoka), Jane and Olson (Washington), Hovde and Moccasin (Cass), Crawford (Wright).
- **Largemouth and smallmouth catch-and-release only:** Clear Lake (Waseca).
- **Otter Tail River:** smallmouth catch-and-release on listed stretches.
- **Mille Lacs:** separate rules on the DNR's Mille Lacs page; culling is allowed for bass only.

Signs at the access are the final word. If you plan to keep fish from a slot lake, the fish has to stay intact so an officer can measure it.

## Month-by-month pattern in Minnesota

Minnesota's open-water bass season is compressed by ice. The timing below is a typical pattern for central Minnesota; the north runs about two weeks behind, the far south a bit ahead.

| Month | What bass are doing | How to fish |
|---|---|---|
| Dec to Feb | Under ice, sluggish, mostly in deeper weed edges | Largemouth sometimes show up while panfishing; keep it brief and release fast |
| March to April | Ice-out, fish move to warming shallow bays | Slow jigs, suspending jerkbaits in dark-bottom bays; catch-and-release only |
| May | Pre-spawn, water climbing through the 50s °F | Jerkbaits, swimbaits, ned rigs on smallmouth flats; largemouth on the first green weeds |
| June | Spawn and post-spawn; harvest open in most of the state | Sight-fish beds only if you release fast; topwater at dawn |
| July to Aug | Smallmouth deep on rock and sand-grass transitions (15 to 25 feet on clear lakes); largemouth on outside weedlines and docks | Drop shot, tube, football jig; frogs and flipping in slop |
| Sept | Smallmouth school up; in the south and north-central zones smallmouth go catch-and-release from Sept. 14 | Swimbaits, jerkbaits, blade baits as water cools |
| Oct to Nov | Cold-water feed before ice | Hair jigs, blade baits, slow jerkbaits; some of the biggest smallmouth of the year |

## Top bass waters

### Mille Lacs

The DNR's own description of Mille Lacs includes "a trophy-sized smallmouth bass" among summer targets, and the lake gave up the state rock bass record in 2024. At 132,516 acres it's a big, windy lake, so smallmouth fishing is about finding fish on structure with electronics and picking your days around the wind. Rules for Mille Lacs smallmouth are set separately from the statewide rules and posted on the DNR site and at the accesses.

### Lake Vermilion

Rocky shorelines, hundreds of islands and shallow weedy bays give Vermilion both bass species in one lake; the DNR lists smallmouth and largemouth among its main fish. Fish points and island saddles for smallmouth, and the weedy back bays for largemouth. Vermilion sits north and east of Highway 53, so it's in the northeast zone: bass harvest opens May 9, two weeks before most of the state.

### Rainy Lake and Kabetogama

Both are listed by the DNR for smallmouth. Rainy is a Canada border water, which means bass are continuous with a 6-fish limit and only 1 line in open water. Kabetogama is inland, so the northeast-zone dates apply. Clear water and boulder shorelines reward tubes, jerkbaits and topwater.

### Lake Minnetonka

About 16 miles from downtown Minneapolis, 14,528 acres, with both bass species, plus muskie and walleye. It fishes under the statewide rules, so it's catch-and-release until May 23. Expect boat traffic on summer weekends; docks and milfoil edges hold largemouth all summer.

### Leech Lake

The DNR lists largemouth bass among Leech's species. Its many bays give largemouth the vegetation they want, and you can usually find a lee shore when the main lake is too rough.

### Mississippi pools and Lake Pepin

The best year-round bass option in the state: the season is continuous, the limit is 5 with a 14-inch minimum, and 2 lines are legal. Wing dams, backwater cuts and rock riprap hold smallmouth on current; largemouth live in the backwaters.

### St. Croix River

Bass harvest from May 23 with a 14-inch minimum, and above the Taylors Falls dam smallmouth go catch-and-release after September 13. The stretch from the Taylors Falls dam to the Highway 8 bridge is closed to all fishing from March 2 through June 15.

### Otter Tail County slot lakes

North Turtle, Norway, Sewell and the Ten Mile lakes protect every bass from 14 to 20 inches. Slots like this are meant to protect mid-size fish so more of them grow past 20 inches. Good places to chase a PB you'll release.

## Lures that work on Minnesota bass

Minnesota bass water is mostly clear, cold, and either rocky (smallmouth) or weedy (largemouth). Our go-to box:

- **Ned rig**, 1/10 oz mushroom head, green pumpkin plastic: smallmouth on rock and sand from May through October.
- **Tube**, 3.5 inch on a 3/8 oz jig, green pumpkin or smoke: classic Mille Lacs and northern smallmouth bait.
- **Drop shot**, 1/4 oz weight, 4 inch minnow-style plastic: deep summer smallmouth, especially when you can see them on electronics.
- **Suspending jerkbait**, 4 to 4.5 inch, ghost minnow or perch: pre-spawn and fall.
- **Spinnerbait**, 3/8 oz, white/chartreuse: windblown points and weed edges.
- **Hollow-body frog** and a 1 oz flipping jig for largemouth in matted weeds on metro lakes.
- **Hair jig or blade bait**, 1/4 to 1/2 oz: cold water in October and November.

## Records and what counts as a big bass here

| Record | Size | Water | Date |
|---|---|---|---|
| Largemouth, certified weight | 8 lb 15 oz, 23½" | Auburn Lake (Carver) | Oct. 3, 2005 |
| Largemouth, catch-and-release | 22.5" | Lura Lake (Blue Earth) | May 16, 2026 (Andy Kilen) |
| Smallmouth, certified weight | Open record | (none) | (none) |
| Smallmouth, non-certified | 8 lb 0 oz | West Battle Lake (Otter Tail) | 1948 |
| Smallmouth, catch-and-release | 22¾" | Basswood Lake (Lake) | June 25, 2024 (Xavier Bradley-Rael) |

The certified smallmouth category was reopened by the DNR so a weighed fish can replace the 1948 non-certified record, which had no certified scale behind it. A sub-9-pound largemouth record tells you something useful: in Minnesota, a 5-pound largemouth is a genuine trophy, and a 6 is a fish of a lifetime. For perspective by length, see [what is a good size bass](/blog/what-is-a-good-size-bass) and the [largemouth bass weight chart](/blog/largemouth-bass-weight-chart). More on all records: [Minnesota state record fish](/fishing/minnesota/state-record-fish).

There is no state-run trophy bass program like Texas's ShareLunker. The closest things are the DNR record program and the Fishing Hall of Fame of Minnesota's Master Angler Program, which recognizes big fish in adult, youth and catch-and-release categories.
