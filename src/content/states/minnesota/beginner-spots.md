The best beginner spot in Minnesota is usually a small lake in a city park: a fishing pier, a parking lot close by, a restroom, and fish the DNR put there on purpose. The DNR's **Fishing in the Neighborhood (FiN)** program runs about 70 of these small lakes across the seven-county Twin Cities metro, stocks more than 10,000 fish a year for put-and-take fishing, and builds piers and shore-fishing spots with local parks departments. That's where to start, whether you're 6 or 46.

## Twin Cities beginner spots at a glance

From the DNR's A-Z list of FiN lakes, these are the ones with the most going for a first trip: a fishing pier, easy parking and restrooms, and a good mix of fish.

"Fish listed" means the species the DNR shows for that lake, not a promise you'll catch them. Bluegill and crappie are the realistic targets almost everywhere. The full list, with amenities for every FiN lake, is the DNR's [A-Z FiN Ponds PDF](https://files.dnr.state.mn.us/recreation/fishing/fin/a-z_fin_ponds.pdf).

## Four metro spots worth a closer look

### Como Lake, St. Paul

If you only try one, try Como. The DNR lists all seven FiN species here (bluegill, black crappie, largemouth bass, channel catfish, northern pike, walleye and yellow perch), and there's a pier, grills, parking, picnic areas, restrooms and a walking path. Fish the pier with a bobber and a piece of worm for sunfish; a nightcrawler on the bottom at dusk is your catfish shot.

### Powderhorn Lake, Minneapolis

Small, urban and regularly stocked: in spring 2025 the DNR put 495 bluegill, 62 black crappie, 600 yellow perch and 8 northern pike into Powderhorn. Pier, playground and restrooms make it easy with kids. Catfish are on the DNR's species list too.

### Lake Wirth (Theodore Wirth Park)

Bigger and wilder-feeling than most FiN lakes, with largemouth, pike and walleye on the species list and a 2025 stocking of 200 bluegill, 100 black crappie, 165 perch and 11 pike. Good choice for a beginner who wants a shot at something bigger than a sunfish.

### Snelling Lake, Fort Snelling State Park

A FiN lake inside a state park, so you need a park vehicle permit. Here's the bonus: Minnesota residents may fish without a license from shore on state-owned land inside a state park, for any species except trout. Pier, restrooms, picnic areas, bluegill, crappie, largemouth, pike and perch.

## Beyond the metro

- **State parks anywhere in Minnesota.** That resident license exemption applies statewide: shore fishing or wading on state-owned land within a state park, or fishing waters that lie entirely inside a park. The DNR's where-to-fish page also links a list of state parks with fishing starter kits; check it for the current list before you count on borrowing gear.
- **Mississippi River below St. Anthony Falls.** Two lines are legal here in open water, and channel catfish are open all year (5 fish, only 1 over 24 inches). Bank fishing from city parks along the river is a classic summer-evening trip.
- **Duluth and the St. Louis River estuary.** The estuary works with a Minnesota license (no Wisconsin license needed). Walleye there are 2 a day with a 15-inch minimum, and bass 5 with a 14-inch minimum from May 23. If you fish Lake Superior itself, anglers 18 to 64 need a trout/salmon stamp.
- **Mille Lacs for a first walleye trip.** It's 1.5 to 2 hours from the Twin Cities and known for walleye catch rates. A launch or guide trip is the easiest way in for beginners; check the lake's separate regulations first.
- **LakeFinder.** The DNR has maps for about 4,500 fishing lakes, plus fishing piers, urban ponds and trout streams. Search near your town and look for a public access or pier.

## Licenses, free weekends and kids

- **Kids:** residents under 16 don't need a license. Nonresident kids under 16 can fish under a licensed parent or guardian.
- **Helping a child:** if you're only showing a child under 16 how to bait, cast and unhook, you don't need a license; the child must hold the rod, set the hook and reel.
- **Free weekends (residents):** Take a Mom Fishing Weekend (May 9-10, 2026), Take a Kid Fishing Weekend (June 5-7, 2026), Take a Kid Ice Fishing Weekend (January 16-18, 2027).
- **Everyone else:** a resident annual license is $25, a nonresident 24-hour is $14. Details on the [Minnesota fishing license page](/fishing/minnesota/fishing-license).
- **Panfish are open all year:** crappie limit 10, sunfish 20, perch 20 per day statewide. Some lakes are lower; check the sign at the access.

## Simple rigs that catch Minnesota panfish

| Target | Rig | Bait | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bluegill and sunfish | Small round bobber, size 8 or 10 hook, 1 split shot, 2-3 feet under the float | Piece of nightcrawler or a wax worm | Off the end of the pier, near weeds |
| Crappie | Slip bobber, 1/32 oz jig with a small plastic or a crappie minnow | Small minnow or 1.5 inch plastic | Pier corners, evenings, spring shallows |
| Channel catfish | Slip sinker (1/2 oz), size 2 hook | Nightcrawler gob or cut bait | On the bottom at dusk |
| Largemouth bass | Weightless 5 inch stick bait or a small spinnerbait | (none) | Weed edges from shore (catch-and-release until May 23 in most of the state) |

Use 4 to 6 lb line on a 5 to 6 foot spinning rod. Keep it simple: one rig, one bait, and move 20 feet if nothing bites in 10 minutes. Remember that in Minnesota you may use only one line in open water (two through the ice), and you can't release leftover bait into the lake; throw unwanted bait in the trash. ## Family tips that actually help

- **Go in June.** Bluegill are in the shallows, the weather is easy, and Take a Kid Fishing Weekend makes it free for residents.
- **Fish the pier corners and the weed edge,** not the open water in front of you.
- **Bring a ruler and take a photo** of the first fish before it goes back. Kids remember the photo. A quick side-profile shot with the fish on a measuring board is all it takes; [how to measure a fish](/blog/how-to-measure-a-fish) shows how. PB Fishing can log that photo with the length and species and keep each kid's personal best, free.
- **Try first ice with an adult who knows the lake.** Small FiN ponds freeze before the big lakes, but the DNR doesn't check ice anywhere.

<p class="callout warn"><strong>Ice safety:</strong> the DNR does not measure ice on any lake or pond. Stay off new clear ice under 4 inches, and double that for white ice. See the <a href="https://www.dnr.state.mn.us/safety/ice/thickness.html">DNR ice thickness guidelines</a>.</p>
