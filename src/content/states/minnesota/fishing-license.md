Minnesota's license rules are simpler than most states' once you know three things: residents need one from 16 to 89, nonresidents from 16, and there's no saltwater/freshwater split to worry about. The details below are as of November 2026, checked against the DNR's **2026 Minnesota Fishing Regulations** (effective March 1, 2026 through February 28, 2027) and the DNR's fishing license page. The DNR moved to a new Electronic Licensing System in 2026, so screens and receipts may look different from last year's; the prices below are the booklet's.

## Who needs a Minnesota fishing license

- **Residents age 16 through 89** need a current Minnesota fishing license to fish, unless an exemption applies.
- **Nonresidents age 16 and older** need a license. Nonresident kids under 16 don't need one if a parent or guardian is licensed; they then share the parent's limit, or they can buy a $5 youth license to keep their own.
- **Catch-and-release counts as fishing.** You need a license even if you keep nothing.
- **Ice fishing, spearing and shelters** have their own add-ons (see below).

**Who counts as a resident:** you've kept a legal residence in Minnesota for at least 60 consecutive days before buying. Residents 21 or older must show a Minnesota driver's license or ID (or a receipt for one that's at least 60 days old). A nonresident under 21 whose parent is a Minnesota resident qualifies, as do some tribal members with a Minnesota address. Nonresident full-time students at a Minnesota school, and nonresident service members stationed in Minnesota, can buy at resident rates with proof.

## Who doesn't need one

| Situation | Rule |
|---|---|
| Residents under 16 | No license needed |
| Residents 90 and older | No license needed |
| Resident military home on leave | No license needed; carry leave or furlough papers |
| Residents discharged from overseas active service in the past 24 months | No license needed; carry discharge papers |
| Adults helping a child under 16 | No license needed if you're only teaching; the child must hold the rod, set the hook and reel |
| Helping a person with a disability | No license needed to assist; the angler needs one unless exempt |
| Residents in a state park | No license from shore or wading on state-owned park land, or on waters entirely inside a park, for any species except trout |
| Take a Mom Fishing Weekend | Resident moms, May 9-10, 2026 |
| Take a Kid Fishing Weekend | Residents 16+ fishing with a child under 16, June 5-7, 2026 |
| Take a Kid Ice Fishing Weekend | Residents 16+ fishing with a child under 16, January 16-18, 2027 |

People who don't need a license may still keep a possession limit. Note that Minnesota's free weekends are for residents only, and the kid weekends require an actual kid.

**Free licenses** (not exemptions; you still get a license, at no charge) are available to: residents 16+ with a developmental disability and veterans with a 100% service-connected disability (from the DNR at 877-348-0498); residents who are legally blind or receive SSI, SSDI or total-and-permanent-disability workers' comp (any agent, with a current benefits letter); some Railroad Retirement and Postal Service disability recipients; residents of licensed nursing or boarding care homes and VA hospital inpatients; and foreign exchange students.

A $5 surcharge is built into nonresident prices. For a week-long trip, the 7-day ($43) costs only $8 less than the annual ($51), so if there's any chance you'll come back before February, buy the annual.

## Stamps, tags and add-ons

| Add-on | Who needs it | Price |
|---|---|---|
| Trout/salmon stamp | Ages 18-64 fishing designated trout streams and lakes or Lake Superior, or keeping trout anywhere | $10 (+75 cents for the pictorial stamp) |
| Walleye stamp | Nobody; it's a voluntary donation that funds walleye stocking | $5 |
| Sturgeon tag/endorsement | Anyone harvesting a lake sturgeon (even if otherwise license-exempt) | $5 |
| Dark house spearing | Residents 18-89 ($6) and nonresidents 18+ ($17), plus an angling license | $6 / $17 |
| Shelter license, resident | Fish houses; portable shelters left unattended midnight to 1 hour before sunrise or when you're over 200 ft away | $15 annual, $42 for 3 years, $30 rental |
| Shelter license, nonresident | Same rule | $37 annual, $21 for 7 days, $111 for 3 years |

The trout stamp is not needed under 18, at 65 and older, with a 24- or 72-hour license, or if you're license-exempt. Shelter licenses aren't required on the Wisconsin, Iowa, North Dakota and South Dakota border waters.

## Where and how to buy

- **Online:** mndnr.gov/buyalicense. A convenience fee is added for web sales. You won't get a paper license; you get an email and can print it.
- **License agents:** about 1,400 agent locations statewide issue licenses and stamps through the DNR's electronic system.
- **Fees:** licenses, including free ones, carry a $1 issuing fee; the DNR's license page lists a $1 agent fee not included in prices.
- **Lifetime licenses:** the first purchase is only through the DNR License Center in St. Paul (applications online or from the DNR Information Center, 888-646-6367). After that, you get a free annual renewal each year you fish.
- **Have your SSN ready:** the DNR collects Social Security numbers for non-commercial hunting and fishing licenses.
- **Lost your license:** any agent can print a duplicate for $2.50.

All sales are final; refunds only if the licensee dies before the season, is called to active military duty for the whole season, or bought two licenses for the same season by mistake.

## Carrying your license

A self-printed or electronic license is sufficient proof in Minnesota, so a screenshot or the email on your phone works. If you fish where there's no signal (much of the north, and most lakes once you're on the ice), save it offline or carry a printout.

## Penalties and revocation

The booklet doesn't list fine amounts, but it does spell out what happens to repeat offenders: two or more fishing convictions in three years can cost you your fishing privileges for a year; gross over-limit violations can mean revocation for 3, 5 or 10 years; and unpaid fines can suspend all game and fish licenses. Violations in other states can also count.

## Key rules every license holder should know

- **License year:** March 1 to the last day of February. A license bought in December expires at the end of February.
- **Lines:** 1 in open water (2 on the Mississippi below St. Anthony Falls and the Minnesota River below Granite Falls), 2 through the ice, 1 on designated trout waters, up to 2 on Lake Superior.
- **Limits:** set by species and often by lake. See the [Minnesota fish species and seasons calendar](/fishing/minnesota/fish-species-seasons) for statewide limits and the [best lakes guide](/fishing/minnesota/best-lakes) for lake-specific slots.
- **Border waters:** on Lake Pepin, the St. Croix and the Mississippi below Prescott, either a Minnesota or Wisconsin license works shore to shore. On Lake Superior you need a Wisconsin license in Wisconsin waters, but the St. Louis River Estuary works with a Minnesota license.
- **Conservation license:** half the regular limit, rounded down, and no walleye over 20 inches.

## Which license should you buy?

- **Resident, fishing a few weekends:** $25 annual. If you'll want trout or Lake Superior, add the $10 stamp.
- **Resident couple:** $40 married combination, each with your own limit.
- **Resident who mostly releases:** $17 conservation license.
- **Nonresident, one long weekend:** $36 for 72 hours; for a week, $43; for two adults and kids, $68 family annual.
- **Nonresident couple on a two-week trip:** $54 married 14-day.
- **Planning to fish Minnesota for decades:** a resident lifetime license bought for a child 3 or under costs $344, about what 14 years of $25 annual licenses would cost at today's price.
