Michigan is three fishing states stacked on top of each other. The Great Lakes state, where boats troll spoons for kings off Ludington and Detroit River anglers jig walleye in April. The inland-lake state, where a Saturday on Houghton or Hubbard Lake means bluegill, pike, walleye and bass from the same water. And the trout-stream state, where the DNR sorts rivers into numbered types and the most famous water is flies-only and no-kill.

The DNR's own numbers give you the scale: about 1.2 million residents and nonresidents fish Michigan each year, and sport fishing is worth an estimated $3.9 billion annually, according to the 2026 regulations digest. You are never short of water. What trips people up is the rules, because a bass lake, a Type 1 trout stream and Lake St. Clair can all have different seasons on the same day.

## What makes Michigan fishing different

Three things set Michigan apart from its neighbors.

**Great Lakes access from almost anywhere.** Michigan touches Lakes Superior, Michigan, Huron and Erie, plus the connecting waters: the St. Marys River, St. Clair River, Lake St. Clair and the Detroit River. The DNR says the southeastern shoreline from Port Huron to Lake Erie is "nationally known" for the size and number of its smallmouth bass.

**Stocked salmon and steelhead runs.** Chinook salmon start running up rivers in late summer and are usually in catchable numbers by mid-August, per the DNR species page. On the Muskegon River below Croton Dam, the DNR rates Chinook fishing "very good" in September and October and steelhead "excellent" from late October through June.

**A rulebook built around water type.** Inland trout streams are Type 1 to 4, trout lakes are Type A to F, and gear-restricted stretches have their own pages. Read the [species and seasons calendar](/fishing/michigan/fish-species-seasons) before you plan around a date.

## Best regions in one paragraph each

**Southeast (Detroit, Monroe, Port Huron).** The St. Clair to Detroit River corridor plus Lake Erie. Spring walleye, summer smallmouth, muskie and perch. The Detroit River walleye limit is six fish per angler, per the DNR. Shore anglers have Belle Isle, Milliken State Park and Sterling State Park.

**Thumb and Saginaw Bay (Bay City).** Walleye water first. On Saginaw Bay and the lower Saginaw River the daily walleye limit is 8 with a 13-inch minimum and the season is open all year, a rare exception to the statewide 5-fish, 15-inch rule.

**Northern Lower Peninsula (Cheboygan to Traverse City).** Big glacial lakes: Burt, Mullett, Torch, Higgins, Houghton and Hubbard. Smallmouth, walleye, pike and muskie, plus lake trout in the deep ones. The Au Sable "Holy Waters" and the Manistee are the trout anglers' pilgrimage.

**West Michigan (Grand Rapids, Muskegon, Ludington).** Lake Michigan piers and ports, the Grand and Muskegon rivers, and fall salmon. The state-record Chinook came out of Lake Michigan near Ludington in 2021.

**Upper Peninsula.** Walleye and pike open later (May 15), brook trout streams, Little Bay de Noc walleye and Lake Superior lake trout. The state-record lake trout, 61.5 pounds, came from Lake Superior in 1997.

## Seasons at a glance

- **January to March:** Ice fishing for perch, walleye and pike. Pike and walleye possession on Lower Peninsula inland waters closes March 15.
- **April:** Detroit River walleye run. Trout, walleye and pike open on the last Saturday in April (April 25 in 2026).
- **May:** Upper Peninsula walleye and pike open May 15. Bass move shallow but are catch-and-immediate-release only until the Saturday before Memorial Day.
- **June:** Muskie possession opens the first Saturday in June. Bass possession on Lake St. Clair and the St. Clair and Detroit rivers opens the third Saturday.
- **July and August:** Smallmouth on Great Lakes structure, salmon trolling offshore, bluegill and largemouth inland.
- **September and October:** Salmon in the rivers. Type 1 and 2 trout streams close September 30.
- **November and December:** Steelhead and late walleye. Bass possession closes December 31.

## The license in two lines

Anyone 17 or older needs a license for public waters. In 2026 it's $26 a year for residents, $76 for nonresidents, $11 for residents 65 and up, and $10 for a 24-hour license, all covering every species. Full details, exemptions and where to buy: [Michigan fishing license](/fishing/michigan/fishing-license).

Michigan runs two Free Fishing Weekends a year, one on Presidents Day weekend in February and one the weekend after the first full week of June. In 2026 they were February 14 and 15 and June 13 and 14.

## Records worth knowing

Michigan's state records are recognized by weight only, must be weighed on a commercial scale and identified by a DNR fisheries biologist. Three that people ask about:

- **Largemouth bass:** 11.94 pounds, held jointly by fish from Big Pine Island Lake (1934) and Alcona Dam Pond (1959).
- **Smallmouth bass:** 9.98 pounds, Indian River, Cheboygan County, 2016.
- **Chinook salmon:** 47.86 pounds, Lake Michigan off Ludington, 2021.

## New to Michigan fishing?

Start near a city with a pier or a stocked pond rather than a 20,000-acre lake. The DNR runs free Hook, Line and Sinker classes at more than two dozen state parks and loans a rod if you don't own one.
