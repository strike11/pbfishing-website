Ranking Michigan's lakes is a little silly, because the right answer depends on what you're after. A smallmouth angler, a walleye troller and a dad with two kids and a bucket of crawlers want very different water. So this list doesn't pretend there's one winner. Each water is here because the DNR's own biologists list it as good water for the species named, and because it does one thing especially well.

Species lists come from the DNR's Better Fishing Waters database, lake descriptions from DNR management unit pages, and every limit or season from the 2026 Michigan Fishing Regulations. Where we couldn't confirm a lake's size from a DNR source, we left the number out.

## 1. Lake St. Clair

**Where:** Between Detroit and Port Huron, shared with Ontario. Michigan access from St. Clair Shores, Harrison Township and Anchor Bay.
**Species (DNR):** Smallmouth and largemouth bass, muskellunge, walleye, yellow perch, crappie, rock bass, pumpkinseed.

Lake St. Clair is the reason Michigan shows up on national bass rankings. The DNR describes the coast from Port Huron to Lake Erie as "nationally known" for smallmouth size and numbers, and its 2022 status report found smallmouth trap-net catches and young-of-year recruitment both up over 2021. It's a shallow, wind-driven lake, so a calm morning and a 20 mph afternoon are different planets.

**Rules that matter here:** Bass possession on Lake St. Clair, the St. Clair River and the Detroit River doesn't open until the third Saturday in June (June 20 in 2026), weeks after the rest of the state. Catch-and-immediate-release is open all year. Muskie possession runs from the first Saturday in June to December 31, and this is one of the few waters where up to 5 northern pike 24 inches or longer may be kept. Remember the international line; Ontario water needs an Ontario license.

## 2. Detroit River

**Where:** The current from Lake St. Clair to Lake Erie, right through downtown Detroit and down to Wyandotte.
**Species (DNR):** Walleye, smallmouth and largemouth bass, muskellunge, yellow perch, catfish, rock bass, bluegill.

In April the Detroit River walleye run brings boats from across the Midwest. The DNR calls the fishery "world-class," and the limit is six walleye per angler. In May 2026 conservation officers cited a group of five anglers who were 34 walleye over that limit, so officers are watching. Vertical jigging with a 3/8 to 5/8-ounce jig and a plastic or minnow, held right under the boat in the current, is the classic approach.

**Shore option:** Milliken State Park on the downtown riverfront and Belle Isle both have fishing access. The walleye run fishes best from a boat, but shore anglers catch smallmouth, perch and the odd walleye along the walls.

## 3. Lake Erie off Monroe

**Where:** Michigan's slice of Lake Erie, from the Detroit River mouth to the Ohio line. Launches around Monroe and Luna Pier.
**Species (DNR):** Walleye, yellow perch, smallmouth bass, catfish.

Michigan's Lake Erie water is small compared with Ohio's, but the walleye are the same fish. The DNR's 2022 status report called the Lake Erie walleye fishery "excellent," with anglers logging an estimated 401,187 hours and catching more than 762,500 fish that year. Perch are the other draw, with a daily limit of 50 on Lake Erie.

**Shore option:** Sterling State Park is "known for its walleye fishing," per the DNR, with three fishing piers, three lagoons and access to the River Raisin.

## 4. Saginaw Bay

**Where:** The big bay in Lake Huron's "thumb," from Bay City out to Port Austin and Tawas.
**Species (DNR):** Walleye, yellow perch, smallmouth and largemouth bass, northern pike, catfish, steelhead.

Saginaw Bay is walleye country with its own rulebook. On the bay and the lower Saginaw River, the daily walleye limit is 8 fish with a 13-inch minimum, and the season is open all year, which beats the statewide 5 fish and 15 inches. It's also one of the state's best-known ice fisheries for perch and walleye. The state-record white bass, 6.44 pounds, came from the bay in 1989.

**Shore option:** Bay City State Park's Tobico Lagoon has an accessible pier for pike, bass, carp and panfish. Check ice conditions with care here; bay ice moves.

## 5. Houghton Lake

**Where:** Roscommon County, central Lower Peninsula, right off US-127.
**Size:** 20,075 acres, per the DNR, the largest inland lake in Michigan.
**Species (DNR):** Bluegill, crappie, yellow perch, largemouth and smallmouth bass, walleye, northern pike.

The DNR sums it up: relatively shallow, "abundant aquatic vegetation," and "excellent summer and winter fishing" for panfish, perch, bass, walleye and pike. Think weed edges, not deep structure. It's also the home of Michigan's channel catfish record, 40 pounds, listed for both 1960 and 1964.

**Rules:** Houghton Lake is on the list of waters where you may keep up to 5 pike of any size, with only 1 over 24 inches. Denton Creek and parts of the Cut River are closed March 23 to April 23 to protect spawners. Five large boat launches spread the pressure.

## 6. Higgins Lake

**Where:** Next door to Houghton Lake, Roscommon County.
**Size:** 10,186 acres and "very deep (120 feet)," per the DNR.
**Species (DNR):** Lake trout, yellow perch, rock bass, smallmouth, northern pike, rainbow trout.

Higgins is Houghton's opposite: deep, clear and cold. The DNR rates lake trout, rock bass, perch and smelt fishing "very good," and notes perch and smelt numbers tend to alternate year to year. It's stocked annually with lake trout and rainbow trout and fished mostly by boat in summer. South Higgins Lake State Park has a pier on Marl Lake for panfish.

## 7. Burt Lake

**Where:** Cheboygan County, along I-75 near Indian River and Alanson.
**Species (DNR):** Walleye, smallmouth bass, muskellunge, northern pike, yellow perch, rock bass, cisco, brown and rainbow trout.

Burt is one of the big three lakes of the Inland Waterway, connected to Mullett Lake by the Indian River. That river produced the current 9.98-pound state-record smallmouth in 2016. Muskie on Burt carry a 46-inch minimum instead of 42.

**Watch this one:** In late 2025 the Natural Resources Commission considered an amendment to close bass catch-and-release fishing from May 1 to the Friday before the third Saturday in June on Burt, Mullett, Black, Crooked and Pickerel lakes, with possession opening the third Saturday in June. The printed 2026 digest doesn't show that change, so check the current Fisheries Order 206 or call the DNR before bass fishing these lakes in May and early June.

**Shore option:** The Burt Lake veterans pier in Tuscarora Township.

## 8. Torch Lake

**Where:** Antrim County, between Elk Rapids and Alden, northeast of Traverse City.
**Species (DNR):** Lake trout, smallmouth bass, lake whitefish, rock bass, Atlantic salmon.

Torch's Caribbean-blue water is famous with boaters, but it's a serious cold-water fishery. The newest story is Atlantic salmon: in September 2026 the DNR said "recent successful Atlantic salmon stocking" has produced an emerging fishery here and changed the Type E lake rules so salmon count in the 3-fish daily limit. Muskie are under a 50-inch minimum on Torch and the rest of the Elk River Chain of Lakes, and the Great Lakes muskie record was a Torch Lake fish (50.5 pounds) until Lake Bellaire beat it in 2012.

## 9. Grand Traverse Bay

**Where:** Lake Michigan's East and West arms at Traverse City.
**Species (DNR):** Smallmouth bass, lake trout, Chinook salmon, brown trout, lake whitefish, yellow perch.

Clear water, rock, sand flats and big smallmouth that see a lot of tubes. Grand Traverse Bay fishes like a giant inland lake with Great Lakes fish in it. Summer smallmouth on the flats, lake trout deep, and kings that stage in late summer. Because it's Great Lakes water, trout and salmon follow the Great Lakes rules: open all year, 10-inch minimum, 5 fish total with no more than 3 of any one species, except you may keep up to 5 Chinook, coho or pink salmon.

## 10. Hubbard Lake

**Where:** Alcona County, northeast Lower Peninsula, west of Ossineke.
**Species (DNR):** Smallmouth bass, walleye, yellow perch, northern pike, rock bass, bullhead.

Hubbard is a quiet, big northern lake that produced a state-record smallmouth: Greg Gasiciel's 9.33-pounder in October 2015, which held the record for less than a year. Walleye and perch fill the rest of the calendar, and it's a solid ice fishery.

## 11. Gull Lake

**Where:** Kalamazoo and Barry counties, between Kalamazoo and Battle Creek.
**Species (DNR):** Largemouth and smallmouth bass, bluegill, yellow perch, northern pike, lake trout.

A deep, clear southwest Michigan lake that holds both bass species and a two-story fishery with lake trout below. Gull is classified as a Type E trout lake (3 trout per day, 15-inch minimum) and has a protected slot on northern pike: release every pike from 24 to 34 inches, with a 2-fish daily limit. Clear water means light line and long casts.

## 12. Little Bay de Noc

**Where:** Lake Michigan's northern arm at Escanaba and Gladstone, Delta County, Upper Peninsula.
**Species:** Walleye, yellow perch, smallmouth, northern pike.

Little Bay de Noc is the Upper Peninsula's walleye headliner and a big ice fishery. The rule to know: north of a line from the Peninsula Point Lighthouse to the mouth of the Bark River, your daily limit may include no more than one walleye 23 inches or longer. Walleye possession in Upper Peninsula Great Lakes and inland waters, the bay included, opens May 15 and closes March 15. The splake record, 17.5 pounds, came from neighboring Big Bay de Noc.

## 13. Muskegon River and Muskegon Lake

**Where:** From Croton Dam in Newaygo County down to Muskegon Lake and Lake Michigan.
**Species (DNR):** Chinook salmon, steelhead, brown trout, smallmouth, walleye, pike, catfish; Muskegon Lake adds bass, panfish and perch.

The DNR's own description sells it: "very good" Chinook fishing in September and October, lake-run brown trout in the lower 10 miles in October and November, and "excellent" steelhead "from late October through June." The river is stocked with steelhead, Chinook, walleye, brown and rainbow trout. Upstream of Newaygo it's shallower and wadeable; below, it's deeper and better by boat. On Muskegon Lake, stocking of Great Lakes muskellunge started in 2013.

**Rules:** Special artificial-lure rules apply on the Muskegon upstream to Croton Dam, and the port of Muskegon has November single-hook rules. Read the Muskegon County exceptions first.

**Shore option:** Newaygo's riverfront walkway, Henning Park, and the Muskegon State Park channel with an accessible pier.

## 14. Au Sable River

**Where:** Grayling to Mio and on to Oscoda, Crawford and Oscoda counties.
**Species (DNR):** Brook trout, brown trout, rainbow trout; walleye and smallmouth downstream.

The Au Sable is Michigan's best-known trout river. The 9 miles of mainstream from Burton's Landing to Wakeley Bridge, the "Holy Waters," are artificial flies only, open all year, with no trout kept except one fish between 8 and 12 inches for kids under 12. Below that, the 8 miles from Wakeley Bridge to McMasters Bridge are artificial lures only, with a 2-trout limit, only one 18 inches or longer, and an 18-inch minimum on browns.

## 15. Pere Marquette River

**Where:** Lake and Mason counties, Baldwin to Ludington.
**Species (DNR):** Steelhead, Chinook and coho salmon, brown trout.

The "PM" is a classic salmon and steelhead river. From M-37 to Gleason's Landing, 8.5 miles are artificial flies only and no-kill except for kids under 12. Downstream, sections switch to Type 4 rules seasonally. Fall brings kings and the anglers who chase them; winter and spring belong to steelhead.

## How to choose

- **One weekend, best odds:** Detroit River walleye in April, Lake St. Clair smallmouth in July, Muskegon River kings in late September.
- **Family trip:** Houghton Lake for panfish, or a state park pier. See [beginner fishing spots](/fishing/michigan/beginner-fishing-spots).
- **Trophy hunting:** Lake Bellaire and Torch for muskie, Lake St. Clair for 5-pound smallmouth, Lake Michigan for kings.
