Michigan bass fishing comes down to two facts. First, smallmouth rule more of the map than in most states: the DNR notes that "as you travel northward, smallmouth are the dominant species," and the Great Lakes shoreline from Port Huron to Lake Erie is nationally known for them. Second, Michigan lets you fish for bass all year but only lets you keep them for about seven months, and less on Lake St. Clair. Get those two straight and the rest is just finding fish.

## Bass species in Michigan

**Smallmouth bass.** Rocky shorelines, gravel flats, rivers, and the clear northern lakes. The DNR says the usual smallmouth anglers see is 8 to 15 inches and under 3 pounds, but they reach 5 to 6 pounds where food and habitat allow. Lake St. Clair, the Detroit and St. Clair rivers, Grand Traverse Bay and the Inland Waterway lakes regularly beat that. More on the species in our [smallmouth bass guide](/species/smallmouth-bass).

**Largemouth bass.** Shallow, weedy lakes, reservoirs with timber, and the drowned river mouths along Lake Michigan. The DNR says Michigan largemouth "can grow larger than 20 inches and exceed 7 pounds" and are "seldom found deeper than 20 feet." Southern Michigan lakes and big weedy water like Houghton Lake hold the most. See our [largemouth bass guide](/species/largemouth-bass).

For practical purposes it's a two-species state, and the DNR notes both often come from the same lakes and even the same areas.

## Bass seasons and limits in 2026

This is the part people get wrong, so here it is straight from the 2026 regulations:

| Rule | Most waters (including the Great Lakes) | Lake St. Clair, St. Clair River, Detroit River |
|---|---|---|
| Catch-and-immediate-release | Open all year | Open all year |
| Possession season | Saturday before Memorial Day to Dec. 31 (May 23 to Dec. 31, 2026) | Third Saturday in June to Dec. 31 (June 20 to Dec. 31, 2026) |
| Minimum size | 14 inches | 14 inches |
| Daily limit | 5 largemouth and smallmouth combined | 5 combined |

**What "catch-and-immediate-release" means in Michigan.** The DNR defines it as returning fish to the water right away, "without injury and without holding in a livewell or similar device." Outside the possession season, a bass in the livewell is a bass in possession. A quick photo and release is fine. A weigh-in is not.

**Exceptions you should know about:**

- **Beaver Island Archipelago:** Within a half-mile of Beaver, Garden, High and Hog islands in Lake Michigan, bass possession runs July 1 to December 31, with CIR open all year.
- **10-inch minimum lakes:** Corey, Little Crooked and Stone lakes in Cass County; Pleasant (Big Pleasant) and Corey lakes in St. Joseph County; Cub Lake in Kalkaska County; and Pratt Lake in Kent County, among others. Check the county exceptions on pages 28 to 31 of the digest.
- **Northern Inland Waterway proposal:** In late 2025 the Natural Resources Commission considered an amendment that would close bass CIR from May 1 to the Friday before the third Saturday in June and move possession to the third Saturday in June on Burt, Mullett, Black, Crooked and Pickerel lakes. The printed 2026 digest doesn't list it, so check the current Fisheries Order 206 before fishing those lakes in spring.
- **Tournaments:** All bass tournaments must be registered with the DNR.

The license that covers all of this is on our [Michigan fishing license page](/fishing/michigan/fishing-license).

## Month-by-month bass pattern in Michigan

Michigan's bass calendar runs about three to four weeks behind the southern states. Water temperature, not the date, drives it. The DNR says smallmouth start spawning when water reaches 60°F or more, and largemouth spawn in late spring or early summer. Our explainer on [water temperature and fish activity](/blog/water-temperature-and-fish-activity) goes deeper.

| Month | Water temp (typical) | What bass are doing | Where to look |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jan to Mar | Ice or under 40°F | Slow, deep, barely feeding | Deep basin edges; CIR only and mostly through ice |
| April | 40s °F | Pre-spawn start, smallmouth on deep rock | Rivers warm first; Detroit and St. Clair rivers |
| May | 50s to low 60s °F | Pre-spawn feeding, best big-fish window | Shallow flats near deep water, sunny north shores |
| Early June | 60s °F | Smallmouth on beds in the north, largemouth spawning | Sand and gravel flats in 3 to 10 ft |
| Late June to July | High 60s to 70s °F | Post-spawn, then summer patterns | Weed edges, rock humps, 15 to 25 ft for smallmouth |
| August | 70s °F | Deep smallmouth, shallow-cover largemouth | Offshore structure, docks, lily pads |
| September | Falling into 60s °F | Fall feed starts | Points, transitions, baitfish schools |
| October | 50s °F | Heavy feeding, big smallmouth group up | Deep rock and drop-offs |
| Nov to Dec | 40s °F | Slows down, possession closes Dec. 31 | Deepest structure near wintering holes |

<p class="callout"><strong>Tip:</strong> Because CIR is open in May on most waters, Michigan's best big-smallmouth fishing of the year happens before you can legally keep one. Bring a camera and a bump board, take the photo fast, and get the fish back in.</p>

## The best bass lakes in Michigan

### Lake St. Clair (Detroit area)

The headline water. The DNR's 2022 Lake St. Clair status report found smallmouth trap-net catch and recruitment both up from 2021. It's big, shallow and wind-swept, so the best smallmouth days are calm ones, drifting flats in 6 to 15 feet. Possession opens later here (third Saturday in June), and the lake is shared with Ontario. Largemouth live in the marshes and canals on the north end.

### Detroit and St. Clair rivers

Smallmouth in current along walls, rock and channel edges. The St. Clair River is on the DNR's Better Fishing Waters list for smallmouth. Same late possession opener as Lake St. Clair.

### Grand Traverse Bay (Traverse City)

Clear Lake Michigan water with sand flats and rock. Summer smallmouth on the flats are visible from a tower or the bow, which also means they see you. Long casts and natural colors.

### Burt, Mullett and the Indian River (Cheboygan County)

The Inland Waterway produced the current state-record smallmouth: 9.98 pounds from the Indian River in 2016, on nightcrawlers. Burt and Mullett are both on the DNR's smallmouth list. Watch for the possible spring CIR change noted above.

### Hubbard Lake (Alcona County)

Hubbard gave up a 9.33-pound state-record smallmouth in October 2015. Rock, walleye and perch keep the bass fat.

### Lake Charlevoix (Charlevoix)

An honest note: the DNR's Better Fishing Waters list names Lake Charlevoix for brown trout and lake trout, not bass. Bass anglers do fish it, but if smallmouth are the only goal, Burt Lake or Grand Traverse Bay are surer bets. As a drowned river mouth lake, Charlevoix (with Round Lake) has its own trout and salmon rules: open all year, 10-inch minimum, 5 fish with no more than 3 of one species except Chinook and coho. Bass follow the statewide rules.

### Houghton Lake (Roscommon County)

Michigan's biggest inland lake at 20,075 acres, shallow and full of weeds. Largemouth and smallmouth both, with panfish and pike as bonus fish. Fish the weed edges.

### Gull Lake (Kalamazoo area)

Deep, clear, both species. Finesse water. It also has a pike protected slot (24 to 34 inches).

### Lake Macatawa, Spring Lake and the Ottawa County bayous

West Michigan's drowned river mouths and bayous (Pottawatomie, Stearns, Lloyds) are on the DNR's list for largemouth, with Lake Macatawa adding smallmouth. Pads, wood and shallow cover.

### Muskegon Lake

The DNR says Muskegon Lake fishes well "during summer and winter" for both bass, and smallmouth and largemouth can be caught "along the channel wall."

## Lures that work on Michigan bass

General advice says "throw a worm." Michigan's clear Great Lakes water and goby-heavy diets reward a slightly different box:

**Smallmouth**

- **Tube jig, 3/8 to 1/2 oz, green pumpkin or smoke-pepper.** Drag it on rock and sand in 8 to 20 feet. On Lake St. Clair and Grand Traverse Bay this is the bait everyone starts with.
- **Drop shot, 1/4 to 3/8 oz weight, 3 to 4 inch minnow or finesse worm.** For suspended or pressured fish, especially in summer on the Great Lakes.
- **Ned rig, 1/10 to 1/6 oz head.** The do-everything bait for tough days and post-front fish.
- **Suspending jerkbait, 4 to 4.5 inch, natural shad or ghost colors.** Pre-spawn in April and May when water is in the 40s and 50s.
- **Topwater walker or popper.** Calm summer mornings on the flats.

**Largemouth**

- **Texas-rigged soft plastic, 3/16 to 3/8 oz, green pumpkin or black and blue.** Weed edges and docks.
- **Wacky-rigged stick bait, 5 inch.** Spawning and post-spawn fish in shallow water.
- **Hollow-body frog.** Lily pads and matted weeds on southern lakes from late June.
- **Spinnerbait, 3/8 oz, white or chartreuse.** Windy days and stained water.
- **Swim jig, 3/8 oz.** Along the outside weed edge in summer.

## Trophy bass in Michigan

Michigan doesn't run a lunker-loan program like Texas ShareLunker. Its trophy recognition is the DNR's Master Angler program: any largemouth or smallmouth 21 inches or longer earns a patch, documented with a photo of the whole fish on a legible measuring device, with one patch per species per angler per year. The state records are 11.94 pounds for largemouth and 9.98 pounds for smallmouth, both judged by weight only.

How big is big here? A 21-inch smallmouth is a true trophy in most Michigan water. ## Handling bass during CIR season

Since much of Michigan's spring bass fishing is release-only, handle fish like you plan to catch them again:

- Wet hands, horizontal support for big fish, no jaw-hanging of heavy smallmouth.
- Keep the photo under 30 seconds.
- Barbless or crimped hooks make quick releases easier on beds.
- Smallmouth from deep water (25 feet and more) can suffer barotrauma; fish shallower in summer if you plan to release.
