Michigan keeps one of the plainest record systems in the country. There are no line classes, no separate fly or tip-up categories, and no catch-and-release record list. A fish either outweighs the current record on a commercial scale, with a DNR fisheries biologist confirming the species, or it doesn't. The DNR's table does note the method used for each fish, which is why a few records were set with a spear or a bow. That matters when you read the list: the 61.5-pound common carp and the 39-pound northern pike were both speared, and the current flathead catfish record was taken bowfishing.

Weights below are copied from the DNR's state record page as of November 2026, in decimal pounds, with our pounds-and-ounces conversion. The DNR table doesn't list anglers, so names appear only where a DNR announcement or news report published them.

## The biggest fish caught in Lake Michigan

People search this one constantly, so here is the honest answer from Michigan's list. The heaviest record fish taken from Lake Michigan itself is the **47.86-pound Chinook salmon** from 2021. Two older Lake Michigan records still stand: the **32.62-pound Atlantic salmon** (Mason County, 1981) and the **26.5-pound steelhead** (1975). The 17.5-pound splake came from Big Bay de Noc, a Lake Michigan bay in the Upper Peninsula.

The **41.45-pound brown trout** came from the Manistee River, a Lake Michigan tributary, so it was almost certainly a lake-run fish, but the record is listed under the river. The overall heaviest fish on Michigan's list, the 193-pound lake sturgeon, came from inland Mullett Lake.

## Four record stories worth knowing

### The 47.86-pound king off Ludington (2021)

On Saturday, August 7, 2021, Luis Ricardo Hernandez Martinez of Ortonville was trolling Lake Michigan near Ludington when a king hit a raspberry carbon Moonshine spoon. The fish measured 47.5 inches. Jay Wesley, the DNR's Lake Michigan Basin coordinator, verified it, and a DNR fisheries biologist weighed it on a certified scale. It broke a record that had stood since 1978: Ray Essex's 46.06-pound Chinook from the Grand River in Kent County.

### Two smallmouth records in eleven months (2015 to 2016)

Michigan's smallmouth record sat at 9.25 pounds, a Long Lake fish from Cheboygan County, from 1906 until October 2015, when Greg Gasiciel of Rhodes caught a 9.33-pound, 24.5-inch bronzeback on Hubbard Lake. It lasted less than a year. On September 11, 2016, Robert Bruce Kraemer of Treasure Island, Florida, who had fished Michigan since 1965 from his cottage in Indian River, landed a 9.98-pound, 23.1-inch smallmouth on nightcrawlers in the Indian River. DNR biologist Tim Cwalinski in Gaylord confirmed it. Note the shorter fish is the heavier one, which is why Michigan judges by weight.

### The Lake Bellaire muskie (2012)

At 10:30 a.m. on October 13, 2012, Joseph Seeberger of Portage boated a Great Lakes muskellunge on Lake Bellaire in Antrim County: 58 pounds, 59 inches long, 29 inches around. DNR biologist Patrick Hanchin and a conservation officer verified it. It replaced a 50.5-pound Torch Lake fish caught by Kyle Anderson in 2009, only three years earlier, in the same Antrim County chain of lakes.

### A bowfishing flathead from Plum Creek (2025)

Early on June 29, 2025, Codie Carlson of Newport arrowed a 64.46-pound, 45-inch flathead catfish in Plum Creek, Monroe County, near Lake Erie. It beat Lloyd Tanner's 53.35-pound St. Joseph River flathead from 2022 by more than 11 pounds. DNR biologist John Buszkiewicz identified it and noted his survey crew may have handled the same fish at the same spot in 2020, when it weighed 55 pounds.

Records are still falling: on April 22, 2026, Sedrick Neinhuis of Lowell caught a 4.45-pound gizzard shad in the Grand River, the newest entry on the list. And the Manistee River brown trout was Tom Healy's, caught in September 2009 with Cadillac guide Tim Roller; Interlochen Public Radio reported state biologists verified it at 41 pounds 7 ounces.

## How to submit a Michigan state record

The DNR's rules are short. Follow them in order, because the clock matters:

1. **Don't release it, and don't clean it.** A record needs the whole fish for species identification.
2. **Weigh it on a commercial scale.** A grocery store or meat market scale works. Write down the business name, and get a printed weight slip if possible.
3. **Call a DNR fisheries biologist as soon as possible.** Identification must be verified by a DNR fisheries biologist. Keep the fish cold.
4. **Beat the current weight.** Your fish must exceed the listed record weight. Length and girth don't count.
5. **Send the application.** Fill out the state record application form and email it to the DNR (the Master Angler page lists Caitlin Boon at BoonC@Michigan.gov as the contact as of November 2026).

<p class="callout"><strong>Tip:</strong> Before you head to the scale, take a clear side-profile photo on a measuring board. If it isn't a record, that photo is still your Master Angler entry and your proof of a personal best.</p>

## Master Angler: the record most anglers can actually get

Michigan's Master Angler program awards a patch to any fish that meets a minimum length for more than 45 species. Entries are online, need at least one photo showing the whole fish on a legible measuring device, and must be caught legally in public Michigan waters. Measure in a straight line from the tip of the nose or lower jaw to the tip of the tail, not along the curve of the body, to the nearest eighth of an inch. Our guide on [how to measure a fish](/blog/how-to-measure-a-fish) covers board technique. Applications for fish caught in 2026 are accepted until January 10, 2027.

## How rare is a record-class fish?

Very. Look at the dates. The walleye record is from 1951, the yellow perch and black crappie from 1947, and the largemouth from 1934. A 21-inch largemouth already earns a Master Angler patch, and Michigan's DNR says largemouth here "can grow larger than 20 inches and exceed 7 pounds," which tells you how far an 11.94-pounder sits outside the normal range.
