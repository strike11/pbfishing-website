Most "fishing calendar" pages are written for the whole country and miss what makes Michigan tricky: the same species can be legal to keep on Saginaw Bay, release-only on an inland lake, and closed on a trout stream, all in the same week. This page splits it out. The first table is the bite calendar, the second is the legal calendar, and then each key species gets its own short section.

All season dates are from the 2026 Michigan Fishing Regulations and the DNR's March 2026 season announcement. Spawning notes come from the DNR's species profiles. Season rules apply to the April 1, 2026 to March 31, 2027 license year.

## Month-by-month bite calendar

| Month | What's biting | Where to go |
|---|---|---|
| January | Perch, walleye, pike, bluegill through the ice | Saginaw Bay, Little Bay de Noc, Houghton Lake |
| February | Same ice bite; Free Fishing Weekend | Inland lakes with safe ice; check DNR ice guidance |
| March | Late ice, river steelhead, early Detroit River walleye | Muskegon, Pere Marquette, Detroit River |
| April | Detroit River walleye run, steelhead, trout opener (last Sat.) | Detroit River, Au Sable, Manistee |
| May | Walleye, pike, pre-spawn smallmouth (release only), crappie | Lake Erie, Saginaw Bay, northern lakes; UP opens May 15 |
| June | Bass spawn and possession opener, bluegill beds, muskie opener | Inland lakes, Lake St. Clair (keep from 3rd Sat.) |
| July | Smallmouth on Great Lakes structure, salmon trolling, bluegill | Lake St. Clair, Grand Traverse Bay, Lake Michigan ports |
| August | Kings staging off river mouths, deep smallmouth | Ludington, Muskegon, Manistee piers and ports |
| September | Chinook in rivers, fall bass, Type 1 and 2 trout close Sept. 30 | Muskegon, Pere Marquette, Manistee |
| October | Salmon tail end, brown trout, big fall smallmouth, muskie | Lower Muskegon, Torch and Bellaire, Lake St. Clair |
| November | Steelhead, walleye trolling, lake-run browns | Muskegon, Grand and Manistee rivers |
| December | Steelhead, first ice up north; bass possession closes Dec. 31 | Rivers; shallow bays as ice forms |

"Sunfish" in Michigan's rules includes black and white crappie, bluegill, pumpkinseed, redear, green and hybrid sunfish, longear, rock bass and warmouth. Many lakes have exceptions, and Saginaw Bay walleye are 8 fish with a 13-inch minimum. Bass details are on [Michigan bass fishing](/fishing/michigan/bass-fishing).

<p class="callout warn"><strong>Check first:</strong> On designated trout streams, the closed season closes the water to all fishing, not just trout. Don't plan an October bass float on a Type 1 stream.</p>

## Walleye

**Spawn:** March, April and even May, over rock shoals in tributaries or lakes, per the DNR.
**Best months:** April for the Detroit River run, May and June on Lake Erie and Saginaw Bay, then again under the ice.

Walleye "feed actively all winter," which is why Michigan has a strong ice fishery for them. Early in the season the DNR recommends bottom jigs tipped with minnows or plastic, switching to trolled crankbaits, spoons and crawler harnesses as water warms. Prime feeding is early morning and evening. Note the Detroit River limit is six fish per angler, Saginaw Bay is eight at 13 inches, and Little Bay de Noc allows only one fish 23 inches or more. See our [walleye guide](/species/walleye).

## Yellow perch

**Spawn:** Spring, in gelatinous egg strings over weeds, roots and fallen trees in shallow water. The DNR says those spawning areas "provide some of the best perch fishing available."
**Best months:** Late spring, fall, and through the ice.

Perch are open all year with no size limit and a 25-fish limit, raised to 50 on Lake Erie. Lake Gogebic is stricter: 25 with no more than 5 at 12 inches or longer. The state record is 3.75 pounds from Lake Independence in 1947.

## Smallmouth bass

**Spawn:** When water hits 60°F or more, per the DNR, typically late May into June in Michigan.
**Best months:** May (release only on most waters), then July through October.

The best big-fish weeks fall in catch-and-immediate-release season. Lake St. Clair, the Detroit and St. Clair rivers, Grand Traverse Bay and the northern Lower Peninsula lakes lead. Full patterns on the [bass page](/fishing/michigan/bass-fishing) and our [smallmouth guide](/species/smallmouth-bass).

## Largemouth bass

**Spawn:** Late spring or early summer.
**Best months:** June through September in weedy southern and central lakes.

The DNR notes largemouth are most active in 70 to 85°F water and rarely deeper than 20 feet in Michigan. Frogs and Texas rigs in the weeds, wacky rigs around docks.

## Bluegill and sunfish

**Spawn:** In the shallows in late spring or early summer when water reaches 65°F, per the DNR. Males build nesting colonies and guard eggs.
**Best months:** June on the beds, then all summer; through the ice in winter.

This is the fish for kids. Open all year, no size limit, 25 per day combined with other sunfish and crappie. The state-record bluegill weighed 2.75 pounds (Vaughn Lake, 1983). See our [bluegill guide](/species/bluegill).

## Crappie

**Spawn:** Among the first panfish to move shallow in spring, per the DNR, when open-water crappie fishing peaks.
**Best months:** April and May in the shallows; suspended over deeper water after the spawn; ice in winter.

Black crappie favor weed beds, white crappie brush and timber. More in our [crappie guide](/species/crappie).

## Northern pike

**Spawn:** In the shallows in April or May, right after ice-out and before muskie.
**Season:** Same openers as walleye. 24-inch minimum and 2 per day statewide, but many lakes allow 5 of any size with only one 24 inches or more, and lakes like Gull Lake have a 24 to 34 inch protected slot.

## Muskellunge

**Spawn:** Early spring shortly after ice-out, after pike, in 15 to 20 inches of water at about 55°F.
**Season:** Catch-and-release all year; possession from the first Saturday in June (June 6 in 2026). Minimum 42 inches statewide, but 38, 46 or 50 inches on specific named waters, and only one muskie per angler per license year. Register any harvested muskie within 24 hours.

## Chinook salmon

**Run:** The DNR says Chinook begin moving upstream in late summer and are usually catchable by mid-August. On the Muskegon River below Croton Dam, September and October are "very good."
**Best months:** July and August trolling in Lake Michigan, August to October in rivers and off piers.

Great Lakes salmon are open all year, 10-inch minimum, up to 5 Chinook, coho or pink salmon in the daily limit.

## Steelhead

**Run:** Lake steelhead enter streams from late October to early May. Fall-run fish often spawn in March, spring-run fish in April, per the DNR.
**Best months:** November through April on the Muskegon, Pere Marquette, Manistee and Grand.

The DNR says the average adult is 6 to 7 pounds, with some reaching 36 inches and 20 pounds. See our [rainbow trout and steelhead guide](/species/rainbow-trout).

## Brown and brook trout

**Spawn:** Brown trout spawn in tributary streams in September and October.
**Season:** Depends on stream type. On Type 1 streams (about 1,400 of them), fishing is open the last Saturday in April to September 30, with 7-inch brook trout and 8-inch brown trout minimums. Type 3 streams are open all year with 15-inch minimums for both. Gear-restricted water like the Au Sable Holy Waters is open all year, flies-only and no-kill.

## Lake trout

**Spawn:** Fall, on shoals and reefs, which pulls some fish within reach of piers.
**Best months:** Spring and fall shallow; summer deep trolling; winter through the ice on bays and inland lakes like Higgins.

Lake Superior's lake trout limits are set by management unit and changed for Stannard Rock and Big Reef in 2026, so check pages 20 and 21 of the digest. The state record is 61.5 pounds from Lake Superior in 1997.

## Ice season

Michigan's ice season is roughly January into March, depending on the year and latitude, and it's when Saginaw Bay, Little Bay de Noc, Houghton Lake and Higgins Lake draw crowds for perch, walleye, pike and lake trout. Pike and walleye possession on inland waters closes March 15. Never trust ice by the calendar; check the DNR's ice safety guidance and local reports before you step out.
