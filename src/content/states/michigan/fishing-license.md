Michigan makes the license itself simple: one license, every species, inland and Great Lakes. The confusion starts with the edges. Does a kid need one? Does the parent helping the kid? What about catch and release, a visiting cousin from Ohio, or a 72-hour license you remember buying years ago? Here are straight answers, all taken from the DNR's license pages and the 2026 Michigan Fishing Regulations.

All prices and rules below are as of November 2026, checked on the Michigan DNR site. The DNR notes that fees can change with the consumer price index, so confirm at [Michigan.gov/DNRLicenses](https://www.mdnr-elicense.com/) before you buy.

## Who needs a Michigan fishing license

- **Age 17 and older:** You need a license to fish public waters in Michigan.
- **Under 17:** No license needed, but you must follow every rule, limit and season.
- **Turning 17 mid-season:** If you turn 17 at any point during the April 1 to March 31 season, buy a license.
- **What counts as fishing:** The DNR says a license is required when "targeting fish, amphibians, crustaceans and reptiles." That covers catch and release too. If you're casting at fish, you need the license, whether you keep them or not.

### Adults helping kids

This is the one that catches parents. Any adult "actively assisting a minor" must have a license. But the DNR lists what an unlicensed adult may still do:

- Help land a fish with a net or by hand
- Unhook a fish
- Set up the rod with the right gear
- Bait the hook
- Fix tangles or snags
- Cast for the young angler, as long as the child is the active participant

If you pick up a second rod and start fishing yourself, you need a license.

### Private ponds

The DNR's license rule is written for "public waters." If you're fishing a genuinely private pond, call the DNR license help desk at 517-284-6057 before assuming you're exempt. Don't take a neighbor's word for it.

## Michigan fishing license prices for 2026

| License | Price | Who it's for |
|---|---|---|
| Resident annual (all species) | $26 | Michigan residents |
| Nonresident annual (all species) | $76 | Everyone else |
| Senior annual (all species) | $11 | Residents 65+ or residents who are legally blind |
| Daily fish license (24 hours) | $10 per day | Residents and nonresidents; you set the start date and time |
| Voluntary youth license | $2 | Anglers 16 and under (optional) |
| Underwater spearfishing | Free | Residents and nonresidents; a DNR Sportcard may be needed |
| DNR Sportcard | $1 | ID card issued by license dealers |
| Hunt/Fish combo, resident | $76 | Base hunting, annual fishing and 2 deer licenses |
| Hunt/Fish combo, senior resident | $43 | Same bundle for residents 65+ |
| Hunt/Fish combo, nonresident | $266 | Same bundle for nonresidents |

The resident, nonresident and senior annual licenses, the youth license and the combos include a $1 surcharge that funds outreach on hunting, fishing and trapping, per the 2026 digest.

**What about a 72-hour license?** Michigan's current fee list, both on the DNR license page and in the 2026 digest, shows only the 24-hour daily license at $10 per day, "purchased as needed." A three-day trip means three daily licenses, $30. If you're fishing more than seven days in a season as a nonresident, the $76 annual starts to make sense; as a resident, the annual pays for itself on the third day.

**Is there a lifetime license?** The DNR's 2026 fee list doesn't include one, so we can't point you to a price.

## Exemptions and free licenses

Michigan has no age at which fishing becomes free for seniors. What it does have:

- **Active-duty military (residents):** License fees are waived for full-time, federal, active-duty U.S. military personnel who kept Michigan resident status. Bring military ID or orders plus a Michigan driver's license or voter registration card. Nonresident active-duty members stationed in Michigan qualify for resident rates, but not the waiver.
- **Disabled veterans (residents):** Free if the VA rates you permanently and totally disabled from service at the 100% rate (for a disability other than blindness) or individually unemployable. Carry the VA documentation when buying and while fishing.
- **Legally blind residents:** Eligible for the $11 senior license.
- **Developmental disabilities:** A free mentored hunting license, which includes an all-species fishing license, is available for an individual with a developmental disability and a court-appointed guardian.
- **Residents of a licensed home for the aged:** May get a DNR permit to fish without a license when in a group accompanied by at least one licensed adult. Permits come from DNR Customer Service Centers.

### Free Fishing Weekends

Twice a year the DNR waives all fishing license fees for two days. Residents and visitors can fish inland and Great Lakes waters for every species, and all other rules still apply. A Recreation Passport isn't required for state parks and boating access sites those weekends.

- **Winter:** the Saturday and Sunday of Presidents Day weekend. In 2026: February 14 to 15.
- **Summer:** the Saturday and Sunday after the first full week of June, held with the "Three Free" weekend (free ORV riding and state park entry too). In 2026: June 13 to 14.

By the DNR's rule, the next winter Free Fishing Weekend lands on February 13 to 14, 2027. Confirm on the [DNR Free Fishing Weekend page](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/things-to-do/fishing/free-fishing-weekend) before you go.

## Resident or nonresident?

You qualify for resident prices if you live in a settled, permanent home in Michigan and intend to stay, if you're a full-time student at a Michigan college living in the state, or if you serve full-time in the U.S. military and are stationed in Michigan or keep Michigan residency. Owning a cabin in Michigan does not by itself make you a resident.

## How to buy a Michigan fishing license

1. **Online** at Michigan.gov/DNRLicenses (the eLicense site). You get a PDF of printable licenses by email. Save it.
2. **Michigan DNR Hunt Fish app.** Buy and store licenses on your phone, and turn on auto-renew.
3. **License agents.** Sporting goods stores and other retailers; the DNR keeps a list at Michigan.gov/DNRLicenseAgents.
4. **DNR Customer Service Centers.**

To buy, you need a Michigan driver's license or state ID, a driver's license from your home state, or a DNR Sportcard. Problems online? The License Sales Help Desk is 517-284-6057.

**Lost license:** If you bought online, log in and reprint it. A license agent can reprint non-kill tag items for $3 with the ID number you used originally.

## Carry it, and the ID you bought it with

When fishing, you must carry your license and the identification used to buy it and show both to a conservation officer or any law enforcement officer who asks. The DNR's electronic license lets you display it on a phone. Agency apps don't always cooperate when your signal drops at the launch, so a saved PDF or a screenshot in your photos is cheap insurance.

<p class="callout"><strong>Tip:</strong> Buy online before you leave home. Rural ramps on the Au Sable or in the U.P. can have zero bars, and a license you can't pull up is a conversation with an officer you don't need.</p>

## When does a Michigan license expire?

Annual licenses are valid from March 1 of a given year through March 31 of the following year. The fishing and regulation season itself starts April 1. So a 2026 license went on sale March 1, 2026 and is good through March 31, 2027. The daily license runs 24 hours from the start time you choose.

## Rules that come with the license

The license is the easy part. The rules that actually get anglers ticketed in Michigan:

- **Limits and sizes by species.** Statewide, bass are 14 inches minimum, 5 in any combination; walleye 15 inches, 5 per day; northern pike 24 inches, 2 per day; muskellunge 42 inches with only 1 per angler per license year. Many waters have exceptions, including Saginaw Bay walleye (8 fish, 13 inches). The full picture is on the [species and seasons page](/fishing/michigan/fish-species-seasons).
- **Seasons by water type.** Bass are catch-and-immediate-release only before the possession opener. Type 1 and 2 trout streams are closed to all fishing after September 30. Details on [Michigan bass fishing](/fishing/michigan/bass-fishing).
- **Muskie harvest registration.** Any kept muskellunge must be registered within 24 hours online, in the Hunt Fish app, by phone at 888-636-7778, or at a DNR Customer Service Center.
- **Tournament registration.** Bass and walleye tournaments and muskie contests must be registered with the DNR.
- **Overlimits are a misdemeanor.** In May 2026 the DNR cited five anglers who were 34 walleye over the Detroit River daily limit of six per angler.

Report poaching to the DNR at 800-292-7800 (call or text).

## Fishing in neighboring states

Two border situations come up often:

- **Michigan-Wisconsin boundary waters** (the Menominee and Brule rivers, Lac Vieux Desert, the Cisco Chain and others): a valid Michigan or Wisconsin license lets you fish either state's side, but you follow the regulations of the side you're on.
- **Michigan-Indiana border lakes** (South Clear, Indiana, Fish, George and Long lakes): residents of either state may fish the whole lake under the rules of the water they're on. Nonresident license holders may only fish the side their license covers.

The Michigan license stops at the international line on Lake St. Clair, the Detroit River and the Great Lakes. Canadian water falls under Ontario's licensing, so watch your GPS when the smallmouth pull you east.
