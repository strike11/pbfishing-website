The most common beginner mistake in Michigan is driving to a famous lake. Big water is hard to read from shore, and a 20 mph wind on Lake St. Clair will turn a first trip into a lesson in untangling. Start small: a pier, a park pond, a river wall in town. You want fish within casting distance, a bathroom nearby, and somewhere a kid can sit down.

Every spot below comes from a DNR source: the accessible-pier list, the free fishing class locations, the Better Fishing Waters database, or a DNR management unit page. If you want more, the DNR's [Family Friendly Fishing Waters map](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/things-to-do/fishing/where/fffw) filters for pier, shore, restrooms and entry fee.

## Before you go: the three rules beginners ask about

1. **License.** Anyone 17 or older needs one; under 17 fishes free. A resident annual is $26 and a 24-hour license is $10 (2026 prices). Details on [Michigan fishing license](/fishing/michigan/fishing-license).
2. **Helping kids.** A parent who actually fishes needs a license. A parent who only baits, casts for, untangles, nets or unhooks for a child does not, per the DNR.
3. **State parks.** Most state parks need a Recreation Passport for your vehicle. It's waived on the two Free Fishing Weekends, when license fees are waived too: in 2026, February 14 to 15 and June 13 to 14.

## Free classes with loaner rods

The DNR's **Hook, Line and Sinker** program teaches knot tying, setting up a pole, casting, bait and unhooking. Classes are free, kids under 17 need no license, and staff "can provide a fishing pole if you don't have your own." Not every site runs a class every week, so call first. ## Beginner spots near Detroit

### 1. Belle Isle Park (Detroit)

A 985-acre island park in the Detroit River with fishing piers and shore fishing. The Detroit River is on the DNR's list for walleye, smallmouth, perch, rock bass, bluegill and catfish. For a beginner, perch and rock bass off a pier with a worm are the realistic goal. Current is strong, so use enough weight to hold bottom.

### 2. Milliken State Park (downtown Detroit)

On the downtown Detroit riverfront. Same Detroit River fish as Belle Isle, plus the free summer classes listed above. Great for a first trip with kids who'd rather be near the city than the woods.

### 3. Maybury State Park (Northville)

Maybury has a fishing pond with an accessible pier, and the park lends a track chair for visitors with limited mobility. A pond this size is the easiest place in metro Detroit to get a kid their first bluegill.

### 4. Dodge #4 State Park, Cass Lake (Waterford)

The DNR says Cass Lake has an "excellent reputation" for largemouth and smallmouth, pike, bluegill, perch and trout, and the park added a universally accessible pier. Cass is also on the DNR's Better Fishing Waters list for walleye and crappie.

### 5. Pontiac Lake Recreation Area (Waterford)

"Excellent fishing for bass, pike and panfish," per the DNR, with two accessible piers at the beach area. A solid summer evening spot.

### 6. Holly Recreation Area (Holly)

An accessible pier on Heron Lake, with bass, bluegill, walleye, pike and crappie in the park's lakes. Holly also hosts Hook, Line and Sinker classes.

### 7. Sterling State Park (Monroe)

On Lake Erie, and the DNR says it's "known for its walleye fishing." Beginners do best in the three lagoons and on the three fishing piers, where panfish and bass are more forgiving than open-lake walleye. Free classes here too.

### 8. Stony Creek Lake (Macomb County)

On the DNR's list for largemouth, pike, catfish and bullhead. The state-record white crappie, 3.39 pounds, came from here in 2000.

## Beginner spots near Grand Rapids and West Michigan

### 9. Reeds Lake (East Grand Rapids)

A city lake on the DNR's Better Fishing Waters list for bluegill, crappie, largemouth, pike and perch. Bluegill under a bobber near weeds is the starter play.

### 10. Grand River downtown (Grand Rapids)

The DNR lists the Grand River in Kent County for catfish, smallmouth and largemouth, pike, walleye, and Chinook, coho and steelhead runs. Beginners should stick to catfish and bass in summer from the banks; fall salmon crowds are not a beginner scene. Note that muskie below Sixth Street Dam carry a 46-inch minimum.

### 11. Yankee Springs Recreation Area (Middleville)

A barrier-free pier on the Gun Lake channel, and Gun Lake itself is on the DNR list for bluegill, crappie, both bass, pike, walleye and perch. Hook, Line and Sinker classes run here.

### 12. Grand Haven State Park pier (Grand Haven)

The pier and boardwalk at the mouth of the Grand River are "popular with anglers," per the DNR, and the DNR lists the Grand Haven and Holland piers for perch, catfish, walleye, steelhead and salmon. Watch the waves; Lake Michigan piers are dangerous in high wind.

## More easy spots around the state

### 13. Sleepy Hollow State Park, Lake Ovid (Laingsburg, near Lansing)

Two fishing piers and shore access on a lake the DNR calls "very popular," with muskie, both bass, bluegill, crappie, perch and catfish. Classes run here.

### 14. Muskegon State Park and the Newaygo riverfront

Muskegon State Park has an accessible pier along the Lake Michigan channel; the DNR says bass can be caught along the channel wall. Upriver, the City of Newaygo has a riverfront walkway "along the entire river" from Bridge Street to the M-37 bridge, with stocked rainbow and brown trout that bite "throughout the year on any type of gear." Parts of the Muskegon have special hook and lure rules, so read the county exceptions in the digest before you go.

### 15. Mitchell State Park (Cadillac)

Two accessible piers at each end of the Clam Lake Canal and a third near the campground playground. A good family camping and fishing combo.

## Simple rigs that work at all of these spots

You only need two setups to start:

**Bobber rig for bluegill, perch and crappie.** A 5 to 6 foot light spinning rod with 6-pound line, a small round bobber set 2 to 4 feet above a #8 or #10 hook, one split shot 6 inches above the hook, and a piece of nightcrawler or a wax worm. Cast near weeds, docks or the pier pilings. When the bobber goes under, lift.

**Bottom rig for catfish and river fish.** A 1/4 to 1/2-ounce egg sinker above a swivel, 18 inches of leader and a #4 to #2 hook with a nightcrawler or cut bait. Use this on the Grand or Detroit rivers where current moves a bobber too fast.

Bluegill are open all year with no size limit and 25 per day combined with other sunfish. If you're keeping fish to eat, check the MDHHS Eat Safe Fish Guide for the lake first.

<p class="callout"><strong>Tip:</strong> Take a photo of the first fish on a ruler before it goes back. Kids remember the number, and the DNR has printable "My First Fish" certificates on its Family Friendly Fishing Waters page.</p>

## Family tips that actually help

- **Go for an hour, not a day.** Early morning or the last two hours before dark. Our [best time to fish](/blog/best-time-to-fish) guide explains why.
- **Pick spots with bathrooms and shade.** Every state park on this list has both.
- **Bring a bucket of water** for a minute of fish-watching, then release.
- **Practice the release.** Wet hands, quick photo. See [how to release a fish](/blog/how-to-release-a-fish).
- **If nothing bites, move.** Twenty minutes without a nibble usually means no fish there. More fixes in [why am I not catching fish](/blog/why-am-i-not-catching-fish).
