TPWD calls largemouth bass the most popular freshwater sport fish in Texas, and the reason is more management than luck. Texas's native largemouth was a stream fish. When the state filled with reservoirs in the 1960s and 1970s, TPWD brought in the Florida largemouth in 1971, stocked it into a few private lakes in 1972, then into public water. As Florida genes spread, the state record went from 13.5 lb (held since 1943) to 14.1 lb in 1980 and, after several more breaks, to the 18.18-lb Lake Fork fish of 1992. TPWD's hatcheries still put 6 to 8 million Florida and northern largemouth into Texas water in a typical year.

## The five black bass you can catch in Texas

| Species | Where | Texas record (rod and reel) | Minimum length |
|---|---|---|---|
| Largemouth (native and Florida) | Statewide | 18.18 lb, Lake Fork, 1992 | 14 in (many lake exceptions) |
| Smallmouth | Texoma, Lake Meredith, Amistad, Ivie | 7.93 lb, Lake Meredith, 1998 | 14 in |
| Spotted | East Texas lakes, Alan Henry | 5.56 lb, Lake O' the Pines, 1966 | None |
| Guadalupe | Hill Country streams only | 3.71 lb, Colorado River, 2014 | None |
| Alabama | Alan Henry (state record water) | 6.5 lb, Alan Henry, 2026 | None |

**Largemouth** is the headline fish. TPWD notes individual fish can live up to 16 years, and in many reservoirs a full-grown one is the top predator.

**Smallmouth** were stocked into the Edwards Plateau from 1974 and TPWD still raises about a million smallmouth fingerlings a year. On Lake Texoma, which TPWD rates excellent for smallmouth, look along the bluffs around Eisenhower State Park and Denison Dam.

**Guadalupe bass** is the official state fish and lives only in Edwards Plateau streams: the San Antonio, Guadalupe and Colorado headwaters and some Brazos tributaries. It stays small because it is built for current. The smallmouth stocking above is the biggest threat to it, through hybridization, and TPWD now stocks pure Guadalupe bass in the Llano, Pedernales, Guadalupe and Blanco rivers. At Lost Maples State Natural Area, all black bass are catch and release.

**Spotted and Alabama bass** look alike to most anglers. The quick test from TPWD's Alan Henry page: on a largemouth the closed jaw reaches past the eye; on a spotted bass it stops near the center of the eye.

## Texas bass fishing month by month

Dates shift across a state this big. TPWD's rule of thumb is that spawning starts when shallow water reaches about 60°F, as early as February or as late as May depending on where you are. Males build nests in 2 to 8 feet of water. Use water temperature, not the calendar.

| Month | What bass are doing | What to throw |
|---|---|---|
| January | Pre-spawn giants staging on deep points and humps. ShareLunker Legacy season opens. | Jigging spoons, jigs, Carolina-rigged worms on 20+ ft humps (Ivie) |
| February | Females move toward spawning flats as water warms; fish in the warmest water may already be bedding. | Lipless crankbaits over grass, suspending jerkbaits, 1/2-oz jigs |
| March | TPWD's pick for a trophy: heavy females near spawning areas. | Spinnerbaits, soft-plastic lizards and worms on shoreline cover |
| April | Spawn wraps up on most lakes; post-spawn fish recover near the beds. | Texas-rigged creature baits, weightless worms around beds |
| May | Post-spawn, shad spawn early mornings. | Topwater walking baits at first light, swim jigs |
| June | TPWD says fish start moving deeper by June. | Deep cranks, Carolina rigs on ledges and creek channels |
| July–August | Heat. Feed early, late and at night. | Topwater at low light; big worms and jigs on brush piles at noon; night spinnerbaits |
| September–October | Schooling bass chase shad on the surface. | Topwaters, small crankbaits, spoons into breaking fish |
| November–December | Fish follow shad into creeks, then slide deep as water cools. | Crankbaits, jigs, spoons |

For the science behind the timing, see [water temperature and fish activity](/blog/water-temperature-and-fish-activity) and [barometric pressure and fishing](/blog/barometric-pressure-and-fishing). Other species are on our [Texas fish species and seasons](/fishing/texas/fish-species-seasons) calendar.

## Best bass lakes in Texas right now

Two lists tell you where the big ones are. TPWD's Top 50 tells you the history: Lake Fork 25 fish, O.H. Ivie 7, Caddo 4, and one each from Sam Rayburn, Conroe, Amistad, Falcon, Possum Kingdom and others. The 2026 ShareLunker Legacy season tells you the present: 18 bass of 13 lb or more from 10 public reservoirs, namely O.H. Ivie (five, the most for the sixth straight season), J.B. Thomas, Alan Henry, Lake Tyler, Toledo Bend, Fort Phantom Hill, Hords Creek, Purtis Creek State Park Lake, Ellison Creek Reservoir and Lake Fork.

A few patterns worth noticing:

- **West Texas is hot.** Ivie, J.B. Thomas, Alan Henry, Fort Phantom Hill and Hords Creek are all West Texas lakes. Ivie alone has thousands of acres of flooded mesquite, oak and juniper for cover.
- **Small water counts.** Purtis Creek State Park Lake gave up a 16.04-lb bass to Austin Miles in February 2026, tied for 27th heaviest on TPWD's list. Lady Bird Lake in downtown Austin produced a 14.05-lb fish in 2025.
- **East Texas is steady.** Lake Fork, Toledo Bend, Sam Rayburn and Caddo produce good bass every year, and more of them.

## Lures that work on Texas water

Texas lakes fall into a few types, and TPWD's lake pages say clearly what works in each. Here is how we'd rig for them.

**Hydrilla lakes (Fork, Rayburn, Toledo Bend, Conroe).** Fish grass edges, flats and humps. In spring, a 1/2-oz red or chrome lipless crankbait ripped out of the tops of the grass. In summer, a Carolina rig with a 3/4-oz weight and a green pumpkin lizard or a 10-inch worm along the deep grass line.

**Flooded brush and saltcedar (Ivie, Choke Canyon, Falcon at high water).** Flip a 1/2-oz jig or a Texas-rigged creature bait with a 1/2-oz weight. Choke Canyon's TPWD page says to use at least 20-lb line in thick cover, and it is right.

**Rock reservoirs (Amistad, Alan Henry).** Crankbaits along rocky banks, Texas- or Carolina-rigged worms and grubs at midday, topwaters and buzzbaits early and late. Clear water means lighter line and natural colors.

**Cypress and pads (Caddo).** A weightless soft-plastic stickbait, a frog, or a spinnerbait slow-rolled past knees. TPWD says March is the top trophy month on Caddo.

**Night (Lady Bird, Fork in summer).** White spinnerbaits, black buzzbaits, and big dark worms. TPWD calls Lady Bird an excellent night lake.

## Bass regulations in Texas

Statewide, largemouth and smallmouth have a **14-inch minimum**, spotted, Guadalupe and Alabama bass have **no minimum**, and the **daily bag is 5 black bass** in any combination. Possession is twice the daily bag. Bass are game fish, so pole and line only.

The exceptions matter more than the rule on the best lakes:

| Water | Largemouth rule | Bag |
|---|---|---|
| Lake Fork | 16–24 in slot; keep 16 in and under or 24 in and over, max one 24 in+ per day | 5 |
| Caddo Lake | 14–18 in slot; keep 14 in and under or 18 in and over | 8 black bass, max 4 largemouth 18 in+; possession 10 |
| O.H. Ivie | No minimum, but only 2 under 18 in per day | 5 |
| Lake Conroe | 16 in minimum | 5 |
| Toledo Bend | 14 in minimum | 8, possession 10 |
| Neighborhood Fishin' lakes | Only 1 bass 14 in or longer | 5 fish, all species combined |
| Lost Maples SNA | Catch and release only | 0 |

Check TPWD's page for your lake before every season; exceptions change. Our [Texas fishing license](/fishing/texas/fishing-license) page covers what license you need.

## ShareLunker: Texas's trophy program

ShareLunker has run since 1986 and turned 40 in 2026. It is the reason Texas knows where its biggest bass live.

**Year-round classes** (bass of 8 lb or 24 inches and up from Texas public water):

- Lunker Class: 8 lb and up
- Elite Class: 10 lb and up
- Legend Class: 13 lb and up

**Legacy Class:** a largemouth of 13 lb or more caught January 1 through March 31 can be loaned to TPWD. These fish go to the Texas Freshwater Fisheries Center in Athens for spawning, big females are paired with pure Florida males, and the offspring are stocked into public lakes. At the end of the season most fish go back to their home lake.

**How to enter:** log the catch in the free Toyota ShareLunker app or at TexasSharelunker.com. For a possible Legacy fish, call the 24/7 hotline at (903) 681-0550 during the January to March window. Legacy fish must be weighed on a certified scale; TPWD can certify the weight on site. You can also send a scale sample for DNA testing. Legacy anglers get a replica mount, and every entry gets a catch kit and a drawing entry.

The 2026 season set marks. Josh Jones caught his fifth Legacy fish, the most by one angler. Lawrence Lee became the first to land two Legacy fish in a day and three in a season from public water. Ross Gomez caught the same Alan Henry bass for a third time. Ted Taylor closed the season with a 14.22-lb fish from O.H. Ivie, ShareLunker 701. Eighty-one public reservoirs have now produced a Legacy fish.

<p class="callout"><strong>Tip:</strong> If you think you have a 13-pounder, keep it in a well-aerated livewell, get an initial weight on a digital scale, and call the hotline before you do anything else. Don't drive across the county to a weigh station first.</p>

## Release the big ones well

Most Texas trophies go back in, and the slot limits on Fork and Caddo mean the biggest fish are often the ones you can keep but shouldn't. Wet your hands, support the belly, keep the photo quick. Our [how to release a fish](/blog/how-to-release-a-fish) guide walks through it.
