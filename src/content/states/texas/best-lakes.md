Every "best lakes in Texas" list names Lake Fork. The useful question is what each lake is best *for*, and in which month, because a lake that is excellent for crappie in February can be a slow bass lake in August. The ratings below (excellent, good, fair) are TPWD's own species ratings from each lake page. Sizes are surface area at conservation pool, and the limits are the ones that differ from statewide rules, checked on TPWD's lake regulation pages in November 2026.

## 1. Lake Fork

**Hopkins, Rains and Wood counties, near Quitman. 27,264 acres, 70 ft max, impounded 1980.**

This is the lake that made Texas famous. It produced the 18.18-lb state record in 1992, and 25 of the 50 heaviest bass on TPWD's Texas Top 50 list. It is still producing: a Lake Fork fish was among the 2026 ShareLunker Legacy Class entries. TPWD rates largemouth and channel catfish excellent, crappie and bluegill good.

TPWD's biologists name hydrilla beds, boat houses and docks, and lake points as the historic big-bass spots, and the agency and the Lake Fork Sportsman's Association have sunk brush and structure you can find with the GPS coordinates on TPWD's Habitat Structure Viewer. From mid-February to April, work spinnerbaits, soft-plastic worms and lizards, jigs and lipless crankbaits along the banks. Summer bass bite at night on worms, spinnerbaits, cranks and topwaters; late summer and early fall bring schooling fish; winter is jigging spoons, jigs and crankbaits.

Crappie hold deep near the dam in winter and stack under the Highway 154 and 515 bridges and the CR 2946 and CR 514 crossings in late spring and early fall.

**Rules:** largemouth have a **16 to 24 inch slot**: you may keep bass 16 inches and under or 24 inches and over, but only one 24 inches or longer per day, 5 black bass total. Crappie have no minimum length from December 1 through the end of February, then 10 inches March through November. Standing timber is everywhere, so run slow off the main channels.

## 2. O.H. Ivie Reservoir

**Concho, Coleman and Runnels counties, 55 miles east of San Angelo. 19,149 acres, 119 ft max, impounded 1990.**

If Lake Fork is the record holder, Ivie is the current champion. It has led the ShareLunker Legacy Class count for six straight seasons and has produced 63 Legacy fish of 13 lb or more since 2020, including 15 in 2023. Seven fish on TPWD's top-50 list came from Ivie, led by Brodey Davis's 17.06-lb bass in February 2022. Ivie also holds the state record largemouth x smallmouth hybrid, an 11.07-lb fish from 2022.

Cover is thousands of acres of flooded mesquite, oak and juniper, plus saltcedar, with hydrilla spreading up the river arms. TPWD says trophy fish come on Carolina-rigged plastic worms, minnows and water dogs fished off humps and drop-offs 20 feet and deeper; flip jigs and soft plastics into the saltcedar for everything else. The main lake and Concho arm run clear.

**Rules:** no minimum length on largemouth, but **only two largemouth under 18 inches** may be kept per day, 5 black bass total. Zebra mussels are present: clean, drain and dry before you leave.

## 3. Sam Rayburn Reservoir

**Angelina River, dam about 15 miles north of Jasper. 114,500 acres, 80 ft max, impounded 1965.**

Rayburn is the all-rounder: TPWD rates largemouth, crappie and catfish all excellent. The lower lake is hydrilla with coontail and pondweed, and fish sit on vegetation edges, flats, humps and creek channels. In the upper third the grass thins out and timber, brush and laydowns take over. Tommy Shelton's 16.80-lb Rayburn bass from 1997 ranks tenth all-time in Texas, and the lake gave up the 283-lb state record alligator gar in 2023.

Fall through spring, fish shallow with crankbaits and spinnerbaits. In summer, throw topwaters early and late, then move to vegetation edges, brush piles, deep ledges and creek channels with worms, jigs and Carolina rigs. Crappie bite year-round on jigs and minnows: shallow near vegetation in spring, brush piles and channels the rest of the year.

**Rules:** statewide bass rules (14 inches, 5 fish). Blue and channel catfish are **50 a day, no more than 5 over 30 inches**. A fish consumption advisory is in effect; check before you keep fish.

## 4. Toledo Bend Reservoir

**Sabine River on the Louisiana line, dam about 24 miles northeast of Jasper. 181,600 acres, 110 ft max, impounded 1967.**

The biggest lake on this list. TPWD rates largemouth, crappie and sunfish excellent, which makes Toledo Bend the best place in Texas to fill a cooler with big bluegill and redear in late spring and summer. Hazel Bolton's 4.0-lb black crappie, the state record, came from here in 2002, and it was one of the 10 public lakes that produced Legacy Class ShareLunkers in 2026.

Hydrilla dominates, with native coontail, eelgrass, pondweed and lily pads. Lower-lake fish relate to grass edges, flats, humps and creek channels; upper-lake fish to timber and brush. Crappie peak during the spring spawn in shallow grass. White bass run upriver in early spring.

**Rules:** this is a reciprocal Texas-Louisiana water, with its own limits for both sides. Black bass: 14 inch minimum, **8 a day, possession 10**. Crappie: no minimum, 25 a day. Blue and channel catfish: 50 a day, no more than 5 over 30 inches. A valid license from either state covers the shared water.

## 5. Caddo Lake

**Big Cypress Bayou on the Louisiana line, northeast of Marshall. 26,800 acres, 20 ft max.**

Caddo began as Texas's only natural lake and was later dammed. It fishes like nowhere else in the state: shallow, flooded bald cypress, and heavy vegetation. TPWD rates largemouth, crappie, white bass and sunfish excellent, with chain pickerel a bonus in winter and early spring. Four of the top-50 Texas bass came from Caddo, including Keith Burns's 16.17-lb fish in 2010. TPWD calls March the most productive month for a trophy here. Caddo Lake State Park at Karnack sells boating maps; the lake is easy to get lost on.

**Rules:** largemouth have a **14 to 18 inch slot**: keep fish 14 inches and under or 18 and over. Black bass bag is 8, of which no more than 4 may be largemouth 18 inches or longer; possession 10. Catfish 50 a day, no more than 5 over 30 inches.

## 6. Lake Texoma

**Red River on the Oklahoma border, north of Sherman-Denison. 74,686 acres, 100 ft max, impounded 1944.**

Texoma is the striper lake. TPWD rates striped bass, smallmouth and blue catfish excellent. The 121.5-lb state record blue catfish came from here in 2004. In February, look for stripers on the river channel near the Willis and Roosevelt bridges with heavy jigs, slabs and live shad. Smallmouth live near the bluffs around Eisenhower State Park and Denison Dam. Blues come on jugs or rod and reel with live gizzard shad.

**Access and rules:** bank fishing is available at Eisenhower State Park and Hagerman National Wildlife Refuge. Your Texas license covers the Texas side; a **$12 Lake Texoma license** covers the whole lake. Stripers and hybrids: no minimum, **10 a day, only 2 over 20 inches, possession 20, no culling**. Blue and channel catfish 15 a day, only one blue 30 inches or longer. Crappie 10 inch minimum, 37 a day. Zebra mussels are present.

## 7. Lake Tawakoni

**Sabine River forks, about 15 miles southeast of Greenville. 37,879 acres, 70 ft max, impounded 1960.**

A schooling-fish lake east of Dallas. TPWD rates striped and hybrid striped bass, white bass and catfish excellent; largemouth and crappie are fair. In spring and summer, watch for gulls diving on surfacing schools at dawn, dusk or on overcast days and throw slabs, spoons or shad-bodied grubs. Trophy blue catfish are fairly common in winter. Tawakoni also produced a Legacy Class ShareLunker in 2025, the 79th public lake to do it.

**Rules:** blue and channel catfish 25 a day, **no more than 5 over 20 inches and no more than 1 over 30**. Lake Tawakoni State Park at Wills Point lends tackle.

## 8. Richland-Chambers Reservoir

**East-southeast of Corsicana off US 287. 41,356 acres, 75 ft max, impounded 1987.**

TPWD rates catfish, crappie, white bass and hybrid stripers excellent, largemouth fair. The old Trinity River levee forms a crescent of underwater structure between the creek arms and the dam. Hybrids and whites roam from the 287 bridge to the dam; find shad on sonar or watch the birds, and use heavy jigs or slabs when the fish are deep. Crappie hold around bridge crossings and timber in the upper Richland and Chambers arms. The lake added a Legacy ShareLunker in 2025, so don't write off the bass entirely.

**Rules:** catfish 25 a day, no more than 5 over 20 inches and 1 over 30. Zebra mussels are present.

## 9. Lake Conroe

**West Fork of the San Jacinto River, Montgomery and Walker counties. 20,118 acres, impounded 1973.**

The closest big bass lake to Houston, rated excellent for largemouth, hybrids, channel catfish and bluegill. Ricky Bearden's 15.93-lb bass from January 2009 sits on the top-50 list. The lower lake is bulkheads and docks; in early spring and mid-to-late fall, bass move shallow around marinas and docks and eat shad imitations and soft plastics. The upper end, inside Sam Houston National Forest, has standing timber just below the surface. Hybrids bite year-round on trolled lures, vertical spoons or live shad.

**Rules:** largemouth **16 inch minimum**. Catfish 25 a day, no more than 5 over 20 inches and 1 over 30. Any grass carp you catch must go back immediately.

## 10. Lake Livingston

**Trinity River, dam west of Livingston, about 50 miles north of Houston. 90,000 acres, 77 ft max, impounded 1969.**

Livingston is a white bass and catfish lake first. Whites are plentiful and big, best in early spring in the feeder creeks. Blues are the main catfish, caught most of the year over the river channel and in the creeks. Striped bass hang around the Highway 190 bridge. Largemouth are fewer, but bays and creeks from the Kickapoo and Penwaugh area north fish well in spring and fall. TPWD has sunk PVC fish habitat you can find with GPS and sonar.

**Rules:** catfish 50 a day, no more than 5 over 30 inches. Water is turbid; zebra mussels are present and a consumption advisory is in effect.

## 11. Falcon International Reservoir

**Rio Grande near Zapata, about 40 miles from Laredo on Highway 83. 83,654 acres, 110 ft max at the dam, impounded 1954.**

Falcon is a big-bass lake that depends on rain. TPWD rates largemouth and channel catfish excellent, but warns the lake can fluctuate 40 to 50 feet; at low water, rock piles and timber are the cover, and at full pool, flooded brush is everywhere. Fish spinnerbaits, crankbaits and Texas- and Carolina-rigged worms around flooded brush, points, humps, rock piles, roadbeds and old buildings. Tommy Law's 15.63-lb bass from 2011 made the top 50. Falcon State Park sells lake maps.

**Rules:** statewide bass limits. On the Texas side, alligator gar are **5 a day**, with no harvest reporting required here (unlike everywhere else). Everyone in the boat needs a **Mexico fishing license** in Mexican water; TPWD lists sellers in Zapata.

## 12. Amistad Reservoir

**Rio Grande, 12 miles northwest of Del Rio. 64,900 acres, 217 ft max, impounded 1969.**

The deepest lake on this list. Amistad is rock: ledges, steep drop-offs, points and rocky shorelines, with isolated flooded timber. TPWD rates largemouth excellent and white and striped bass good. Throw topwaters, buzzbaits and spinnerbaits early and late, crankbaits on rocky banks, and Texas- or Carolina-rigged worms and grubs at midday. White bass run from roughly late January through March. Tom Sutherland's 15.68-lb bass from December 2005 is on the top-50 list.

**Rules:** statewide limits on the Texas side, and a **Mexico license** for everyone aboard in Mexican water (sold in Del Rio). Zebra and quagga mussels are present.

## 13. Choke Canyon Reservoir

**Frio River, 4 miles west of Three Rivers. 25,670 acres, 95.5 ft max, impounded 1982.**

The South Texas sleeper. TPWD rates alligator gar, largemouth and all three catfish excellent. Cover is steep rock, flooded timber, brushy flats, hydrilla and, at high water, flooded brush. TPWD's own advice: use at least 20-lb line when you flip the heavy cover. White bass gather in the Frio River channel from December through February before they run. Big blues and flatheads come on live bluegill and shad, and trotlines and jugs take many of the largest.

**Rules:** blue and channel catfish **14 inch minimum, 15 a day**. Alligator gar 1 a day statewide, reported within 24 hours. Choke Canyon State Park's Calliham Unit lends tackle and sells maps.

## 14. Alan Henry Reservoir

**Double Mountain Fork of the Brazos, 45 miles south of Lubbock. 2,880 acres, 100 ft max, impounded 1993.**

Small, narrow and steep, but a genuine trophy lake for the Panhandle. It produced Legacy Class ShareLunkers in both 2025 and 2026, and in July 2026 a 6.5-lb Alabama bass that set a new state record. Spotted bass hold near the dam; tell them from largemouth by checking whether the jaw reaches past the eye (largemouth) or only to its center (spotted). Spring, when water reaches about 55 to 60°F, is best, on worms, spinnerbaits and crankbaits. Weekend pressure is heavy in spring and summer, and a consumption advisory applies.

## 15. Lady Bird Lake

**Colorado River, downtown Austin. 468 acres, 18 ft max.**

Proof you don't need a big lake. TPWD says good anglers often catch and release 20 to 30 bass a day here in the 2 to 3 lb range, and Willie Pipkin landed a 14.05-lb lake-record Legacy ShareLunker on February 5, 2025. It also holds the state record redear sunfish (2.99 lb) and common carp (44.25 lb). Cover is along the bank: laydowns, cypress roots, bulrush and cattails. Clear water makes it a strong night lake for bass on white spinnerbaits, topwaters and jigs. Barton Creek stays warm in winter.

**Rules:** no outboard motors, electric trolling motors only. TPWD lists special limits for some species on the lake's regulation page. Zebra mussels are present.

## How to pick from this list

- **You want a 10-pounder:** Lake Fork or O.H. Ivie, January through March. Read our [Texas bass fishing guide](/fishing/texas/bass-fishing) first.
- **You want fish in the boat for a family:** Toledo Bend or Sam Rayburn for crappie and sunfish, Tawakoni for schooling fish.
- **You fish from the bank:** Texoma at Eisenhower State Park, Lady Bird Lake, or any state park lake. Our [beginner fishing spots in Texas](/fishing/texas/beginner-fishing-spots) page has more.
- **You want to know what bites this month:** check the [Texas fish species and seasons](/fishing/texas/fish-species-seasons) calendar.

None of these lakes owes you a fish. [How to find fishing spots](/blog/how-to-find-fishing-spots) explains how to break down a big reservoir, and our [Texas fishing license](/fishing/texas/fishing-license) page covers what you need before you launch.
