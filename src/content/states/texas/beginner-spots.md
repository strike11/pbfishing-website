The hardest part of starting out is not the knot or the cast. It is knowing where you can walk up to the water, legally, with a decent chance of a bite. Texas has a better answer to that than most states: TPWD stocks fish in city park ponds, lets anyone fish state parks without a license, and lends rods and reels for free. Below are the easy spots near the four big metros, then the rigs and rules you need.

## The rules that apply to all of these spots

- **Kids under 17** never need a fishing license in Texas.
- **State parks:** no license or endorsement is needed to fish from shore or pier inside a Texas state park. You pay the park entry fee, and bag and length limits still apply. On docks and piers, it is pole and line only, two poles per person.
- **City and county parks, including Neighborhood Fishin' ponds:** adults 17 and older need a license with a freshwater endorsement. The cheapest way to try it is a one-day all-water license for $11; a full-year freshwater package is $30. Our [Texas fishing license](/fishing/texas/fishing-license) page has every option.
- **Neighborhood Fishin' limits:** 5 fish per day, all species combined, of which only 1 can be a black bass 14 inches or longer. Pole and line only, two poles max; no trotlines, jug lines, cast nets or traps.

## How Neighborhood Fishin' works

TPWD runs 18 Neighborhood Fishin' ponds in city and county parks with parking, bank access, restrooms and lighting. Channel catfish are stocked every two weeks from April through October, with a pause in the August heat. TPWD's hatcheries raise about 300,000 catfish of 10 to 12 inches a year for urban sites like these, so you are fishing for eating-size fish, not fingerlings. Rainbow trout go in from late November or early December through March, because trout only survive the Texas winter. Check TPWD's stocking locator for each pond's dates; the day after a stocking is the easiest fishing of the year.

## Houston

### Neighborhood Fishin' ponds

Four ponds, all stocked: **Burke Crenshaw Park** (Pasadena), **Community Park Lake** (Missouri City), **Herman Little Park** (Spring) and **Mary Jo Peckham Park** (Katy). Burke Crenshaw has history: the 1.3-lb state record green sunfish came from there in 2005.

### Sheldon Lake State Park

Fifteen minutes from downtown on Garrett Road. Fish the 1,200-acre lake from the bank near Garrett Road, no license needed. Families with kids get two catch-and-release fishing ponds of their own. Bring your own gear; we found no loaner listed here.

### Brazos Bend State Park

About 45 miles southwest of downtown, near Needville. Fish from shore or the fishing pier, license-free. Alligators are common in parts of the park; keep kids and fish stringers back from the water's edge, and read the park's alligator rules before you go.

### Galveston Island State Park

Fish the bay side, the ponds or the surf, all without a license from shore. There is a fish cleaning station and kayak launches. Saltwater bag limits apply even when the license doesn't.

## Dallas-Fort Worth

### Neighborhood Fishin' ponds

Five ponds, the most of any metro: **Chisholm Park** (Hurst), **Greenbriar Park** (Fort Worth), **Lakeside Park** (Duncanville), **City Lake Park** (Mesquite) and **South Lakes Park Pond** (Denton).

### Cedar Hill State Park

Twenty miles from downtown Dallas on Joe Pool Lake. Two lighted fishing jetties and a perch pond just for kids, with largemouth, crappie and catfish in the main lake. The marina is permanently closed, so buy bait before you arrive or ask at headquarters.

### Loaner rods at DFW libraries

You can borrow fishing tackle for free at the **Mountain Creek Public Library** in Dallas, **Hurst Public Library**, **Farmers Branch Manske Library**, **Little Elm Public Library**, **The Colony Public Library** and the City of Anna. Pair Hurst Library with Chisholm Park, the Neighborhood Fishin' pond in the same town. Adults need their license to borrow outside a state park.

### Ray Roberts and Eisenhower state parks

North of the metro, **Ray Roberts Lake State Park, Johnson Branch Unit** (Valley View) lends tackle. Further north on Lake Texoma, **Eisenhower State Park** lends tackle and is one of TPWD's two named bank-fishing spots on the lake.

## Austin

### Neighborhood Fishin' ponds

**Bullfrog Pond** and **Kingfisher Lake**, both stocked with catfish in the warm months and trout in winter.

### Lady Bird Lake

Right downtown, 468 acres of the Colorado River. TPWD says good anglers often catch 20 to 30 bass a day here in the 2 to 3 lb range, and the lake holds big redear and redbreast sunfish and carp over 30 lb. Most of the cover is along the bank: laydowns, cypress roots and cattails. It is a city lake, so adults need a license, and gas outboards are not allowed.

### McKinney Falls State Park

About 13 miles from the Capitol. Fish Onion Creek or Williamson Creek with no license, and ask at the visitor center about borrowing rods and tackle.

### Bastrop, Buescher and Inks Lake state parks

Within about an hour's drive, all three are on TPWD's tackle loaner list, and all are license-free inside the park.

## San Antonio

### Neighborhood Fishin' ponds

**Millers Pond** and **Southside Lions Park**, stocked on the same catfish and trout schedule.

### Calaveras and Braunig lakes

South of the city, off Loop 1604 and Highway 37. These are the place to step up from pond fishing. TPWD rates channel and blue catfish, hybrid striped bass and, unusually, red drum excellent in fresh water here. Catfish peak March through May on stinkbait, chicken liver or shad. Both lakes have special red drum limits and are run by CPS Energy; check current access rules before you drive out. License required.

### Guadalupe River State Park and the San Antonio Public Library

Guadalupe River State Park in Spring Branch lends tackle and was one of the parks hosting Free Fishing Day events in 2026. In town, the **San Antonio Public Library** is a tackle loaner partner.

## On the coast

**Goose Island State Park** in Rockport has a 1,620-foot fishing pier, a boat ramp, a fish cleaning station and free loaner rods, reels and tackle boxes, all license-free inside the park. **Sea Rim State Park** near Sabine Pass is also a loaner site. For saltwater limits, see our [Texas species and seasons](/fishing/texas/fish-species-seasons) page.

## Free Fishing Day and other free starts

On the first Saturday in June, anyone can fish public water in Texas with no license. TPWD lists the next one as **June 5, 2027**. On Free Fishing Day 2026 the Texas Freshwater Fisheries Center in Athens opened free, with loaner gear and tagged catfish to catch for prizes. If you can't wait for June, more than 70 state parks are license-free every day of the year.

## Three simple rigs that work at these spots

<p class="callout"><strong>Tip:</strong> Bring a small cooler, wet wipes, needle-nose pliers and a towel. The pliers matter more than the rod: a catfish or a sunfish spine will end a kid's day fast if you unhook with fingers.</p>

**Catfish on the bottom (stocked ponds, April to October).** A 1/2-oz sliding egg sinker on the main line, a barrel swivel, an 18-inch leader and a size 4 to 1/0 hook. Bait it with chicken liver, stinkbait, shrimp or a chunk of nightcrawler, cast to the middle, and wait with a slightly slack line.

**Bobber and worm (sunfish, any spot, spring and summer).** A small bobber 2 to 3 feet above a size 8 hook with one split shot. Half a nightcrawler or a cricket. Fish next to docks, cattails and laydowns. This is the rig most first fish come on.

**Winter trout (stocked ponds, December to March).** A size 8 hook with a small ball of floating trout bait or a kernel of corn on a short leader, or a 1/16-oz inline spinner retrieved slowly. Trout bite best in the first days after a stocking.

## Making it a good day for a kid

- Go for numbers, not size. Sunfish under a bobber beat a two-hour catfish wait.
- Keep the first trip to an hour or two. Leave while they still want to stay.
- Snap a photo of every fish before it goes back, and show them how to hold it wet and supported. Our guide on [how to release a fish](/blog/how-to-release-a-fish) covers it.
- Teach the 5-fish limit early. It's a good first lesson in why rules exist.
