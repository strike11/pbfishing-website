"When should I fish in Texas?" has a better answer than "spring." Each species in Texas runs on its own water temperature trigger, and a South Texas reservoir usually warms well before a lake on the Red River. Use the calendar below as a starting point, then watch the thermometer. A cheap pocket thermometer or your sonar's temp reading is worth more than any date.

## Texas fishing calendar by month

| Month | Best bets | Where to look |
|---|---|---|
| January | Trophy largemouth, blue catfish, stocked rainbow trout, deep crappie | Pre-spawn bass on deep points (Ivie, Fork); trophy blues on Tawakoni; trout at city ponds and state park lakes |
| February | Big bass, white bass start running, trout | Bass moving to spawning flats; white bass in creek arms and river mouths after rain |
| March | Peak trophy bass month, white bass run, crappie start | Shallow spawning areas; tributary riffles and shoals; crappie in protected coves as water nears 60°F |
| April | Bass and crappie spawning, channel catfish, hybrids | Shallow cover, flooded brush; hybrid stripers on San Antonio's Calaveras and Braunig lakes |
| May | Crappie wrap up, catfish heat up, sunfish beds | White bass drop back to the main lake; bluegill and redear on beds |
| June | Channel catfish spawn, schooling fish, bluegill | Bass move deeper; channel cats in creek channels; spotted seatrout in shallow bays |
| July | Night bass, crappie under lights, flatheads | Crappie above the thermocline at 15–25 ft; flatheads on live bait |
| August | Early and late only; red drum spawn starts | Lighted docks at night; adult reds gather near Gulf passes |
| September | Schooling bass and whites, red drum | Watch for birds over breaking shad; passes and beaches for reds |
| October | Fall flounder run begins, schooling bass | Channels and passes to the Gulf; creek arms for bass |
| November | Bass chase shad in creeks; flounder closed from Nov 1 | Back ends of creeks; trout stocking starts late in the month at some city ponds |
| December | Crappie and bass go deep, trout stocking, white bass stage | Crappie 20–30 ft over structure; whites gather in the upper reservoir |

Coastal anglers: flounder are closed **November 1 through December 14**. Freshwater bass anglers: the ShareLunker Legacy window, when 13-lb bass can be loaned to TPWD, runs **January 1 through March 31**. That one is a program window, not a fishing season.

| Species | Spawn window in Texas | Water temp trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Largemouth bass | February to May, by region | About 60°F |
| White bass | Runs February to March, done by May | 55–60°F with river flow |
| Crappie | Mid-March to mid-May | Starts 60°F, peaks 68–72°F |
| Channel catfish | Late spring to early summer | About 75°F |
| Flathead catfish | Late May through August | 75–80°F |
| Red drum | August into November, nearshore Gulf | Late summer and fall |

## Largemouth bass

Bass spawn when water reaches about 60°F, which TPWD says can happen as early as February or as late as May. March is the agency's pick for a trophy because the heaviest females sit near spawning areas loaded with eggs. By June the fish move deeper, and summer becomes a dawn, dusk and night bite. Fall brings schooling fish on shad. The full month-by-month bass pattern, lures and lake limits are on our [Texas bass fishing guide](/fishing/texas/bass-fishing).

## Crappie

TPWD's crappie biologists put the spawn from mid-March to mid-May, starting when water hits 60°F and peaking between 68 and 72°F, in 1 to 6 feet of water in protected coves, around flooded brush and near vegetation. In summer crappie suspend just above the thermocline; hang a light at night near a creek channel or flooded timber in 15 to 25 feet. In fall they move shallower but rarely above 10 feet. In winter they school 20 to 30 feet deep over ridges and river channels, and lighted, baited fishing piers produce. Use a medium minnow or a 1/32 to 1/8 oz jig. Crappie populations cycle, with two or three good years followed by two or three lean ones. Read more on our [crappie species guide](/species/crappie).

Statewide: 10 inches, 25 a day. Some lakes, like Lake Fork, drop the minimum in winter (December 1 through February) because deep fish rarely survive release.

## White bass

Called sand bass by many Texans. Schools move to the upper ends of reservoirs in December and January, then run up major tributaries in February and March when rain brings flow and water reaches 55 to 60°F. They spawn over gravel or rock in 2 to 8 feet of moving water, and males go up about a month before females. The run is usually over by May. The rest of the year, look for birds diving on shad in open water. TPWD lists white, silver, pearl and chartreuse as the productive colors on jigs, spinners and small cranks. Limit: 10 inches, 25 a day (no minimum on Texoma and Louisiana border waters).

## Striped and hybrid striped bass

Stripers and hybrids school on shad most of the year. On Texoma, TPWD points to the river channel near the Willis and Roosevelt bridges in February. On Tawakoni, watch for gulls over surfacing fish in spring and summer at dawn, dusk and on overcast days. On San Antonio's Calaveras Lake, hybrids are good January through August and peak March through May. Statewide limit is 18 inches and 5 a day; Texoma has its own 10-fish rule.

## Catfish: channel, blue and flathead

**Channel catfish** spawn in late spring or early summer once water reaches about 75°F. They bite all year; spring and early summer are the most popular months. TPWD also stocks them every two weeks from spring through October at Neighborhood Fishin' lakes, with a break in August. See our [channel catfish guide](/species/channel-catfish).

**Blue catfish** are the largest freshwater sport fish in Texas. They move upstream in summer for cooler water and downstream in winter, and winter is trophy time on lakes like Tawakoni.

**Flathead catfish** spawn late May through August at 75 to 80°F. Once they reach about 10 inches they eat only live fish, so fish live sunfish or shad in deep, slow pools. Limit: 18 inches, 5 a day.

## Sunfish (bluegill and redear)

Bluegill and redear spawn on beds in late spring and summer, and that is when Toledo Bend's big ones are easiest to find. On Lake Fork, TPWD suggests crickets, earthworms and small spinners shallow in early summer, then around piers, boathouses and humps the rest of the year. No statewide size or bag limit. Good first fish for kids; see our [bluegill guide](/species/bluegill).

## Rainbow trout (winter only)

Rainbow trout don't survive Texas summers in most places, so TPWD stocks them as a winter fishery from December through March at more than 100 state park lakes and similar sites, and at Neighborhood Fishin' lakes from late November or early December through March. Limit: 5 a day, no minimum. The state record rainbow, 12.38 lb, came from the Guadalupe River in 2023. See our [rainbow trout guide](/species/rainbow-trout).

## Alligator gar

Texas's biggest freshwater fish. The bag is 1 per day of any size statewide, and every alligator gar harvested from public water must be reported to TPWD within 24 hours, except on Falcon, where the limit is 5 and no report is required. TPWD may temporarily close certain areas to alligator gar fishing when spawning conditions are ideal, so check before a spring trip.

## Red drum

Redfish spawn from August into November in nearshore Gulf water on the outside of the barrier islands, near passes and channels. They mature at more than four years and 30 inches, and can live past 40 years, which is why the slot is tight: 20 to 28 inches, 3 a day, with one oversized fish per license year on your Red Drum Tag. Inland, San Antonio's Braunig and Calaveras lakes hold red drum in fresh water, best March through August.

## Spotted seatrout

Speckled trout live in shallow bays in spring and summer, move to deeper bay water and the Gulf in fall, and return to the shallow bays as water warms. Most large trout are females that commonly live to 9 or 10 years. The 2026–27 limit is a **15 to 20 inch slot, 3 a day**, plus one over 28 inches per year with a Spotted Seatrout Tag.

## Southern flounder

Flounder leave the bays in fall to spawn in the Gulf at 50 to 100 feet, and the best fall catches come from the channels and passes that lead out. The spring return is gradual. The fishery is **closed November 1 to December 14**, which covers the heart of that fall run. Outside that window: 15 inches, 5 a day.
