TPWD lists weights in decimal pounds, so the 18.18-lb largemouth everyone quotes is about 18 lb 3 oz. In the tables below the first number is TPWD's figure and the lb-oz in brackets is our conversion, rounded to the nearest ounce. All records here are the **rod and reel** category, which is what most people mean by "state record." TPWD also keeps fly-rod-only lists, junior angler records (under 17), catch-and-release records by length, private-water records and water body records for every public lake.

## The stories behind four records

### The 18.18-lb Lake Fork largemouth (1992)

This record is the end of a 20-year experiment. TPWD brought Florida largemouth bass to Texas in 1971, and as Florida genes spread through new reservoirs, the bass got bigger. A 13.5-lb fish had been the state record since 1943. In 1980 an angler broke it with a 14.1-lb bass, the record fell several more times through the 1980s, and on January 24, 1992 Barry StClair brought in an 18.18-lb, 25.5-inch fish from Lake Fork. Lake Fork had only been impounded in 1980.

The lake still owns the trophy list. On TPWD's Texas Top 50 Largemouth Bass list, Lake Fork holds 25 of the 50 places, including the top six. O.H. Ivie is second with seven, and its 17.06-lb fish from February 24, 2022 (Brodey Davis) is the heaviest bass on the list since StClair's record. The 50th fish on the list weighs 15.60 lb, which tells you how deep the Texas trophy pool is.

### Cody Mullennix's 121.5-lb blue catfish (2004)

Blue catfish are the largest freshwater sport fish in Texas, and TPWD says 50-lb fish are "not unusual" where mature populations exist. Mullennix's fish came from Lake Texoma on January 16, 2004 and measured 58 inches. Winter is not an accident: TPWD notes blues move downstream to warmer water in winter, and its Lake Tawakoni page says trophy blues are fairly common there in the cold months. Texoma today limits anglers to one blue catfish 30 inches or longer per day.

### Arthur Weston's 283-lb alligator gar (2023)

The newest giant on the freshwater list came out of Sam Rayburn on September 2, 2023: a 283-lb, 72-inch alligator gar on rod and reel. If you plan to try for one, know the rules. The statewide bag is one alligator gar of any size, and any alligator gar taken from public water (except Falcon) must be reported to TPWD within 24 hours.

### Filip Spencer's double on the Nueces River (2009)

The freshwater list has a quirk worth knowing. On November 17, 2009, Filip Spencer set two freshwater records on the same day on the Nueces River: a 36.625-lb black drum and an 8.31-lb spotted seatrout. Saltwater species caught in fresh or brackish water count on the freshwater list, so a tidal river can hand you a record you would never get in the bay.

## How rare is a Texas trophy?

Weight records are once-in-a-generation fish. A better yardstick for an ordinary good angler is ShareLunker. In 2025, according to TPWD, 469 anglers entered 1,071 largemouth of 8 lb or 24 inches and up from 93 public waters:

| ShareLunker class | Threshold | 2025 entries |
|---|---|---|
| Lunker Class | 8 lb+ (or 24 in) | 714 |
| Elite Class | 10 lb+ | 335 |
| Legacy Class (Jan 1 to Mar 31) | 13 lb+ | 14 |
| Legend Class (year-round entry) | 13 lb+ | 8 |

So across all of Texas's public water in a year, 22 bass over 13 lb were reported. If you have a 10-pounder on your wall, you are in a club of a few hundred a year. ## How to submit a Texas record

TPWD's Angler Recognition Program runs the record books. The rules for a weight record:

1. **Catch it legally** in Texas waters, by yourself (someone else may net or gaff it), with no tracking tags or electronics in the fish.
2. **Weigh it on a certified or legal-for-trade scale** within 3 days of the catch. If you used an uncertified scale, it must be certified within 30 days of the weighing. TPWD publishes a list of official weigh stations; find one before the trip.
3. **Have one or more disinterested witnesses** watch the weighing.
4. **Photograph it**: clear color side views from several angles with a ruler or measuring device beside the fish, plus one photo of you holding it. TPWD does not accept video.
5. **Send the application** (form PWD BR T3200-349B) so TPWD receives it within 60 days. State record applications must be notarized. Email photos to anglers@tpwd.texas.gov.
6. **Expect a species check.** TPWD will not certify a record until the species is confirmed and may ask to see the fish.

The minimum weight is 8 ounces, or 2 ounces for Junior Rod and Reel, and anglers under 17 go into the junior division automatically. Allow 6 to 8 weeks for a certificate. Questions go to the program in Athens at (903) 670-2275.

<p class="callout"><strong>Tip:</strong> If you plan to release the fish, the catch-and-release record category is decided by length, needs a witness to the live release, and makes the scale optional. TPWD wants the fish on its side, jaw closed, tail squeezed for maximum length. Our guide on <a href="/blog/how-to-measure-a-fish">how to measure a fish</a> shows the technique.</p>

For a big bass caught between January 1 and March 31 that might top 13 lb, call the ShareLunker hotline, (903) 681-0550, before you do anything else. TPWD can certify the weight on site, and the fish may be loaned for spawning. See our [Texas bass fishing guide](/fishing/texas/bass-fishing) for how that works.

## Your own record still counts

Most of us will never see a certified scale. A personal best is a record too, and it is worth logging properly: species, length, weight, photo, date and water.
