Anglers on r/texas ask for a straight answer about fishing licenses every spring, and the TPWD site does have every answer; it is just spread over a dozen pages. This page pulls them together. Every price and rule below was checked on tpwd.texas.gov in November 2026 for the license year that runs September 1, 2026 to August 31, 2027. Prices change, so confirm on the [TPWD Fishing Licenses and Packages page](https://tpwd.texas.gov/regulations/outdoor-annual/licenses/fishing-licenses-stamps-tags-packages/fishing-licenses-and-packages/) before you pay.

## Who needs a Texas fishing license

TPWD's rule is short: any resident who fishes in the public waters of Texas needs a resident license, and any non-resident needs a non-resident license. In Texas the license alone is not enough. To take fish, mussels, clams, crayfish or other aquatic life you also need a **freshwater endorsement**, a **saltwater endorsement**, or both. The packages below already bundle the right endorsement, which is why almost everyone buys a package instead of the parts.

"Fishing" means fishing, not keeping. If you are 17 or older on public water, catch and release still needs a license.

## Who does not need one

- **Anyone under 17**, resident or non-resident.
- **Texas residents born before January 1, 1931.**
- **Certain residents with an intellectual disability** fishing as part of medically approved therapy or under family supervision, with the documentation TPWD describes.
- **Louisiana residents 65 or older** who hold a valid Louisiana recreational fishing license, and **Oklahoma residents 65 or older**.
- **Anyone fishing inside a Texas state park.** No license or endorsement is needed on state park property or in water completely enclosed by a state park. TPWD says this covers more than 70 parks, all year. You still pay the park entry fee, and bag and length limits still apply.
- **Anyone fishing water completely enclosed within private property.** If you take fish off that water, TPWD suggests carrying something that shows where they came from.
- **Everyone on Free Fishing Day**, the first Saturday in June. TPWD lists the next one as June 5, 2027. One catch: on the 2026 Free Fishing Day TPWD reminded anglers that private recreational red snapper fishing in federal waters still required a license.

<p class="callout warn"><strong>Check first:</strong> The state park exemption covers only parks run by TPWD. City parks, county parks, river authority land and Corps of Engineers parks are not state parks, so adults need a license there.</p>

## Texas fishing license prices (2026–27)

Annual packages are valid from the day you buy them through August 31. New-year licenses go on sale August 15.

| License or package | Resident | Non-resident |
|---|---|---|
| Freshwater package | $30 | $58 |
| Saltwater package (includes red drum and/or spotted seatrout tag) | $35 | $63 |
| All-water package (fresh and salt) | $40 | $68 |
| Year-from-purchase all-water package | $47 | not offered |
| One-day all-water license (tags included, limit one per customer) | $11 | $16 |
| Senior (65+) freshwater / saltwater / all-water | $12 / $17 / $22 | not offered |
| Special resident all-water (legally blind) | $7 | not offered |

The **year-from-purchase** package is the one to buy in spring. It runs through the end of your purchase month in the next license year, so a license bought March 13 expires March 31 of the following year instead of August 31.

### Combo and lifetime options (residents only)

| Package | Price |
|---|---|
| Super Combo (hunting + all fishing endorsements, red drum and seatrout tag) | $68 |
| Senior Super Combo (65+) | $32 |
| Combo hunting + freshwater / saltwater / all-water | $50 / $55 / $60 |
| Senior combo hunting + freshwater / saltwater / all-water | $16 / $21 / $26 |
| Lifetime resident fishing package | $1,000 |
| Lifetime resident combination hunting and fishing | $1,800 |

Lifetime licenses are sold only by application, processed at a TPWD law enforcement office or the Austin headquarters. Call (800) 792-1112 for the form. Lifetime holders get their fishing tags free. Residents 17 and older can also buy a $5 entry into the drawing for a lifetime super combo.

### Military and disabled veterans

- **Texas resident active-duty military** can get the All-Water Fishing Package free, with proof of Texas residency.
- **Disabled veterans**, resident or non-resident, get the Super Combo hunting and all-water fishing package free if the VA rates a service-connected disability at 50% or more, or for loss of use of a foot or leg, and they receive compensation for it. Bring VA proof of the rating issued in the last 12 months, or a Texas driver license with the "Disabled Veteran" designation, each year. This one is sold only in person at a license retailer.

## Freshwater, saltwater or all-water?

This is where people waste money. A freshwater package will not cover a bay trip, and an r/Fishing thread made the point with a game warden's line: "Ma'am are you aware you have a coastal fishing license." Pick by where you will actually fish:

- **Lakes and rivers only:** freshwater package, $30.
- **Coast only:** saltwater package, $35, which includes the tags you need to keep an oversized red drum or spotted seatrout.
- **Both, or not sure:** all-water, $40. It costs $10 more than freshwater and ends the guessing.

Saltwater bag limits follow saltwater fish into brackish and fresh water, which is one more reason Gulf Coast anglers lean toward all-water.

## Red drum and seatrout tags

Saltwater packages include a Red Drum Tag. In a license year you may keep one red drum over the 28-inch maximum with that tag, plus one more with a Bonus Red Drum Tag. Spotted seatrout over 28 inches work the same way with a Spotted Seatrout Tag. Tagged fish do not count against your daily bag. If you hold a digital license, you report the tag in the Texas Hunt & Fish app or online instead of attaching a paper tag.

Kids under 17 do not need a license, but to keep an oversized red drum or seatrout they need an Exempt Angler Red Drum Tag ($3) or Exempt Angler Spotted Seatrout Tag ($3).

## How to buy, and how to carry it

1. **Online:** TPWD's official license sales site (tpwd.texas.gov/business/licenses/online_sales/). Give a valid email and the receipt arrives right away.
2. **In person:** about 1,700 license retailers statewide. TPWD keeps a retailer finder on the same site.
3. **Phone help:** TPWD's general lines are (800) 792-1112 and (512) 389-4800.

You can carry the license on paper, as a digital license in the Texas Hunt & Fish app, or, for fishing that does not involve tags, as the emailed purchase receipt. App Store reviewers say the app gets slow with weak cell signal, so take a screenshot or keep the receipt email downloaded before you head to a remote ramp. A replacement license usually costs $10.

## Border waters and Mexico

- **Lake Texoma:** your Texas license covers the Texas side. The $12 Lake Texoma license covers the whole lake, Texas and Oklahoma waters.
- **Toledo Bend:** TPWD's Toledo Bend page says anglers with a valid license from either Texas or Louisiana may fish the shared Texas-Louisiana waters. Bass, crappie, catfish and white bass limits are the same on both sides; some species, like alligator gar, differ.
- **Falcon and Amistad:** if you cross into Mexican waters, every person in the boat needs a Mexico fishing license. TPWD lists tackle shops in Zapata and Del Rio that sell them.

## The rules you will be asked about at the ramp

A license is not the whole story. Statewide in fresh water, largemouth and smallmouth bass have a 14-inch minimum and a 5-fish combined black bass limit. Crappie are 10 inches and 25 a day. Channel and blue catfish have no minimum, 25 a day, no more than 10 of them 20 inches or longer. On the coast, red drum must be 20 to 28 inches (3 a day) and spotted seatrout 15 to 20 inches (3 a day), and flounder are closed November 1 to December 14. Many lakes have exceptions, like Lake Fork's 16 to 24 inch bass slot. Our [Texas bass fishing guide](/fishing/texas/bass-fishing) lists the big ones and the [best lakes page](/fishing/texas/best-lakes) gives each lake's rules.

## Fishing free with a family

If you only want to try fishing once, you have three no-cost routes: Free Fishing Day in June, any of more than 70 state parks year-round, or a kid under 17 fishing anywhere while you watch from the bank. TPWD also lends rods and reels for free at dozens of state parks and libraries. Our [beginner fishing spots in Texas](/fishing/texas/beginner-fishing-spots) page lists them by city. For the gear itself, see [fishing for beginners](/blog/fishing-for-beginners).

## Quick answers to edge cases

- **Fishing from a public pier on the coast:** it is public water, so you need a saltwater or all-water license unless the pier is inside a state park.
- **Bank fishing at a city pond:** license needed for adults.
- **Guiding:** guides need a separate fishing guide license (resident freshwater $132, resident all-water $210).
- **Taking turtles or frogs:** that requires a hunting license, not a fishing license.

This page is a summary, not legal advice. When in doubt, the [Outdoor Annual](https://tpwd.texas.gov/regulations/outdoor-annual/licenses/fishing-licenses-stamps-tags-packages/fishing-licenses-and-packages/) wins.
