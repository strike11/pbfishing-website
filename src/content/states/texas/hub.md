Texas did not get its bass fishing by luck. At the start of the 20th century the state had one natural lake, Caddo, and a handful of man-made reservoirs. Most of the big water you fish today was built in the 1960s and 1970s, and TPWD then stocked it with a fish built for big lakes: the Florida largemouth. That is why a state the size of Texas sends anglers from every other state chasing a double-digit bass, and why this page starts with bass before it gets to catfish, crappie, stripers, trout and the Gulf.

## What makes Texas fishing different

Three things, and all of them are on the record.

**Florida genes.** TPWD brought Florida largemouth bass to Texas in 1971 and stocked them into a few private lakes the next year. A 13.5-lb bass had held the state record since 1943. In 1980 a 14.1-lb fish broke it, the record fell several more times, and in 1992 Lake Fork gave up the current 18.18-lb record. TPWD's hatcheries still stock 6 to 8 million Florida and northern largemouth in a typical year.

**A trophy program that runs on anglers.** Since 1986 the Toyota ShareLunker program has collected data on bass of 8 lb or 24 inches and up. Fish of 13 lb or more caught January 1 through March 31 can be loaned to TPWD for selective breeding, and their offspring go back into public lakes. The 2026 collection season produced 18 Legacy Class fish from 10 public reservoirs, and 81 public reservoirs have now produced at least one.

**Range.** Within one state you get East Texas timber-and-hydrilla lakes on the Louisiana line, clear rocky border reservoirs on the Rio Grande, Hill Country streams with the Guadalupe bass (the state fish, found only on the Edwards Plateau), and a Gulf coast with red drum, speckled trout and flounder.

## Best regions, in one paragraph each

**East Texas (Pineywoods).** Sam Rayburn (114,500 acres), Toledo Bend (181,600 acres), Lake Fork and Caddo. Hydrilla, standing timber and big crappie. This is where most of Texas's top-50 bass were caught: Lake Fork alone holds 25 of the 50 spots on TPWD's list.

**North Texas.** Lake Texoma for stripers and trophy blue cats, Lake Tawakoni for blues and schooling white bass, Richland-Chambers for hybrids and crappie. Close enough to Dallas-Fort Worth for an after-work trip.

**West and South Texas.** O.H. Ivie east of San Angelo has led the ShareLunker count six seasons running. Amistad and Falcon on the Rio Grande, and Choke Canyon south of San Antonio, are big-bass reservoirs that live and die by water levels; TPWD notes Falcon can swing 40 to 50 feet.

**Hill Country.** Lady Bird Lake runs through downtown Austin and produces 2 to 3 lb bass by the dozen for good anglers. The rivers hold Guadalupe bass, and the Guadalupe River produced both the state rainbow and brown trout records.

**The coast.** State parks like Galveston Island and Goose Island let you fish the bay or surf with no license. Bag limits for red drum, trout and flounder are strict and slot-based.

Every one of these waters gets its own section on our [best lakes in Texas](/fishing/texas/best-lakes) page.

## Seasons at a glance

- **January to March:** Big-bass time. TPWD calls March the best month for a trophy, when heavy females sit near spawning areas. ShareLunker Legacy season runs January 1 to March 31. White bass push up the rivers in February and March. Rainbow trout are stocked December through March.
- **April to June:** Bass spawn as water passes about 60°F, anywhere from February to May depending on where you are in the state. Crappie spawn from mid-March to mid-May. Channel catfish spawn when water hits about 75°F.
- **July to September:** Fish early, late or at night. Bass move deeper by June. Stripers, hybrids and white bass school on shad and give away their position with birds.
- **October to December:** Fall schooling bass, the flounder run toward the Gulf passes, and red drum spawning near the passes from August into November. Flounder season is closed November 1 to December 14.

## The license in two lines

As of November 2026, a Texas resident freshwater package costs $30, saltwater $35 and all-water $40; non-residents pay $58, $63 and $68, and anyone under 17 fishes free. You can fish without a license from shore or pier in a state park, on private water fully enclosed by private land, and statewide on Free Fishing Day (the first Saturday in June). Exemptions, seniors, military, lifetime and day licenses are all on our [Texas fishing license](/fishing/texas/fishing-license) page.

## Starting out or fishing with kids

Texas makes it easy. TPWD stocks channel catfish every two weeks from spring to fall, and rainbow trout in winter, at 18 Neighborhood Fishin' lakes in city parks, including five in Dallas-Fort Worth, four in Houston and two each in Austin and San Antonio. More than 70 state parks offer license-free fishing, and dozens lend rods and reels for free. Our [beginner fishing spots in Texas](/fishing/texas/beginner-fishing-spots) page names the ponds and piers city by city, and [fishing for beginners](/blog/fishing-for-beginners) covers the gear.
