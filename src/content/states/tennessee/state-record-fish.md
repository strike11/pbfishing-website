## The stories behind four records

### Dale Hollow, 1955: the smallmouth that put Tennessee on the map

D. L. Hayes caught his 11-lb 15-oz smallmouth on Dale Hollow on July 9, 1955. It has stood for more than 70 years and still sits at the top of both the Tennessee and the world list, according to TWRA. It is a big reason the smallmouth replaced the largemouth as Tennessee's official sport fish in 2005; TWRA notes the state has produced the five largest smallmouth in the world. Dale Hollow's 16 to 21 inch protected slot is designed to keep that kind of fish in the lake.

### Old Hickory, 1960: the 25-pound walleye

Mabry Harper's 25-lb walleye from Old Hickory on August 3, 1960 carries TWRA's world-record asterisk. It came from a Cumberland River lake next to Nashville, not from the Great Lakes.

### Chickamauga 2015 and Nickajack 2026: what Florida genes did

Tennessee's largemouth record stood from 1954 at 14.5 pounds until February 13, 2015, when Gabe Keen landed a 15-lb 3-oz fish on Chickamauga in the early morning. TWRA's testing showed a 12-year-old F1 hybrid of native and Florida largemouth, the product of a stocking program started in 2000. Eleven years later, on February 28, 2026, Darren Nunley caught a 15-lb 7.5-oz bass on Nickajack, the next reservoir downstream, where TWRA began its own Florida stocking in 2015. Outdoor Life reported it measured 27 7/8 inches and was weighed three times; genetic testing finished May 9 and TWRA announced the record in May 2026. Some early reports said 15.75 pounds; TWRA's list says 15 lb 7.5 oz, and that is the number that counts.

### Barkley, 2023: a 122-pound blue cat

Micka Burkhart's 122-lb 3-oz blue catfish from Barkley Reservoir on June 28, 2023 is the heaviest game fish on the rod-and-reel list. Only the 149-lb paddlefish from Cherokee (2023) outweighs it.

## How to submit a Tennessee state record

From TWRA's application rules:

1. **Weigh it on a certified or legal-for-trade scale**: supermarket meat counters, farm and feed stores, post offices and FedEx locations have them. A hand scale on the boat does not count.
2. **Two witnesses** sign Part B of the application; one must be the scale operator or owner.
3. **Get a TWRA fisheries biologist to identify the fish.** It must be alive, fresh or frozen. Photos are not accepted for ID, and TWRA may run genetic tests.
4. **Licensed anglers only:** applicants 13 and older must give their TWRA ID number.
5. **Mail the application within 30 days** of the catch to the TWRA Fisheries Division.
6. **Beat the record by at least 1 ounce.** Ties are accepted. Only listed species count, and only two hybrids: Cherokee bass (striper x white bass) and saugeye.

TWRA points out the examination will likely kill the fish, and you have every right to release it instead.

<p class="callout"><strong>Tip:</strong> if you think you have a record, keep the fish alive in a livewell, call TWRA Fisheries at (615) 781-6575, and find a certified scale before you do anything else.</p>

## Not a record, still a trophy: TARP

The Tennessee Angler Recognition Program certifies trophy fish by length, with a photo of the fish flat on a measuring board or a witness, a $5 fee and 90 days to apply. Some minimums:

| Species | TARP minimum |
|---|---|
| Largemouth bass | 22 in |
| Smallmouth bass | 20 in |
| Spotted bass | 18 in |
| Black or white crappie | 14 in |
| Bluegill | 10 in |
| Walleye | 28 in |
| Sauger | 20 in |
| Striped bass | 40 in |
| Blue or flathead catfish | 36 in |
| Channel catfish | 28 in |
| Rainbow trout | 24 in |
| Brown trout | 26 in |

Five trophies of any kind earns a Master Angler I patch; twenty different species earns an engraved plaque.

## How rare is a trophy?

Very. On Kentucky Lake, TWRA's ten-year average for harvested largemouth is just over 2.3 pounds, and on Dale Hollow the average smallmouth is about 3. A fish that clears the TARP line is a fish of a lifetime for most anglers, and a state record is the top fraction of that.
