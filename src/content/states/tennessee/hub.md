Most Tennessee fishing happens on water that did not exist a century ago. The Tennessee Valley Authority and the Army Corps of Engineers dammed the Tennessee, Cumberland, Clinch, Holston and Caney Fork rivers into a chain of reservoirs, and the cold water released from the deep ones turned warm rivers into trout streams. The one big exception sits in the far northwest corner: Reelfoot Lake, formed by the 1811-1812 earthquakes and, at 10,427 acres, the largest natural lake in the state.

That history explains the fishing. You get deep, clear highland lakes for smallmouth, fertile river reservoirs with ledges for largemouth and catfish, shallow cypress water for crappie and bluegill, and tailwaters where a 20-inch rainbow is a normal fish. This page is the overview. Each section links to a deeper page.

## What makes Tennessee different

**Smallmouth history.** The smallmouth bass has been the official state sport fish since 2005. TWRA's reasoning was simple: Tennessee has produced the five largest smallmouth in the world, including D. L. Hayes' 11-lb 15-oz fish from Dale Hollow on July 9, 1955, which still stands as the state and world record.

**Big largemouth on the Tennessee River.** In 2000 TWRA started stocking Florida-strain largemouth into Chickamauga, more than 3 million fingerlings since. The 15-lb 3-oz record Gabe Keen caught there in February 2015 was a 12-year-old hybrid of native and Florida bass. Then on February 28, 2026, Darren Nunley caught a 15-lb 7.5-oz largemouth on Nickajack, the next lake downstream, and TWRA certified it as the new record. Both fish came in February. Remember that when your buddies say it is too cold to bass fish.

**Giant catfish.** The rod-and-reel blue catfish record is 122 lb 3 oz from Barkley Reservoir (Micka Burkhart, June 28, 2023). Kentucky Lake and Barkley are where most serious catfish anglers start.

**Trout without mountains.** Stocked tailwaters below Norris Dam (Clinch River), Center Hill Dam (Caney Fork) and South Holston Dam produce trout across Middle and East Tennessee. The state brown trout record, 28 lb 12 oz, came from the Clinch in 1988.

## Regions at a glance

TWRA splits the state into four regions, and the fishing changes with them.

- **West Tennessee (Region 1).** Kentucky Lake (108,217 acres in Tennessee), Pickwick, Barkley, Reelfoot and the Mississippi River. Crappie, bluegill, catfish, ledge bass. Reciprocal license agreements cover parts of Kentucky Lake, Pickwick and the Mississippi.
- **Middle Tennessee (Region 2).** Cumberland River lakes around Nashville: Old Hickory, Percy Priest, Cheatham, plus Tims Ford and Normandy. Good bank access, hybrids, crappie, winter trout below Percy Priest Dam.
- **Cumberland Plateau (Region 3).** Dale Hollow, Center Hill, Chickamauga, Nickajack, Watts Bar. Smallmouth country up north, Florida-strain largemouth down by Chattanooga.
- **East Tennessee (Region 4).** Norris, Cherokee, Douglas, Fort Loudoun, Tellico, South Holston, Watauga. Deep lakes with big drawdowns, striped bass, smallmouth, and trout tailwaters.

## Seasons in one paragraph

Winter is better than people think: sauger stack below the TVA dams from December to March, Norris smallmouth fishing is best from November to April, and both recent largemouth records came in February. Walleye run up the Caney Fork and the Obey from January into April, which is why special single-hook rules apply there. April is the month for crappie and largemouth in the shallows. Summer pushes bass to 15 to 25 feet and to night fishing, while hybrids blitz shad on Percy Priest. Fall bass follow shad back into the creeks.

## License in two lines

Under 13 fishes free. Everyone 16 to 64 needs at least the $33 Annual Hunt/Fish Combination, plus the $21 trout supplemental for trout; non-residents pay $49 a year without trout or $98 with trout (TWRA prices as of November 2026). Full price tables, permits and exemptions are on the [Tennessee fishing license](/fishing/tennessee/fishing-license) page.

<p class="callout warn"><strong>Check first:</strong> TWRA's commission approved fee increases in August 2026 (the combo would go from $33 to $42) that still need legislative approval. Confirm prices at the TWRA site before you buy.</p>

## Records and trophies

TWRA keeps records in Class A (rod and reel, sport methods) and Class B (bow, trotline, snagging and other methods). It also runs the Tennessee Angler Recognition Program for trophy-length fish that are not records: 22 inches for largemouth, 20 for smallmouth, 14 for crappie. The full table and the record stories are on [Tennessee state record fish](/fishing/tennessee/state-record-fish). If you think you caught a hawg but had no scale, the [fish weight calculator](/tools/fish-weight-calculator) gives you a sane estimate from length.

## New to fishing here?

You do not need a boat. TWRA runs 19 Family Fishing Lakes with piers and rental boats, stocks trout into city ponds every winter, and builds bank-fishing sites on Percy Priest. Kids under 13 fish free every day. The [beginner fishing spots in Tennessee](/fishing/tennessee/beginner-fishing-spots) page lists easy places near Nashville, Memphis, Knoxville and Chattanooga, and our [fishing for beginners](/blog/fishing-for-beginners) guide covers the first trip.

## Keeping track

A lot of Tennessee anglers fish three or four lakes a year and forget what worked where. Writing down date, water temperature, lure and depth beats memory every time; our [fishing log guide](/blog/fishing-log-what-to-record) covers what to record. If you want it on your phone, PB Fishing saves a photo, length, weight and the weather with each catch, and stores it even when you have no signal on the back end of Dale Hollow.

Safety notes: TVA and Corps lakes can rise fast and tailwaters rise faster when generators start, so check the release schedule before you wade. Some lakes carry fish consumption advisories; TWRA's Tellico page, for example, notes a TDEC advisory against eating catfish there.
