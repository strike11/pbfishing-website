Tennessee does not have one "best lake". It has a best lake for each fish, and the right answer for a smallmouth angler from Cookeville is the wrong one for a crappie family from Jackson. We picked fifteen waters from TWRA's own lake pages, survey data and regulations, and ranked them by what they do best. All sizes and limits below are from TWRA and the 2026 Tennessee Fishing Guide; check the [license page](/fishing/tennessee/fishing-license) and the lake's TWRA page before your trip because limits do change.

## 1. Dale Hollow Reservoir

Dale Hollow covers 27,700 acres and 620 miles of shoreline on the Obey River near Celina, Byrdstown and Cookeville, straddling the Kentucky line. It is the lake that holds the 11-lb 15-oz world-record smallmouth from 1955, and TWRA's roving creel surveys put the average smallmouth weight around 3 pounds, which is remarkable for any lake.

**Best for:** smallmouth, with good largemouth (average around 2.5 pounds in TWRA creel data). Smallmouth spawn at 59 to 60 °F on points and gravel flats. In summer, most anglers fish at night with jigs, spinnerbaits and soft plastics on points in 15 to 25 feet. In winter, live shad on circle hooks, or football jigs on tapering points in 20 to 40 feet.

**Rules that matter:** black bass 5 a day, only two may be smallmouth, with a 16 to 21 inch protected slot (one under 16, one over 21). Largemouth 15-inch minimum. Muskie 50-inch minimum. No more than four rods per angler. From January 1 to April 15 the East Fork of the Obey has single-hook walleye rules. The water is famously clear: lighter line and longer casts help.

## 2. Kentucky Lake (Kentucky Reservoir)

The biggest lake in the state, with 108,217 acres in Tennessee and 52,083 in Kentucky, nine Tennessee counties and about 113 tributary creeks. Towns include Paris, Camden and New Johnsonville.

**Best for:** crappie, ledge largemouth, blue catfish and sauger. Harvested largemouth have averaged more than 2.3 pounds over ten years. TWRA has sunk Christmas trees, stake beds and artificial structures; shallow attractors are marked with PVC pipe, deep ones with buoys. For crappie, vertical jigging with a #1 or #2 hook tipped with a minnow, spider rigging and trolling all work. Blue catfish are the most harvested cat; fish 15 to 60 feet with cut shad or skipjack during slow to medium current. Sauger peak December to March below the dams.

**Rules that matter:** crappie 20 a day, 10 inches. Largemouth and smallmouth 15 inches. One catfish over 34 inches per day. Dewatering areas close five days before and during the late waterfowl season. A Tennessee or Kentucky license works between the US 79 and US 68 bridges.

## 3. Chickamauga Reservoir

36,200 acres with 810 miles of shoreline from Dayton down to Chattanooga. The upper half is riverine; the lower half has big sloughs and embayments with grass, stumps and laydowns.

**Best for:** trophy largemouth. TWRA started stocking Florida largemouth here in 2000 and has put in more than 3 million fingerlings. Gabe Keen's 15-lb 3-oz record from February 13, 2015 was a 12-year-old native-Florida hybrid. TWRA also stocks striped bass and walleye. On warm winter days, look at shallow, stained embayments such as Chester Frost Park.

**Rules that matter:** largemouth 15 inches. Smallmouth, spotted and Alabama bass have no minimum, but only one over 16 inches per day. Crappie 15 a day, 10 inches. The Hiwassee Refuge is closed November 15 through the end of February.

## 4. Nickajack Reservoir

10,370 acres wrapping around Chattanooga, impounded in 1967. The upper two-thirds is riverine with riprap; the lower end has the coves, grass and stumps.

**Best for:** largemouth and spring white bass. TWRA describes historically high largemouth catch rates and launched its own Florida bass stocking here in 2015. Darren Nunley's 15-lb 7.5-oz record came from Nickajack on February 28, 2026. White bass run in spring below Chickamauga Dam and in North and South Chickamauga creeks, which is good bank fishing.

**Rules that matter:** same bass rules as Chickamauga (largemouth 15 inches, one smallmouth/spotted/Alabama over 16 inches).

## 5. Pickwick Reservoir

Pickwick is 43,100 acres in three states, but only about 6,159 acres sit in Tennessee (Hardin County), with depths to 85 feet. A three-state reciprocal agreement covers the impounded water up to the mouth of Bear Creek.

**Best for:** smallmouth that TWRA says "rivals Dale Hollow" for size, and ledge largemouth. Over ten years, harvested largemouth averaged more than 2.6 pounds and smallmouth more than 2.2. Smallmouth go shallow in March; Rat-L-Traps, spinnerbaits and shad swimbaits work, then deep points and ledges after the spawn. Night fishing is popular in summer. TWRA names Dry Creek, Winn Springs and Pompey Branch for largemouth.

**Rules that matter:** crappie 30 a day with a 9-inch minimum, the only reservoir in West Tennessee with that combination. Black bass 15 inches.

## 6. Reelfoot Lake

Tennessee's largest natural lake, 10,427 acres in Lake and Obion counties, formed when the 1811-1812 earthquakes sent the Mississippi backward into a basin. About 68% of it is 3 feet deep or less, full of stumps, cypress and lily pads.

**Best for:** crappie and bluegill, both among the best in the state. White crappie are the most harvested fish. In winter, locals troll the "Reelfoot Lake rig" (three-way swivel, two leaders, 3/8 to 1/2 oz sinker, minnows) in Lower Blue Basin. In spring, fish 4 to 6 feet near Swan Basin and Grooms Pocket. Bluegill peak on the full moons of May, June and July around cypress bases with crickets under a slip float.

**Rules that matter:** a Reelfoot Preservation Permit ($3 a day, $16 a year) unless you are under 16, a resident 65 or older, or hold a lifetime or sportsman license. Crappie 20 a day, 10 inches. Gas motors are banned in posted areas April 1 to May 31, and USFWS units close in winter. Wind gets dangerous on open basins; the ditches give shelter.

## 7. Old Hickory Reservoir

22,500 acres on the Cumberland River north and east of Nashville (Hendersonville, Gallatin), with 44 free boat ramps and eight public fishing piers.

**Best for:** largemouth, which take 40% of angler effort here. TWRA's 2019 spring survey caught 27 largemouth over 15 inches per hour. April is prime: spinnerbaits, soft plastics, lipless and square-bill crankbaits on gentle banks in Drakes Creek, Station Camp, Bledsoe and Spring Creek. In summer, fish grass near the main channel around the Highway 109 bridge.

**Rules that matter:** largemouth 14 inches, smallmouth 18 inches, crappie 30 a day at 10 inches.

## 8. J. Percy Priest Reservoir

14,200 acres on the east side of Nashville, with about 132 TWRA fish attractors you can load into your graph.

**Best for:** hybrid striped bass in summer, crappie in early April, and bank fishing. The Stewart Creek site in Smyrna has a full pier and five platforms on a paved greenway; the Vivrette Creek site off I-40 has a pier, a jetty and four platforms. Below the dam, winter trout stockings begin the first Friday in December.

**Rules that matter:** crappie 30 a day at 10 inches. Largemouth 15, smallmouth 18. Hybrids and stripers 2 a day at 15 inches.

## 9. Center Hill Reservoir

18,220 acres of steep, rocky lake on the Caney Fork near Smithville, McMinnville and Cookeville.

**Best for:** smallmouth and walleye. TWRA surveys show above-average smallmouth density and several good spawns. Walleye are stocked every year and make a major run in mid-March into the Blue Hole area of the Caney Fork near Rock Island State Park, spawning at 45 to 50 °F.

**Rules that matter:** smallmouth 18 inches. Single-hook rule and three-rod limit on the Caney Fork from Rock Island to Great Falls Dam, January 1 to April 30. Paddlefish are closed on the reservoir.

## 10. Watts Bar Reservoir

39,600 acres and 722 miles of shoreline near Kingston, Harriman and Spring City, with a 5 to 6 foot winter drawdown. TWRA stocks striped bass, black crappie, walleye and Florida largemouth.

**Best for:** smallmouth (TWRA says the population responded well to an earlier 18-inch minimum and fish over 18 inches are common) and crappie, though crappie spawns have been inconsistent lately.

**Rules that matter:** black bass 15 inches. Striped bass: 15-inch minimum April 1 to October 31; from November 1 to March 31 only one striper, 36-inch minimum. Paddlefish only May 1 to 15, one a day.

## 11. Norris Reservoir

TVA's first reservoir (1936), 34,200 acres on the Clinch and Powell rivers near Lake City and LaFollette. Water levels can swing more than 45 feet a year and the lake is infertile, so fish numbers are lower than on the river lakes.

**Best for:** smallmouth, best from November to April, and striped bass. Largemouth are second in abundance.

**Rules that matter:** smallmouth one a day with an 18-inch minimum from June 1 to October 15, five a day at 15 inches the rest of the year. Crappie only 10 a day. Winter stripers one a day, 36 inches.

## 12. Cherokee Reservoir

30,300 fertile acres near Morristown with shad and alewife forage. Stripers average about 10 pounds in TWRA netting but do not grow huge because of low summer oxygen; Cherokee bass (hybrids) handle warm water better.

**Best for:** stripers and hybrids from fall to spring, plus smallmouth and largemouth. Summer oxygen below 30 feet gets too low for most fish, so stay shallow or find cool-water refuges.

**Rules that matter:** smallmouth 1 a day at 18 inches June 1 to October 15. Spotted and Alabama bass 15 a day, no size limit.

## 13. Douglas Reservoir

30,600 acres near Dandridge, with a 60-foot drawdown. Strong largemouth and crappie year classes from big shallow embayments.

**Best for:** largemouth, crappie, and the late-winter sauger, walleye and white bass spawning runs to the headwaters. TWRA points newcomers to Flat, Muddy, McGuire and Nina creeks. In summer, fish the lower lake shallower than 10 feet, above the thermocline.

**Rules that matter:** June 1 to September 30, only one largemouth or smallmouth over 16 inches. White bass, stripers and hybrids 15 a day combined, no size limit.

## 14. Tellico Reservoir

16,056 acres at the foot of the Smokies (Loudon, Monroe, Blount counties), fed by cold water from Chilhowee and the Tellico River. It is relatively infertile but holds smallmouth, largemouth, rainbow trout and walleye.

**Rules that matter:** walleye and sauger 10 combined, no more than 5 walleye, one 24 inches or larger allowed. TWRA notes a state advisory against eating Tellico catfish because of PCBs and mercury; check TDEC's current advisory list before keeping fish here or on Fort Loudoun.

## 15. Clinch and Caney Fork tailwaters

The cold water released from Norris and Center Hill dams makes two of the best trout rivers in the Southeast. The Clinch below Norris produced the 28-lb 12-oz state record brown trout (1988) and is stocked March through August; all trout fall under a 14 to 20 inch protected slot with seven a day, only one over 20 inches. The Caney Fork below Center Hill is stocked March through December, with a five-trout limit, a 14 to 20 inch slot on rainbows, brookies and cutthroat, and one brown a day at 24 inches or more. You need the trout supplemental on top of a fishing license.

<p class="callout warn"><strong>Check first:</strong> tailwaters rise quickly when the dam starts generating. Check the TVA or Corps release schedule before you wade, and get out when the water starts coming up.</p>

## How to pick

If you have one day and a bass boat, go to Dale Hollow for smallmouth or Chickamauga for a giant largemouth. If you have kids and a cooler, go to Kentucky Lake or Reelfoot for crappie and bluegill. If you have no boat, Percy Priest's bank sites and the Old Hickory piers are hard to beat. Seasonal timing for each species is on [Tennessee fish species and seasons](/fishing/tennessee/fish-species-seasons), and if you are new, our guide on [how to find fishing spots](/blog/how-to-find-fishing-spots) explains reading contour maps and access points.

Keep a record of which lake, depth and lure produced, because these lakes behave differently year to year with water levels and grass. A photo log with length and weight (in a notebook or an app like PB Fishing) turns three seasons of trips into a pattern you can actually use. For bass-only advice, read [Tennessee bass fishing](/fishing/tennessee/bass-fishing).
