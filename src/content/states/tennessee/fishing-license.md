The Tennessee license system is not complicated once you know three things: there is no plain "fishing only" annual license for residents (the cheapest annual is a hunt/fish combo), trout always costs extra unless you hold a Sportsman license, and some lakes need a permit on top of everything. Every price below is from TWRA's License Structure and Fees page and the 2026 Tennessee Fishing Guide, as of November 2026 (checked on the agency site). Processing fees are added at checkout.

## Who needs a license

TWRA's rule is broad: anyone who takes or attempts to take fish (crayfish and salamanders included) by any method needs a license, and so does anyone **assisting** someone else. Catch and release is still fishing.

- **Under 13:** no license, resident or not. Some special permits apply to all ages, such as the Tellico-Citico trout permit.
- **13 to 15:** Junior Hunt/Fish/Trap, $9 for residents (buy it before the 16th birthday). Non-resident juniors pay $10.
- **16 to 64:** an adult license, plus trout and lake permits where needed.
- **65 and older (residents):** still need a license, but a cheap one (see below), and no lake permits.

So the answer to "what is the minimum age for a fishing license in Tennessee" is **13**.

|---|---|---|---|

| Annual Trout Supplemental | $21 | | Add-on to the Combo, County of Residence or 1-Day No Trout |
| 1-Day Fishing, No Trout | $6 | 13 to 64 | One day |
| 1-Day Fishing, All Species | $11 | 16 to 64 | One day, trout included |
| County of Residence Fishing, No Trout | $10 | 13+ | Natural bait only (worms, crickets, corn), no minnows or artificial lures, home county only |
| Junior Hunt/Fish/Trap | $9 | 13 to 15 | Everything for the year |
| Annual Sportsman | $165 | 16 to 64 | All hunting, fishing, trapping, trout, and no lake or special permits needed |
| Annual Senior Citizen Hunt/Fish/Trap | $4 | 65+ | |
| Permanent Senior Citizen Hunt/Fish/Trap | $49 | 65+ | One-time purchase |
| Senior Sportsman | $49 | 65+ | All-inclusive |
| South Holston Reservoir Supplemental | $20 | 13+ | Fishing the Virginia part of South Holston |

Lifetime Sportsman licenses are priced by age: $320 under 3, $659 for ages 3 to 6, $988 for 7 to 12, $1,976 for 13 to 50, $1,153 for 51 to 64 and $329 for 65 and over. You must have lived in Tennessee for the 12 months before buying, and the license stays valid if you move away.

**Disability and veteran licenses** cost $10 one time (wheelchair, certified blind, intellectually disabled, and residents with a VA-certified 30% war-service or 100% service-connected disability) or $5 a year for disabled residents under 18. From July 1, 2026, Tennessee National Guard members in good standing can apply for a free National Guard Sportsman License.

## Exemptions

- **Farmland owners:** resident landowners and their spouses, children and children's spouses may fish their own farmland without a license, even if they live elsewhere in Tennessee. Not if the land is jointly owned by unrelated people. Non-resident landowners do not qualify.
- **Tenants:** resident agricultural tenants who live on the land, plus spouse and dependent children, with the owner's permission.
- **Grandchildren:** resident grandchildren and great-grandchildren under 16 on a resident grandparent's farmland.
- **Military on leave:** carry a copy of your leave orders (a pass does not count).
- **Born before March 1, 1926:** carry proof of age and residency.
- **Free fishing days:** see below.

There is no general exemption for "private ponds". The farmland exemption is about who owns or works the land, not about whether the water is private. Pay lakes may still charge on free days.

## Free Fishing Day

TWRA has held a free fishing day every year since 1991. It is named for Bobby Wilson, who spent more than 40 years at TWRA and retired as Executive Director in 2022. In 2026 it was Saturday, June 6, with kids 15 and under fishing free through June 12. TWRA lists the 2027 date as **Saturday, June 12, 2027**, with free fishing for kids through June 18. It covers public waters, TWRA lakes and state park facilities. Local kids' events are listed on the TWRA events calendar; see [beginner fishing spots in Tennessee](/fishing/tennessee/beginner-fishing-spots) for places to go.

## Where to buy and how to carry it

- **Online:** Go Outdoors Tennessee (gooutdoorstennessee.com), any time.
- **App:** TWRA On The Go.
- **License agents:** about 1,000 statewide, including most Walmarts, bait and tackle shops, boat docks and sporting goods stores.
- **TWRA regional offices:** for lifetime and some special licenses that are not sold online.

You need a Social Security number to buy a Tennessee hunting or fishing license. Annual licenses run 365 days from purchase, not by calendar year, and paper licenses are not mailed. Every license, lifetime included, can be pulled up, emailed or printed free from Go Outdoors Tennessee, and a lost annual license can be reprinted at no charge. Phone signal is poor on a lot of Tennessee lakes, so keep a printed or saved copy before you leave the house.

## Border waters

Reciprocal agreements let you fish some shared water on either state's license, but you follow the rules of the state whose water you are on:

- **Kentucky Lake** between the Ned McWherter Bridge (US 79) and the Eggner's Ferry Bridge (US 68), excluding the Blood River embayment.
- **Dale Hollow**, the Wolf River arm including Illwill Creek.
- **Pickwick** with Alabama and Mississippi, from the dam up to the mouth of Bear Creek.
- **Mississippi River** with Arkansas and Missouri.
- **South Holston** needs the $20 supplemental for the Virginia side.

## Key rules after you buy

Statewide: 5 black bass, 15 crappie at 10 inches, 5 walleye at 16 inches, 10 sauger at 15 inches, 7 trout, one catfish over 34 inches a day. Nearly every reservoir has exceptions, so check the lake list on [best fishing lakes in Tennessee](/fishing/tennessee/best-lakes) and [Tennessee bass fishing](/fishing/tennessee/bass-fishing). TWRA does not publish a fine schedule on its license pages, so we do not quote one here; treat fishing without a license as a citation you do not want.

For Tennessee, it is this: 13 and up needs one, $33 gets an adult resident fishing, and trout costs $21 more.
