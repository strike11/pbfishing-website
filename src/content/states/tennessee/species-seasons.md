Tennessee runs from the Mississippi River bottoms to the Smokies, and spring arrives in the shallow west before it reaches the mountain lakes. Still, the state has a shared rhythm because nearly every big water is a reservoir: shad drive the predators, dams create current that concentrates fish below them, and summer stratification pushes fish up or out. Statewide there is no closed season on bass, crappie, catfish or trout. The closures that exist are site-specific, and they are listed at the end.

## Month-by-month calendar

| Month | What bites | Where to go |
|---|---|---|
| January | Sauger and walleye below dams; smallmouth deep; stocked trout | Pickwick Dam tailwater, Norris, city trout ponds, below Percy Priest Dam |
| February | Pre-spawn largemouth (both recent records); sauger; walleye runs build | Chickamauga, Nickajack, Kentucky Lake tailwaters |
| March | Walleye run at 45 to 50 °F; smallmouth shallow; trout tailwater stockings start | Center Hill Blue Hole, Pickwick, Caney Fork, Clinch |
| April | Crappie and largemouth spawn; white bass runs; paddlefish opens April 24 | Percy Priest, Old Hickory, below Percy Priest and Old Hickory dams |
| May | Bluegill and redear on beds; post-spawn bass; crappie | Reelfoot, Kentucky Lake, Pickwick |
| June | Bluegill full moon; hybrids start; night smallmouth; Free Fishing Day | Reelfoot, Percy Priest, Dale Hollow |
| July | Hybrids on shad; night bass; catfish in current | Percy Priest, Dale Hollow, Kentucky Lake channel |
| August | Thermocline; fish shallow on lower lake; tailwater trout | Douglas lower end, Clinch, Caney Fork |
| September | Catfish; early fall bass; crappie move toward shad | Kentucky Lake, Barkley |
| October | Bass follow shad into creeks; crappie on brush; Hiwassee goes catch-and-release Oct 1 | Chickamauga, Cherokee, Hiwassee River |
| November | Smallmouth season on Norris; winter trout stocking begins; stripers come up | Norris, Cherokee, city ponds |
| December | Trout below Percy Priest Dam (first Friday); sauger | Percy Priest tailwater, below TVA dams |

## Largemouth bass

Spawn at 68 to 72 °F, usually March to May on the Tennessee River lakes. Early April is TWRA's pick for Old Hickory and Percy Priest, when fish sit on gently sloping banks. Summer pushes them to grass and points in 15 to 25 feet, with better fishing at night once surface water nears the low 80s. Fall bass follow shad back into the same creeks they spawned in. Full detail on [Tennessee bass fishing](/fishing/tennessee/bass-fishing).

## Smallmouth bass

The state sport fish spawns earlier, at 59 to 60 °F, on points and gravel flats. Norris is best from November to April. Note the seasonal limits: on Norris and Cherokee you may keep one smallmouth at 18 inches from June 1 to October 15, and five at 15 inches the rest of the year. Dale Hollow uses a year-round 16 to 21 inch protected slot.

## Crappie

Black and white crappie are in nearly every reservoir. They move shallow to spawn in early April on the Nashville lakes and hang on deeper brush (10 feet and more) when the water is cold. In fall they follow shad. Limits vary: 15 a day at 10 inches statewide, 20 on Kentucky Lake, Barkley and Reelfoot, 30 on Old Hickory, Percy Priest and Cheatham, 30 at 9 inches on Pickwick, and only 10 on Norris. See [crappie](/species/crappie) for rigs.

## Bluegill and redear

April to June is prime on Pickwick. Reelfoot bluegill peak on the full moons of May, June and July, with crickets under a slip float 1 to 3 feet deep near cypress trees. Bluegill have no statewide limit; redear (shellcracker) are limited to 20 a day. More on [bluegill](/species/bluegill).

## Catfish

Channel, blue and flathead catfish bite all year. On Kentucky Lake, blues are the most harvested; fish 15 to 60 feet depending on temperature, during slow to medium current, with cut shad or skipjack. The rule is the same nearly everywhere: no limit on fish 34 inches and under, and only one over 34 inches a day. Read the [channel catfish](/species/channel-catfish) guide for rigs, and check the TDEC advisory list before keeping cats from Tellico or Fort Loudoun.

## Sauger and walleye

Sauger are more common than walleye in Kentucky Lake. TWRA calls December to March the prime window, mostly on the downstream side of TVA dams such as Pickwick, with heavy jigs and minnows. Limits: sauger 10 a day at 15 inches, walleye 5 at 16 inches. Walleye run in late winter: Center Hill's run peaks in mid-March at 45 to 50 °F, and special single-hook rules apply on the Caney Fork (January 1 to April 30), the East Fork Obey above Dale Hollow (to April 15) and the Emory River above Watts Bar (to April 30). Douglas sees sauger, walleye and white bass runs to its headwaters in early spring. See [walleye](/species/walleye).

## White bass, stripers and hybrids

White bass concentrate to spawn in April and May below Percy Priest and Old Hickory dams, and in spring below Chickamauga Dam. Hybrids (Cherokee bass) are raised in TWRA hatcheries and stocked; Percy Priest is the summer hybrid lake. TWRA stocks striped bass in Cherokee, Watts Bar and Chickamauga. Norris and Watts Bar switch to a 36-inch striper minimum from November 1 to March 31, and Boone keeps a 36-inch minimum all year.

## Trout

Statewide limit is seven trout, no size limit, but the tailwaters have slots. The Clinch is stocked March to August with a 14 to 20 inch protected slot. The Caney Fork is stocked March to December with five trout, a 14 to 20 inch slot on rainbows and one brown a day at 24 inches or more. The Hiwassee is catch-and-release with artificial lures only from October 1 to February 28. Winter stockings put about 10-inch rainbows into city ponds from late November. You need the trout supplemental license. See [rainbow trout](/species/rainbow-trout).

## Muskellunge and paddlefish

Muskie are limited to one a day at 36 inches statewide and 50 inches on Dale Hollow, Center Hill and the Caney Fork above Center Hill. The state record, 43 lb 14 oz, came from Melton Hill in March 2017. Paddlefish harvest is open April 24 to May 31, two a day, no culling. Watts Bar allows one a day from May 1 to 15, and Center Hill and its tributaries are closed to paddlefish.

## Water levels and summer oxygen: the hidden calendar

On Tennessee reservoirs, the lake itself changes with the seasons, and that moves fish as much as temperature does.

- **Winter drawdown.** Tributary lakes drop hard in fall and winter: Douglas can swing 60 feet between its 1,000-foot summer level and 940-foot winter level, and Norris more than 45 feet a year. Watts Bar draws down 5 to 6 feet. Spring refill floods new shoreline cover just as bass and crappie move shallow, which is part of why April is so good. Barkley typically hits full pool around May 1 and starts falling back after July 4.
- **Summer stratification.** On fertile lakes such as Cherokee and Douglas, the water layers in summer and the deep, cool layer runs out of oxygen. TWRA says oxygen below 30 feet on Cherokee often gets too low for most fish, and stripers and walleye hunt for cool refuges until fall. On Douglas, TWRA's advice for July and August is to fish the lower lake in less than 10 feet of water, above the thermocline.
- **Dam releases.** Current below TVA and Corps dams is what stacks up sauger in winter and catfish all year. Blue cats on Kentucky Lake bite best during slow to medium flows.

TVA posts lake levels and release schedules for its reservoirs; check them the night before a trip.
