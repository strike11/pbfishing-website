Ask on r/bassfishing how people keep track of their catches and you'll get a dozen answers: the Notes app, a spiral notebook, Google Sheets, a pinned map, "my memory." A lot of anglers try the big social apps first and give up because they wanted a record, not a feed of strangers' fish.

This list is for them. Every app here can work as a private log. We ranked them on what matters when a fish is flopping on the deck, not on how many features they list.

## What makes a fishing log app worth using

Before the list, here's what we looked for. These come straight from what anglers complain about in reviews and on Reddit.

1. **Speed.** You should be able to log a fish and release it. If the app wants ten fields before it saves, you'll stop using it by July.
2. **Automatic conditions.** Weather, air pressure and time should fill in on their own. Nobody remembers the wind direction three weeks later.
3. **Works without signal.** The best water often has one bar or none.
4. **Searchable later.** After two seasons you'll have hundreds of entries. You need to filter by species, water and month.
5. **Privacy by default.** Your log shouldn't post your honey hole anywhere unless you tell it to.
6. **Your data survives.** Apps shut down. Export, backup, or at least screenshots of your best fish.

## 1. Anglers' Log

**Best for:** anglers who want a journal and nothing else.

Anglers' Log does one thing. You log catches with photos, species, bait, gear, spot, water conditions and weather, pin spots on a map, and get stats on which spots, baits, times of day and conditions produce. No feed, no ads. App Store reviewers mostly praise it for staying focused and free of clutter.

Pro adds automatic weather, atmosphere and tide data, live GPS trails, custom fields for catches and baits, cloud backup and **CSV export** of your full history. Export is the feature that matters most long term: if the app ever goes away, your catches don't.

**Price:** free. Pro is $1.49 a month or $11.99 a year as of November 2026.

**Watch out for:** without Pro you type in weather yourself. No forecasts, no community.

## 2. Infinite Livewell

**Best for:** logging fast and keeping every spot to yourself.

Infinite Livewell is built for speed and privacy. You can log a catch with a swipe from an Apple Watch or iPhone, add length, weight and a photo if you want, and get back to fishing. Location is optional. If you turn it on, the app builds a private heat map you can filter by species, season and morning or afternoon. Buddies you approve get notified when you're on the bite but don't see where. Photos stay on your device unless you share them, and there's a background blur for trophy shots. It logs offline.

**Price:** free, with no in-app purchases listed on the US App Store as of November 2026.

**Watch out for:** it's brand new, with only a couple of App Store ratings so far. No automatic weather in the description, no forecasts.

## 3. MyCatch

**Best for:** quick catch-photo-release logging, with optional events.

MyCatch, from Angler's Atlas, is designed to get a fish recorded and back in the water in under a minute. One App Store reviewer put it simply: "quick and easy so you can safely release fish." You get stats like catch rate and species counts, plus the option to join fishing events with prizes. Your catch data can help university fisheries researchers, under data agreements the company says protect anglers' locations.

**Price:** free, with no in-app purchases listed on the US App Store as of November 2026.

**Watch out for:** no forecasts or detailed analytics. If you want a deep look at patterns, Anglers' Log or ANGLR go further.

## 4. PB Fishing

**Best for:** anglers who want every catch logged and their personal bests tracked without thinking about it.

This one's ours, so here's the plain version. You take a side-profile photo; the app suggests the species and estimates the length from the photo, and you confirm or correct it. Weight goes in if you weighed it. Date, water and a conditions snapshot (weather and barometric pressure) are saved with the catch. If you're out of signal, the catch is saved on your phone and uploads when you're back online. Your best fish per species updates on its own, so "what's my biggest smallmouth?" is one tap.

If you want a reason to keep logging, each species has a state and a national leaderboard that resets every six months. Entries show your name, state, length and weight, not the lake.

**Price:** unlimited catch logging is free, along with 5 photo IDs. Premium ($3.99 a month or $29.99 a year, billed by Apple) adds unlimited photo IDs, catch insights and trends, advanced stats, and 10-day weather and catch history.

**Watch out for:** iPhone only (iOS 17.6+). There's also a community feed with likes and comments. You don't have to use it, but if you want an app with zero social features, Infinite Livewell or Anglers' Log are a cleaner fit.

## 5. ANGLR

**Best for:** anglers who want the full trip on record: route, waypoints, gear changes and catches.

ANGLR goes deeper than anything else here. It records your GPS route, lets you drop 15+ kinds of waypoints, logs catches with weather, water, time and location filled in, tracks a virtual tackle box, and lets you drop a marker when you switch lures. It pairs with optional tracker accessories. The developer describes the core app as free and private by default. Premium adds map layers (USGS contours and gauges, streamflow, wind, tides) and waypoint export to fish finders.

**Price:** free core app. US App Store in-app purchases are listed at $2.99, $29.99 (ANGLR Member) and $39.99 (ANGLR Pro) as of November 2026.

**Watch out for:** App Store reviewers report crashes during trips, and once you have a lot of data it isn't easy to review. Its rating is 4.2 stars from about 640 ratings, lower than the simpler logs.

## 6. Fishbrain (free tier)

**Best for:** anglers who want a log and also want to see what others catch.

Fishbrain's free version logs catches with time and weather, and lets you search past catches by species or location. That's a solid log, and one Reddit fans single out. The difference from everything above is that Fishbrain is a social network first, so your log lives next to a feed and a public catch map.

**Price:** logging is free. Pro is $79.99 a year or $12.99 to $14.99 a month as of November 2026, and you don't need it for logging.

**Watch out for:** check location sharing in privacy settings before you post. If you want to know whether Pro is worth it, see [Is Fishbrain worth it?](/blog/is-fishbrain-worth-it)

## 7. A paper log (or the Notes app)

**Best for:** anglers who want no battery, no account and no subscription.

Don't laugh. A waterproof notebook in a zip bag has some real advantages: it never crashes, never needs signal, never gets bought by another company, and you can scribble a sketch of a weed line. Plenty of serious anglers still keep one and type the highlights into a spreadsheet at home.

**What to write each trip:** date, water, start and end time, air and water temp (°F), wind speed and direction, sky, pressure trend, and for each fish: species, length, weight, lure (with weight and color), depth and the time it bit.

**Watch out for:** no photos, no automatic weather, and searching 300 handwritten entries for "every walleye over 24 inches in October" is slow. Most people who start on paper move to an app once the notebook fills up.

<p class="callout"><strong>Tip:</strong> whatever you use, log water temperature. It explains more about why fish moved than any other single number. See <a href="/blog/water-temperature-and-fish-activity">water temperature and fish activity</a>.</p>

## How to pick

- **You want a record and nothing else:** Anglers' Log.
- **Privacy is the top priority:** Infinite Livewell.
- **You catch and release and want to be fast:** MyCatch or Infinite Livewell.
- **You're chasing bigger fish and want your PBs tracked:** PB Fishing on iPhone.
- **You track every waypoint and lure change:** ANGLR, if you can live with the occasional crash.
- **You hate apps:** paper.

## Getting real value from your log

The app matters less than the habit. A log only pays off when you read it back. Once a month, filter by species and look for repeats: same water temperature, same wind, same time of day. That's how anglers find patterns that no forecast app can give them.
