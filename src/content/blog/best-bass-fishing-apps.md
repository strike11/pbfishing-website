Ask r/bassfishing which app to use and the thread splits four ways. Some guys want a forecast because they haven't memorized the patterns yet. Some want contours to find the ledge. Some only care about logging the fish and proving the PB. And some want a tournament with a cash payout. One new bass angler there summed up the logging side: "bass people go by weight."

No single app does all four well, so this list is sorted by job. We make PB Fishing and rank it #1, because for most bass anglers the thing they come back to is their own record: the 4-pounder from the dock, the first 6, the PB they can finally prove. Every other pick gets an honest "best for."

## 1. PB Fishing

**Best for:** logging every bass, tracking your PB without thinking about it, and seeing where your best fish ranks in your state.

Bass anglers chase numbers: the 5-pound mark, the 8-pound mark, the 10. PB Fishing is built around that. You photograph the bass side-on, the app suggests the species and estimates length from the photo, you confirm, and it saves date, water, weather and barometric pressure with the catch. Your PB per species updates automatically, so your best largemouth and best smallmouth each have their own line. Unlimited logging is free forever.

The leaderboards are where it fits bass culture. Each species has a national board and a state board, seasons reset every six months (like "Season H1 2026"), and an entry shows your name, state, length and weight. It never shows the lake. So a Texas angler can see how a Lake Fork 9-pounder stacks up statewide without telling anyone it came from Lake Fork. That matters on pressured bass water, where one post can fill a cove with boats the next weekend. The free tier shows the top 10; Premium shows the full national board.

**Price:** free tier includes unlimited logging, 5 photo IDs, the top-10 leaderboard preview, a daily bite forecast and 2 planned trips. Premium is $3.99 a month or $29.99 a year (7-day trial on yearly) and adds an hourly forecast with species odds, catch insights and stats.

**Where it falls short:** the leaderboards are a free, season-long competition, not a judged tournament with payouts. The community is young, there are no depth charts, and its forecast is general rather than bass-tuned like BassForecast's. If you need a weight, no app weighs your fish; see [how to measure a fish](/blog/how-to-measure-a-fish).

## 2. BassForecast

**Best for:** anglers still learning how weather moves bass.

BassForecast rolls weather, barometric pressure trend, water temperature and solunar timing into a daily Bass Forecast Rating over 10 days. Its bait tool picks from 22 lure categories across 9 bass seasons, matched to your cover type: grass, wood or rock. There's a catch log, lake maps, and an account that works on iPhone, Android and the web. It has 20K App Store ratings at 4.6 stars.

**Price:** $5.99 a month or $29.95 a year as of November 2026. The full 10-day forecast and all bait picks are Pro.

**Where it falls short:** an "Epic" day can still be dead. Reviewers also ask for more of the "why" behind the score. Our guide to [barometric pressure and fishing](/blog/barometric-pressure-and-fishing) fills that gap.

## 3. Deep Dive

**Best for:** tournament anglers and anyone fishing a big-name bass lake.

Deep Dive is built on pro tournament data rather than community posts: a tournament patterns map filtered by season and style, a best-areas map, satellite water clarity maps for 170+ top lakes with 30-day history, lake level and flow graphs, wave maps and a 7-day forecast. If you fish a well-known tournament lake, there's a lot here. App Store rating: 4.7 from 6.9K.

**Price:** free 1-week trial. Pro is $79.99 a year, with monthly Pro listed from $9.99 to $19.99. A lighter Plus tier is $4.99 a month or $39.99 a year.

**Where it falls short:** the depth is concentrated on well-known tournament lakes. On a 40-acre city reservoir you'll get much less.

## 4. Fishbrain

**Best for:** seeing what bass anglers are catching on a lake you don't know.

On busy bass lakes, Fishbrain's catch reports show which baits keep showing up and how hard the water is getting hit. Pro adds Garmin HD depth maps and bait recommendations by species and area.

**Price:** free to download. Pro is $79.99 a year or $12.99 to $14.99 a month on the US App Store.

**Where it falls short:** crowd pins get mis-tagged on purpose, and some catch weights are generous. Full breakdown in [Is Fishbrain worth it?](/blog/is-fishbrain-worth-it)

## 5. Navionics Boating

**Best for:** finding ledges, humps, points and channel swings.

SonarChart HD contours down to 1-foot detail, offline charts, tracks and markers. For offshore summer bass on a ledge in 15 to 25 feet, this is the map most anglers end up paying for.

**Price:** $49.99 a year for US & Canada.

**Where it falls short:** a chart app, not a bass app. 2.9 stars on both the App Store and Google Play, mostly over price and changes.

## 6. onX Fish

**Best for:** Midwest bass anglers picking a new lake.

onX Fish shows agency survey data by lake: species, size distribution, catch per effort and stocking history, with filters like "trophy potential." That's how you find a Minnesota or Michigan lake with real smallmouth size before you drive.

**Price:** free version with the Lake Finder, GPS and offline maps. Midwest data is $34.99 a year.

**Where it falls short:** Midwest and Montana only.

## 7. FishAngler

**Best for:** free bass groups and a community map.

FishAngler's free account has bass groups organized by region and water, a 45-field logbook, fish ID and access points. VIP ($49.99 a year) adds bait intel and exact catch locations.

**Where it falls short:** busy feed, ads on the free tier, and the usual crowd-map accuracy issues.

## 8. TourneyX PRO

**Best for:** catch-photo-release tournaments with judges and payouts.

TourneyX runs online and live tournaments: you register, submit catches from the water, and the app culls your limit across several events at once. It's free to download; you pay each tournament's entry fee. 4.7 stars from 112 App Store ratings.

**Where it falls short:** it's a tournament tool, not a log. Rules, boards and payouts are set by each event's director, so read them before you enter.

## 9. ShareLunker and TrophyCatch

**Best for:** trophy bass in Texas and Florida.

Texas Parks and Wildlife's free ShareLunker app takes entries for bass of 8 pounds or 24 inches and up all year. Bass of 13 pounds or more caught January 1 through March 31 can be loaned to the program for spawning. Florida FWC's TrophyCatch app and website reward bass of 8 pounds or more, documented with a photo of the whole fish on a scale with the weight readable, then released. Details are on our [Texas bass fishing](/fishing/texas/bass-fishing) and [Florida bass fishing](/fishing/florida/bass-fishing) pages.

**Where it falls short:** single-state, few app ratings, and they only matter once you're near trophy size. Michigan's Master Angler program, covered on [Michigan bass fishing](/fishing/michigan/bass-fishing), recognizes bass of 21 inches or longer.

## Which bass setup makes sense

- **Bank angler chasing a first 5-pounder:** PB Fishing for the log and your PB, plus our [best bass lures by season](/blog/best-bass-lures-by-season). $0.
- **Weekend boater:** PB Fishing, Navionics for contours, BassForecast for a daily read. About $80 a year.
- **Tournament angler:** Deep Dive and Navionics for prep, TourneyX for events, PB Fishing for your season-long PB record.
- **Smallmouth trips:** onX Fish to pick Midwest lakes, plus our [Minnesota](/fishing/minnesota/bass-fishing) and [Tennessee](/fishing/tennessee/bass-fishing) bass pages for seasonal patterns.

## Before you pay for anything

Apps don't catch bass. The biggest jump most anglers make comes from fishing the right depth for the water temperature and season, and from keeping a record of what worked.
