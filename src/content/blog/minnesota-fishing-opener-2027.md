In Minnesota the fishing opener is closer to a state holiday than a date on a calendar. Cabins fill, bait shops open at midnight, and the big walleye lakes light up with running lights the minute the season starts. If you're planning for 2027, here's the date, what it covers and what you can already fish for before it.

## The 2027 opener date

The Minnesota DNR's published opener table puts the **2027 walleye and northern pike opener on Saturday, May 15, 2027**. Fishing for those two species opens at **12:01 a.m.** that Saturday on inland waters.

| Season | 2026 | 2027 | 2028 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Walleye and northern pike (inland) | May 9 | **May 15** | May 13 |
| Muskie (inland) | June 6 | June 5 | June 3 |
| Stream trout harvest (streams) | April 11 | April 10 | April 8 |

These dates come from the 2026 Minnesota Fishing Regulations booklet, which prints the next two years ahead. The 2027 booklet, published before the license year starts on March 1, is the final word, so check it before you drive north.

## The 2027 Governor's Fishing Opener: Cold Spring

The 79th Governor's Fishing Opener will be held **May 14-15, 2027 in Cold Spring**, on Long Lake and the connected Sauk River Chain of Lakes, which locals call the Horseshoe Chain. It's the first time Cold Spring hosts. The event follows the statewide opener: state statute puts the opener two weeks before the Saturday of Memorial Day weekend, which is why 2027 lands on May 15.

The Horseshoe Chain is a connected string of lakes off the Sauk River west of St. Cloud, with walleye, northern pike, bass and panfish. If you're planning to fish there on opener weekend, expect boat traffic and media around the host lake, and book lodging early.

## The proposed 4-walleye limit

The other 2027 story is the limit itself. The Minnesota DNR has proposed cutting the **statewide walleye and sauger limit from 6 to 4 combined**, keeping the rule that only **1 walleye over 20 inches** may be in possession. If adopted, it would take effect **March 1, 2027**, the start of the 2027 license year, so the 2027 opener would be the first one under the new limit. Border waters, large-lake special regulations and treaty-managed waters keep their own rules.

It isn't final. The public comment period closed March 5, 2026, and because more than 50 valid hearing requests came in, the rule has to go before an administrative law judge. As of August 2026, Outdoor News reported the hearing still hadn't been scheduled. Until the DNR announces a decision, treat 4 as proposed and 6 as the current limit, and check the 2027 regulations booklet before opener.

Big walleye lakes already run their own, lower limits (Leech 4, Lake of the Woods and Mille Lacs have their own slots and bags), so for many opener trips the statewide change matters less than the lake's special regulation.

## What opens on May 15, 2027

- **Walleye and sauger** on inland waters. The statewide limit is 6 combined with only 1 walleye over 20 inches (4 if the proposed rule above is adopted), but many of the famous lakes have their own slot or bag limits: Leech, Vermilion, Winnibigoshish, Lake of the Woods and Mille Lacs are all different. Look up your lake before opener. Our [Minnesota best lakes guide](/fishing/minnesota/best-lakes) lists the major special regulations.
- **Northern pike**, with zone limits: a 2-fish limit in the northeast zone with a 30–40 inch protected slot, 10 in the north-central zone with 22–26 inches released, and 2 with a 24-inch minimum in the southern zone.

## What's already open before the opener

You don't have to wait for May 15 to fish. In the 2026 regulations these are open all year on inland waters:

- **Crappie** (10 statewide, lower on many special-regulation lakes)
- **Sunfish** (20 statewide, 5 or 10 on a growing list of lakes)
- **Yellow perch**
- **Channel catfish**

Late-ice crappie and sunfish in March and early-April crappie in warming, dark-bottom bays are the classic pre-opener trips. Just don't target walleye or pike before the opener: catch-and-release doesn't make it legal during the closed season.

**Bass** run on a catch-and-release season in spring and open to harvest later in May; in 2026 the harvest opened May 23 in the southern and north-central zones and with the walleye opener in the northeast zone. Check the 2027 booklet for next year's date. The [Minnesota bass fishing guide](/fishing/minnesota/bass-fishing) has the zone map.

## Other 2027 dates worth knowing

- **Stream trout harvest:** April 10, 2027 (the second Saturday in April, a change that started in 2026).
- **Muskie:** June 5, 2027, 54-inch minimum inland. Canada border waters open later with a 50-inch minimum.
- **End of the current ice season:** the inland walleye and pike season that opened May 9, 2026 runs through **February 28, 2027**, so hard-water walleye and pike are open all winter.
- **Ice shelters** must be off inland lakes by March 1, 2027 in the southern part of the state and March 15, 2027 in the north. The DNR's ice-thickness guidance is the safety rule for every trip: at least 4 inches of new clear ice to walk on.

## How the opener bite usually goes

Opener falls only a few weeks after ice-out on the northern lakes, and walleye have just finished spawning. Expect fish shallow and close to where they spawned:

- **Shallow rock and gravel** near river mouths and wind-blown shorelines, often in 4 to 10 feet.
- **Jig and minnow** (a 1/8 to 1/4 oz jig with a fathead or shiner) is the opener standard. Slow down if the water is still in the 40s °F.
- **Low light matters.** The midnight start isn't just tradition; walleye feed hardest at dusk, at night and at dawn, especially in clear water.
- **Wind is your friend.** A steady wind pushing onto a rocky shoreline stirs up bait and colors the water, and opener walleye follow it.

If the opener weekend is cold and windy, which it often is, fish the protected bays and river current for pike and the deeper breaks closest to spawning areas for walleye. ## Before you go: checklist

1. **Buy your 2027 license** after March 1, 2027. Residents and nonresidents both need one from age 16. Details: [Minnesota fishing license](/fishing/minnesota/fishing-license).
2. **Check your lake's special regulations** in the 2027 booklet or the DNR's LakeFinder.
3. **Book early.** Resorts on Mille Lacs, Leech, Winnie and Lake of the Woods often fill for opener weekend months ahead.
4. **Pack for 40°F on the water**, even in mid-May, and wear a life jacket: early-season water is cold enough to be dangerous.
5. **Log your first walleye of the year.** Length, photo, lake, wind and water temp. After a few openers, your own log tells you more about where to start than any report.

## Where Minnesota anglers go for opener

The big, famous opener lakes are walleye factories with their own rules: Mille Lacs, Leech Lake, Lake Winnibigoshish, Lake Vermilion, Upper Red Lake and Lake of the Woods with the Rainy River. Metro anglers often stay close to home on Lake Minnetonka, the Mississippi River pools and the St. Croix.
