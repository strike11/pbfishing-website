Lure lists that rank "the 10 best bass lures" without saying when to throw them aren't much help. A jerkbait that dominates in 45 °F water in February will barely get looked at in July, and a frog that's deadly over summer grass is useless in winter. So this list is built around two things you can actually check at the ramp: **water temperature** and **water clarity**.

## The season cheat sheet

Use water temperature, not the calendar. A Florida February and a Minnesota February are different seasons.

| Season | Water temp | Bass are | Top picks from this list |
|---|---|---|---|
| Winter | Below 50 °F | Deep, sluggish, schooled up | Jerkbait (#3), jig (#8), blade bait (#12) |
| Pre-spawn | 50 to 60 °F | Moving shallow, feeding hard | Lipless crankbait (#4), jerkbait (#3), squarebill (#5) |
| Spawn | 60 to 70 °F | On beds, protective | Wacky stick worm (#2), Texas-rigged creature bait (#1), swim jig |
| Post-spawn and early summer | 70 to 80 °F | Recovering, chasing shad | Walking topwater (#9), bladed jig (#7), swimbait |
| Summer | 80 °F and up | Offshore deep, or shallow in shade and grass | Deep crankbait (#11), Texas worm (#1), frog (#10), drop shot |
| Fall | Falling from 75 to 55 °F | Following baitfish into creeks | Spinnerbait (#6), squarebill (#5), lipless (#4), topwater (#9) |

For how temperature drives everything a bass does, see [water temperature and fish activity](/blog/water-temperature-and-fish-activity).

## The clarity cheat sheet

| Clarity | How to tell | Colors | Lure style |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clear | You can see a lure 6 ft down or more | Green pumpkin, watermelon, smoke, ghost or natural shad | Finesse, slower, lighter line (fluorocarbon 8 to 12 lb) |
| Stained | Lure disappears at 2 to 4 ft | Chartreuse and white, sexy shad, red craw, green pumpkin with chartreuse tail | Moderate vibration and flash, willow and Colorado blades |
| Muddy | Lure disappears inside 1 ft | Black and blue, black and chartreuse, solid white, chartreuse | Thump and rattle: Colorado blades, chatterbaits, rattling crankbaits |

Now the twelve.

## 1. Texas-rigged plastic worm

**Seasons:** all, best in summer and spawn. **Clarity:** all.

The most versatile bass lure ever made. Rig a 6- to 10-inch ribbon-tail or straight-tail worm on a 3/0 to 5/0 offset or EWG hook with a bullet weight. Use 1/8 oz in shallow water and light cover, 1/4 oz for most situations, and 3/8 to 1/2 oz for summer ledges in 12 to 20 feet or for punching thicker grass. Peg the weight with a toothpick or bobber stop in cover.

**Colors:** green pumpkin and watermelon in clear water, junebug or plum in stained, black and blue in muddy. **Retrieve:** lift the rod from 9 to 11 o'clock, let it fall on slack line, reel up slack, repeat. Most bites come on the fall and feel like a tick or the line jumping sideways.

## 2. Wacky-rigged stick worm

**Seasons:** spawn, post-spawn, summer. **Clarity:** clear to lightly stained.

A 5-inch soft stick worm (the Senko style) hooked through the middle on a size 1 to 1/0 wacky or octopus hook, with an O-ring to save baits. No weight, or a small nail weight in the head for a faster fall. It falls with a shimmy that bedding and pressured bass can't stand.

**Colors:** green pumpkin, watermelon red, natural shad. **Retrieve:** cast near docks, laydowns or beds, let it sink on slack line, and watch the line. Twitch it twice, let it fall again. Fishing it too fast is the most common mistake.

## 3. Suspending jerkbait

**Seasons:** winter and pre-spawn, roughly 42 to 55 °F. **Clarity:** clear to lightly stained.

A 4- to 4.5-inch suspending minnow that dives 4 to 8 feet. The pause is the whole technique: the bait hangs motionless in front of a cold bass that won't chase.

**Colors:** ghost shad and translucent colors in clear water, chrome with a blue or black back in light stain. **Retrieve:** jerk-jerk-pause. In 50 °F water, pause 3 to 5 seconds. In 42 °F water, pause 10 to 20 seconds. Use 8 to 10 lb fluorocarbon so it stays down.

## 4. Lipless crankbait

**Seasons:** pre-spawn and fall, also winter around grass. **Clarity:** stained to muddy is best.

A 1/2 oz rattling lipless crankbait covers huge amounts of water and finds active fish fast. It's the go-to in early spring over submerged grass flats.

**Colors:** red craw in early spring (the classic pre-spawn color), chrome or gold in fall, chartreuse in muddy water. **Retrieve:** steady over grass, ripping it free when it ticks the weeds; in colder water, a yo-yo retrieve where you let it flutter to the bottom between lifts.

## 5. Squarebill crankbait

**Seasons:** pre-spawn, fall, and shallow summer mornings. **Clarity:** all, best in stained.

A shallow-diving crankbait (2 to 5 feet) with a square lip that deflects off wood, rock and docks. The deflection is what triggers strikes.

**Colors:** shad patterns in fall, craw colors in spring, chartreuse and black back in muddy water. **Retrieve:** cast past cover and bang the bait into it. If you're not hitting things, you're fishing it wrong.

## 6. Spinnerbait

**Seasons:** fall and pre-spawn, also windy days any season. **Clarity:** stained to muddy.

A 3/8 to 1/2 oz spinnerbait is the lure that's hardest to fish badly. Double willow blades for flash and speed in clearer water; a Colorado blade (or Colorado and willow) for thump in muddy water and cold water.

**Colors:** white and chartreuse with silver blades in most water; black with a gold Colorado blade at night or in mud. **Retrieve:** a steady wind just fast enough that you can see the bait 1 to 2 feet down. Bump it into cover. Add a trailer hook when fish are short-striking.

## 7. Bladed jig (chatterbait)

**Seasons:** pre-spawn through early summer, and fall. **Clarity:** stained is best.

A 3/8 to 1/2 oz jig with a hex blade on the nose that makes it vibrate hard. Excellent around grass and in wind.

**Colors:** green pumpkin with a craw-colored trailer in spring, white or shad with a swimbait trailer when bass are on baitfish. **Retrieve:** steady, just fast enough to feel the vibration, with an occasional pop to rip it off grass.

## 8. Jig

**Seasons:** all, best in winter and on summer structure. **Clarity:** all.

A 3/8 oz flipping jig for shallow wood and docks, a 1/2 to 3/4 oz football jig for rocky bottoms and deep points in winter and summer, with a craw trailer. Jigs catch bigger-than-average bass, which is why tournament anglers trust them.

**Colors:** green pumpkin or brown in clear water, black and blue in stained or muddy. **Retrieve:** drag the football jig slowly along the bottom; pitch the flipping jig to cover and hop it once or twice before moving on.

## 9. Walking topwater

**Seasons:** post-spawn, summer mornings and evenings, fall. **Clarity:** clear to stained.

A 4- to 5-inch walking bait (Zara Spook style) zigzags across the surface. The blowups are why a lot of anglers fish for bass at all.

**Colors:** bone or white in most water, clear or shad in very clear water, black at dusk. **Retrieve:** rod tip down, short rhythmic twitches with slack in the line so the bait walks side to side. When a fish blows up, wait until you feel weight before setting the hook.

## 10. Hollow-body frog

**Seasons:** summer and early fall. **Clarity:** any; it's about cover, not clarity.

Built to crawl over lily pads, matted hydrilla and slop where no other lure can go. Fish it on 50 to 65 lb braid and a heavy rod, because you have to pull a bass out of thick vegetation.

**Colors:** black when the sky is dark or the water is dark, white or a natural frog color under bright skies. **Retrieve:** twitch it across the mat and pause over holes. On the blow-up, count one-two (or wait until the frog disappears), then set the hook hard.

## 11. Deep-diving crankbait

**Seasons:** summer, also late fall. **Clarity:** clear to stained.

A crankbait that dives 12 to 20 feet to reach offshore ledges, humps and points where summer bass school. This is a boat technique mostly, but long casts from a steep bank or a dam can reach the edge.

**Colors:** sexy shad and citrus shad, chartreuse with a blue back in stain. **Retrieve:** long cast, reel hard to get it down, then a steady retrieve with the bill digging the bottom. Use 10 to 12 lb fluorocarbon to reach maximum depth.

## 12. Blade bait

**Seasons:** winter, roughly 38 to 48 °F. **Clarity:** clear to lightly stained.

A 3/8 to 1/2 oz metal blade bait vibrates hard on a short lift and then falls. It's a cold-water specialty for bass and smallmouth schooled on deep structure, and it's one of the few baits that still works when bass barely move.

**Colors:** silver or gold in most water. **Retrieve:** let it hit bottom, lift the rod 12 to 18 inches until you feel it vibrate, then let it fall on a semi-slack line. Most bites come on the fall.

## What is the best bass lure of all time?

If you have to pick one, it's the plastic worm. Nick Creme began making soft plastic worms in Akron, Ohio, in 1949, and the Texas rig built around worms like his became the foundation of modern bass fishing. Seventy-plus years later it still catches bass in all four seasons, all clarities and every state. It's cheap, it's weedless, and it teaches a beginner to feel bites. Second place is a matter of taste: a jig catches bigger fish, and a spinnerbait is easier to fish well.

## What lure was banned by Bassmaster?

The **Alabama rig**, a weighted head with several wire arms, each carrying a swimbait, that imitates a small school of baitfish. After it caught on in 2011, B.A.S.S. announced in January 2012 that Bassmaster Classic and Elite Series anglers could use only one lure at a time in practice and competition, effective February 1, 2012. That rule eliminated umbrella rigs, along with double-lure rigs. The Elite Series rules committee felt the rig took away some of the skill that should be needed at the top level. In 2014, B.A.S.S. extended the umbrella-rig restriction to events that feed Classic qualification, including the Opens.

It's not banned for you. The Alabama rig is legal for recreational anglers in most places, but several states limit how many hooks or hooked lures a rig can carry. Check your state's regulations before you tie one on. See your state's bass page for local rules: [Texas](/fishing/texas/bass-fishing), [Florida](/fishing/florida/bass-fishing), [Tennessee](/fishing/tennessee/bass-fishing), [Michigan](/fishing/michigan/bass-fishing), [Minnesota](/fishing/minnesota/bass-fishing).

## A three-lure starter kit

If you're new or on a budget, buy these first and learn them well:

1. A bag of 7-inch green pumpkin worms, 4/0 offset hooks and 1/4 oz bullet weights.
2. A 3/8 oz white and chartreuse spinnerbait with double willow blades.
3. A squarebill crankbait in a shad color.

That covers bottom, mid-depth and shallow cover across most of the year. Add a suspending jerkbait for winter and a walking topwater for summer once you're catching fish. And remember that lures matter less than location: the best bait in the box won't catch fish that aren't there, which is the point of the [90/10 rule](/blog/90-10-rule-fishing).
