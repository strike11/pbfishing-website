"Finally broke my PB after 6 years of trying" is one of the most common post types on r/bassfishing, and the stories behind them repeat. The fish came from a lake known for big bass, in the cold months, on a slow bottom bait, usually on a day when the angler caught three fish instead of thirty. You can't schedule a personal best, but you can stack those odds on purpose.

## Step one: fish where big bass live

The size of your PB is capped by the lake. Genetics and growing season decide how big bass get, and Texas A&M's pond management guide puts it bluntly: a northern largemouth tops out around 10 pounds, while a big female Florida largemouth can reach about 20. A small northern lake may not hold a 6-pounder at all. A Florida-genetics reservoir in Texas may hold dozens of 10s.

How to find the right water:

- **Look at trophy program results.** Texas and Florida publish where their big fish come from. In 2025, Texas ShareLunker collected 14 bass of 13 pounds or more from nine public lakes: O.H. Ivie (six of them), Richland-Chambers, Lady Bird, J.B. Thomas, Alan Henry, Tawakoni, Sam Rayburn, Tyler and Toledo Bend. By late 2025, 79 Texas public lakes had produced at least one.
- **Check lake records and state agency survey reports.** Biologists' reports often list the size structure of the bass population, which tells you whether big fish exist in numbers.
- **Don't ignore small water with history.** Lady Bird Lake, a reservoir in downtown Austin, gave up a 14.05-pound bass on February 5, 2025, a lake record.

## Step two: fish when big bass eat

The best single piece of data on this comes from Texas. In the Wildlife Management Institute's August 2025 bulletin, biologists Steve Miranda and Darren Shoemaker described 2,750 bass of 8 pounds or more that Texas anglers logged from 2018 to 2024. **The biggest of those lunkers were caught in February and the smallest in June.**

That lines up with everything else in the record books:

- Texas ShareLunker's 13-pound-plus collection season runs January 1 through March 31.
- ShareLunkers 686 and 687, a 14.35-pounder from Fort Phantom Hill and a 13.10-pounder from Toledo Bend, came in early 2026.
- Tennessee's current state record largemouth, 15 lb 7.5 oz from Nickajack Reservoir, was caught on February 28, 2026.

The usual explanation: big females are at their heaviest just before the spawn, when they're carrying eggs.

### Southern vs northern timing

| Where | Prime PB window | What's happening |
|---|---|---|
| Florida, South Texas | Winter into early spring | Florida-strain bass move toward spawning areas early; cold fronts slow them down |
| Texas, the Deep South, Tennessee Valley | Late January through March | Pre-spawn females staging on points, creek channels and the first breaks off spawning flats |
| Michigan, Minnesota, the North | Spring pre-spawn, and again in fall | Pre-spawn largemouth and smallmouth fatten up; fall fish feed heavily before winter |

In the South, the cold fronts that make February miserable are part of the deal. Fish the stable days between fronts, and fish the warmest water you can find in the afternoon.

## Step three: throw bigger, fish slower

The same Texas data shows where in the water column those 8-pound-plus fish ate: **about 54% on bottom-oriented lures, about 40% on midwater lures**, and surface catches less common. Topwater blowups make the videos. Jigs and soft plastics make the record lists.

A trophy-hunting box built around that:

- **Football or flipping jig, ¾ to 1 oz,** green pumpkin or black and blue, dragged on rock, points and channel swings.
- **10-inch plastic worm on a Texas rig** with a ⅜ to ½ oz weight, or a big creature bait for flipping cover.
- **Swimbaits in the 6 to 8 inch range,** slow-rolled near the bottom in clear water, especially where bass eat shad, trout or big bluegill.
- **Deep-diving crankbaits** for the midwater share, banged off the bottom on the first drops near spawning areas.
- **Live shiners** where legal, the classic Florida trophy bait.

Gear up to match: 17 to 25 lb fluorocarbon or 50 to 65 lb braid on a heavy rod, and a reel that can stop a fish from reaching the brush. A PB lost to light line is the worst story in fishing.

Then accept the trade-off. Big baits get fewer bites. A good trophy day can be a handful of bites. If you keep switching to a finesse rig to "get on the board," you'll catch more 2-pounders and fewer 8s.

## Trophy waters by state

These are the starting points. Each state page has lakes, seasons, regulations and trophy programs in detail.

- **Texas.** ShareLunker data is the best map of giant bass in the country. O.H. Ivie led 2025 in 13-pound-plus fish, Toledo Bend produced one for the third collection season running in 2026, and Lake Fork gave up the 11.7-pounder described in the Wildlife Management Institute bulletin. Year-round, ShareLunker accepts bass of 8 pounds or 24 inches from Texas public waters. See [Texas bass fishing](/fishing/texas/bass-fishing).
- **Florida.** TrophyCatch counts bass of 8 pounds and up that are documented and released. The state reports more than 17,000 of them, including 176 Hall of Fame fish over 13 pounds. FWC has also tagged 8-pound-plus bass with $100 reward tags since 2011. See [Florida bass fishing](/fishing/florida/bass-fishing).
- **Tennessee.** The state record largemouth, 15 lb 7.5 oz, came from Nickajack in February 2026, and Dale Hollow still holds the 11 lb 15 oz smallmouth record from 1955. See [Tennessee bass fishing](/fishing/tennessee/bass-fishing).
- **Michigan and Minnesota.** Northern-strain largemouth top out lower, so many anglers chase their PB in smallmouth instead. See [Michigan bass fishing](/fishing/michigan/bass-fishing) and [Minnesota bass fishing](/fishing/minnesota/bass-fishing).

## When it happens: handling a PB

You'll be shaking. Have a plan before the fish is in the net.

1. **Use a big rubber net** so a heavy fish isn't swung over the gunwale by the line.
2. **Hold it right.** Texas 4-H handling guidance says fish under about 5 pounds can be held vertically by the lower jaw; larger fish should be held horizontally and supported with two hands. A big bass hanging by its jaw puts its full weight on the jaw.
3. **Keep air time short.** Trout Unlimited's summary of catch-and-release research suggests keeping fish out of the water for no more than 15 to 20 seconds at a time. Put the fish in the livewell or back in the net in the water between steps.
4. **Weigh it carefully.** A lip-grip scale is quick, but the same summary notes lip-gripping devices can injure mouth tissue in some species. A wet weigh sling or a scale with the fish supported is kinder to a trophy.

## Document it so it counts

A PB is a number you'll repeat for years. Make it one you can back up.

- **Length first, on a board,** mouth closed, tail pinched, with the board in the photo. See [how to measure a fish](/blog/how-to-measure-a-fish).
- **Weight on a scale with the display readable** in a second photo.
- **A side-profile shot** of the whole fish, then the hero shot.
- **Write down the date, water and conditions** while you remember them.
- **Enter it** in your state's program if it qualifies, ShareLunker in Texas or TrophyCatch in Florida, and follow that program's rules exactly.

PB Fishing saves the photo, length, weight, date and a conditions snapshot (weather and pressure) every time you log a catch, and updates your personal best for the species on its own. That history is useful later, when you want to know what the weather was doing the last three times a big fish ate.

Then let her go if you can. See [how to release a fish](/blog/how-to-release-a-fish) for the details that matter on a heavy fish, and [what is a good size bass](/blog/what-is-a-good-size-bass) for where your new number ranks.
