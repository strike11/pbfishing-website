Most "best fishing app" lists rank ten apps as if they all do the same thing. They don't. A depth chart app and a catch log solve different problems, and paying for two apps that overlap is how you end up with $150 a year in fishing subscriptions you barely open.

So this list is sorted by job. For each pick you get what it's good at, what it costs, and where it falls short. Every price below was read off the app's US App Store listing in November 2026. Prices change, sales come and go, and the App Store shows the current figure before you pay, so check it there too.

## How we picked

We read the App Store listings, the in-app purchase lists and the descriptions for more than 20 fishing apps, plus a few hundred user reviews and Reddit threads from r/bassfishing, r/FishingForBeginners and r/kayakfishing. Three things came up again and again:

- **Billing surprises.** Trials that roll into a yearly charge, cancel buttons that are hard to find, and "free" apps where almost everything sits behind Pro.
- **Bad spot data.** Catches pinned to the wrong pond, ponds that dried up years ago, and anglers who tag the wrong lake on purpose.
- **Feeds instead of tools.** Plenty of anglers want a log and a map, not another social network.

An app made this list if it does one job well and is honest about what you pay for. We left out apps that throw a paywall on the first screen.

## 1. Fishbrain

**Best for:** finding out what's being caught on a water near you, and depth maps without buying a separate chart app.

Fishbrain is the biggest name in the category. It describes its community as more than 20 million anglers, and that scale is the real product: on popular lakes you can see what species people are catching, which baits keep showing up, and how hard a spot is getting hit. Its App Store description lists Garmin Navionics depth charts for the US and Canada, BiteTime forecasts, regulations for 30+ US states and a fish ID tool.

**Price:** free to download. As of November 2026 the US App Store lists the Power Fishing Package at $12.99 or $14.99 a month, $41.99 a quarter and $79.99 a year. Fishbrain's own Pro page offers a 3-day free trial. Pro adds exact catch locations, bait and lure recommendations, Garmin HD depth maps, local regulations and private waypoints.

**Where it falls short:** the yearly price is high for something many anglers open on weekends. The catch map is only as honest as the people pinning catches, and a lot of anglers mis-tag spots on purpose. If you want a quiet log, the feed can feel like noise. Full details in [Is Fishbrain worth it?](/blog/is-fishbrain-worth-it)

## 2. Navionics Boating

**Best for:** depth contours, navigation and marking structure from a boat or kayak.

When ice anglers and kayakers argue about apps, Navionics is usually the one they keep. SonarChart HD bathymetry with 1-foot contour detail, offline charts, NOAA charts in US coverage, tracks and markers. If you're hunting a 12-foot hump or a channel swing, this is the tool.

**Price:** $49.99 a year for the US & Canada region as of November 2026, after a short free trial.

**Where it falls short:** it's a chart app, not a fishing log. Its US App Store rating sits at 2.9 stars, and recent reviews complain about price increases and features being cut. Many anglers still pay because nothing else has its lake coverage.

## 3. PB Fishing

**Best for:** logging every catch for free, tracking a personal best per species, and seeing how your fish stack up in your state.

Full disclosure: this is our app. We put it third because it does one job, the log and the PB, and it doesn't try to be a chart plotter.

You snap a side-profile photo, the app suggests the species and estimates length, and you confirm or fix it. Date, water, weather and barometric pressure are saved with the catch. If you're out of signal, the catch is stored on the phone and uploads later. Your PB per species updates on its own. Species leaderboards run nationally and by state, and reset every six months. A leaderboard entry shows your name, state, length and weight. It doesn't show the lake.

There's also a map of 20,000+ named US waters with species lists, live conditions and a bite forecast, plus links to each state's official license pages.

**Price:** unlimited catch logging is free, with 5 photo IDs, a top-10 leaderboard preview, a daily bite forecast and up to 2 planned trips. Premium is $3.99 a month or $29.99 a year (7-day trial on yearly), billed by Apple.

**Where it falls short:** iPhone only, iOS 17.6 or newer. No Android. The community is young, so on many waters you won't find the volume of catch reports Fishbrain has. No depth charts: pair it with Navionics if you fish structure.

## 4. FishAngler

**Best for:** a generous free tier with a community catch map.

FishAngler is the app Reddit often names when someone asks for a free Fishbrain alternative. The free account includes fishing maps with access points and boat ramps, a logbook with 45+ fields per catch, fish ID, angler groups and built-in IGFA and state records. Its App Store listing also mentions property-line data, which matters if you bank fish and don't want to trespass.

**Price:** free. VIP is $6.99 a month or $49.99 a year as of November 2026.

**Where it falls short:** it's social first. Expect a busy feed and notifications. The catch map has the same honesty problem every crowd-sourced map has.

## 5. BassForecast

**Best for:** bass anglers who want a daily read on conditions and lure suggestions.

BassForecast rolls weather, barometric pressure trends, water temperature and solunar timing into a daily Bass Forecast Rating, then suggests baits by season and cover type (grass, wood or rock). It also has a simple catch log and works across iPhone, iPad, Android and the web.

**Price:** $5.99 a month or $29.95 a year as of November 2026.

**Where it falls short:** bass only. And like every forecast app, some days rated "epic" will be dead. Use it to learn how pressure and wind affect the bite, not as a guarantee. Our explainer on [barometric pressure and fishing](/blog/barometric-pressure-and-fishing) covers what the numbers actually mean.

## 6. Fishing Points

**Best for:** saltwater and big-water anglers who live by tides, wind and marine weather.

Fishing Points puts tides, solunar periods, hourly wind and gusts, pressure and marine forecasts in one place, with offline NOAA nautical charts, more than 50,000 boat ramps, trolling path recording and seagrass, oyster bed and mangrove layers. Inshore anglers in Florida and the Carolinas use it as a pre-trip checklist.

**Price:** free basic version. Premium in-app purchases on the US App Store range from $9.99 to $49.99, with yearly Premium at $49.99 and a Basic Premium tier at $29.99, as of November 2026.

**Where it falls short:** freshwater lake detail is thin outside a handful of states. Its listing says lake depth maps are available in Florida, Minnesota, Nebraska, New Hampshire, North Dakota and Washington. Reviewers also note that "high activity" days don't always fish that way.

## 7. onX Fish

**Best for:** Midwest anglers who want state survey data, stocking info and lake maps without a social feed.

onX Fish pulls agency data (species, size and catch-per-effort numbers from surveys) into lake pages with contours and GPS navigation. Reviewers like that it's all substance and no posts. If you fish Minnesota, Wisconsin or Michigan, it's worth a look before you commit to anything else.

**Price:** free version with the Lake Finder, GPS, offline maps and aerial imagery. The Midwest region is $34.99 a year with a 7-day trial, as of November 2026.

**Where it falls short:** coverage is Midwest only. If you live in Texas or Florida, skip it for now.

## 8. Fish Rules

**Best for:** saltwater regulations: bag limits, size limits, open seasons and gear rules.

Fish Rules covers recreational saltwater rules for state and federal waters from Maine to Texas, plus California, Hawaii and the Caribbean. It uses GPS to show the rules where you are, works offline, and answers questions like whether you need a circle hook or a venting tool. One App Store reviewer said it saved them "from a major fine."

**Price:** free, with a ProStaff purchase at $29.99 as of November 2026.

**Where it falls short:** saltwater only, and its own disclaimer says the rules shown don't have legal force. The state agency is the final word.

## 9. Anglers' Log

**Best for:** anglers who want a private, ad-free journal and nothing else.

Anglers' Log is a straight logbook: catches with photos, species, bait, gear, spot, water conditions and weather. Spots pinned on a map, stats on which baits, times and conditions produce. No feed. Pro adds automatic weather and tide data, GPS trails, custom fields, cloud backup and CSV export.

**Price:** free. Pro is $1.49 a month or $11.99 a year as of November 2026. That's the cheapest paid tier on this list.

**Where it falls short:** no community, no forecast, no leaderboards. That's the point for most of its users. We compare it with other logs in [Best fishing log apps](/blog/best-fishing-log-apps).

## 10. Your state's agency app

**Best for:** carrying your license and checking official rules.

Florida's Fish|Hunt FL (FWC) and Texas Outdoor Annual (TPWD) are free on the App Store, and most states have something similar. They're the only apps here that sell licenses and show official regulations. Expect clunky design and spotty offline support: several have App Store ratings in the 3s. Screenshot your license before you lose signal.

Not sure what license you need? Start with [do you need a fishing license for catch and release](/blog/do-you-need-a-fishing-license-for-catch-and-release), or your state's page, like [the Texas fishing license guide](/fishing/texas/fishing-license).

## Which combo makes sense

You don't need ten apps. Here's what most anglers actually run:

- **Bank angler on a budget:** a free log (PB Fishing, Anglers' Log or MyCatch) plus your state agency app. Total: $0.
- **Weekend bass angler with a kayak:** Navionics ($49.99/yr) for contours, a free log, and BassForecast if you want a daily read. Around $50 to $80 a year.
- **Moved to a new state:** Fishbrain or FishAngler for a month to learn which waters hold what, then a log to track your own results.
- **Inshore saltwater:** Fishing Points for tides and wind, Fish Rules for limits, a log for your own patterns.

<p class="callout warn"><strong>Check first:</strong> before you start any free trial, note the renewal date and price on the Apple confirmation sheet. To cancel on iPhone, open Settings, tap your name, then Subscriptions.</p>

## What no app will do for you

No app knows where the fish are today. Crowd-sourced maps lag and get gamed, forecasts are averages, and the best data you'll ever have is what you write down yourself: date, water temperature, wind, what bit and where. If you want to start keeping that kind of record, our guide on [what to record in a fishing log](/blog/fishing-log-what-to-record) is a good first read. And if you've had a run of empty trips, [why am I not catching fish](/blog/why-am-i-not-catching-fish) covers the fixes an app can't make for you.
