Ice anglers are a tough crowd for apps, and fair enough. One r/IceFishing regular put it plainly: "fishing is about escaping my screen." So this list skips anything that only looks good on a couch. On the ice, an app earns its spot if it does one of four things: shows the contours before you drill, saves a GPS point so you can find a hole again, keeps working with zero bars, or tells you something useful about the ice. You can run it with gloves half off at 5°F, or it doesn't help.

We opened every App Store and Google Play listing below in November 2026 and only describe features the listings or the developers' own pages state. Prices are US App Store prices unless noted; Google Play doesn't show subscription prices on its store pages.

We make PB Fishing, and it's #1 here. We explain why, and we're just as plain about what it doesn't do.

## 1. PB Fishing

**Best for:** logging every fish through the ice for free, even with no signal, and seeing your personal best for each species update on its own.

We rank PB Fishing first for the ice because it does the one job every other app on this list treats as an extra. You take a side-profile photo, the app suggests the species and estimates length from the photo, and you confirm it. Date, water, weather and barometric pressure are saved with the catch. If you're three miles out on Lake of the Woods with no bars, the catch is stored on the phone and uploads when you're back in range. Logging is unlimited on the free plan, so a 40-perch day costs nothing.

Your PB for each species is tracked automatically, which turns "was that my biggest crappie?" into a one-tap answer. Leaderboards run by state and nationally, reset every six months, and show your name, state, length and weight, never the lake, so posting a big walleye doesn't hand anyone your spot. The app also lists 20,000+ named US waters with species, seasonal activity and a bite forecast, and links to each state's official regulation pages.

**Price:** free forever for unlimited logging, 5 photo IDs, the top-10 leaderboard preview, a daily bite forecast and up to 2 planned trips. Premium is $3.99 a month or $29.99 a year (7-day free trial on yearly), billed by Apple or Google Play. It adds unlimited photo IDs, the hourly forecast, the full national leaderboard, catch insights and 10-day weather.

**Where it falls short:** no depth charts, no flasher integration and no ice reports. The community is young, so many lakes have few catch reports yet, and the Android app is new (November 2026). Pair it with a map app.

## 2. Navionics Boating

**Best for:** the most detailed contours on the most lakes, and markers and tracks you can follow back to a spot.

Garmin's chart app offers SonarChart HD bathymetry with 1-foot (0.5-meter) bottom contour detail, a relief shading overlay, markers, track recording, and charts you can use offline. On the ice, that means finding the break, dropping a marker and walking the GPS dot to it before you drill.

**Price:** free trial, then $49.99 a year for US & Canada charts.

**Where it falls short:** 2.9 stars on both the US App Store (2.4K ratings) and Google Play. Its listing doesn't mention ice features. Download your lakes on Wi-Fi before you leave.

## 3. onX Fish

**Best for:** Midwest anglers who want state survey data, waypoints and a look at where ice is forming.

onX Fish covers 13 states including Minnesota, Wisconsin and Michigan. The free version includes Lake Finder, GPS navigation, offline maps, map layers and aerial imagery. The membership adds the full lake data and paid layers such as **Recent Imagery**, which onX says can show ice development during early ice. onX's own ice page adds that imagery doesn't prove the ice is safe, which is the right attitude. Waypoints save access points, hazards and spots worth drilling, and Tracker lets you retrace your route.

**Price:** $34.99 a year for the Midwest region, with a 7-day trial.

**Where it falls short:** a few App Store reviewers say contour detail is low on some lakes. Coverage stops at its 13 states.

## 4. Fishbrain

**Best for:** seeing what other anglers report catching on a busy lake.

Fishbrain's value is volume: 78K ratings at 4.7 stars on the US App Store. Its listing includes depth charts from Garmin Navionics and C-MAP, and private waypoints. On heavily fished winter lakes you can see which species are showing up.

**Price:** Pro is $79.99 a year, $41.99 a quarter, or $12.99 to $14.99 a month.

**Where it falls short:** the listing doesn't mention ice fishing or offline use, and it's the most expensive app here. See [is Fishbrain worth it?](/blog/is-fishbrain-worth-it)

## 5. Fishidy

**Best for:** Fishing Hot Spots maps with contours, offline.

Fishidy, now sold by Teledyne FLIR Maritime (Raymarine's parent), calls itself the only fishing app powered by Fishing Hot Spots maps. Premium adds depth contours, Hot Spots with coordinates and offline maps, and it syncs waypoints with a Raymarine display.

**Price:** $9.99 a month or $49.99 a year.

**Where it falls short:** 3.4 stars on the App Store and 2.2 on Google Play, and nothing ice-specific in the listing. If you own a Humminbird, the **Minn Kota Fishing** app (formerly One-Boat Network) is the LakeMaster equivalent, with up-to-1-foot contours and a $24.99 USA and Canada map purchase, though it's built around Johnson Outdoors hardware.

## 6. Fish Deeper

**Best for:** anglers with a Deeper castable sonar who want to mark holes and use the phone as a flasher.

The app works without a sonar for maps and offline downloads, but the ice features need the hardware. With a Deeper sonar, **ice fishing mode** turns the phone into a flasher and lets you **mark ice holes**. The sonar can also build your own bathymetric maps in open water.

**Price:** free app; listed plans run from $6.99 a month to $59.99 a year for Premium+.

**Where it falls short:** without the sonar, it's mostly another map app.

## 7. Ice Report

**Best for:** a quick look at what other anglers measured this week.

Ice Report does one thing: anglers drill, measure, and post the thickness at their GPS position. Reports newer than 3 days show dark, older ones light, and the map defaults to the last 7 days. The developer says the data is user-submitted, not guaranteed accurate, and tells you to check with your local DNR.

**Price:** free with ads on Google Play; Premium Supporter is $9.99 a year.

**Where it falls short:** last updated December 2024, 3.7 stars on Google Play and 3.2 on the App Store. Posting needs GPS and signal, and one long-standing Play review asks for an offline mode.

## 8. State DNR tools

**Best for:** official maps and the rules that apply.

- **Minnesota LakeFinder** (web, works on a phone): DNR depth maps, fish survey results and stocking reports for about 4,500 lakes. Free, and the survey data shows what's actually in a lake. The [best ice fishing lakes in Minnesota](/blog/best-ice-fishing-lakes-minnesota) list covers the big hardwater lakes.
- **Michigan DNR Hunt Fish** (iPhone and Android, free): buy and view your license, read the regulation digests and report harvest. It needs a connection for purchases and reports. The DNR also posts inland lake maps on its website. See [best ice fishing lakes in Michigan](/blog/best-ice-fishing-lakes-michigan).

Neither state's DNR measures or reports ice thickness on individual lakes. Their tools tell you what's in the lake and what you can keep, not whether the ice will hold.

## How to set up your phone for the ice

1. **Download offline maps at home** for every lake you might fish, on Wi-Fi.
2. **Airplane-mode test:** open each app with no connection. Whatever doesn't load at home won't load on the lake.
3. **Save waypoints before the trip** for accesses and the breaks you want to try, then save each productive hole.
4. **Keep the phone warm.** Cold drains batteries fast; an inside pocket and a power bank in the sled help.
5. **Log while you're still on the hole.** Depth, time and bait are what you'll want next season.

<p class="callout warn"><strong>Ice first:</strong> no app measures the ice under you. Test it yourself with a spud or auger.
