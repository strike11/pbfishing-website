Fishing Points is the app a lot of inshore anglers open before they hook up the trailer. Tides, wind and gusts, waves, solunar periods and nautical charts all sit on one screen. PB Fishing is built for something else: logging fish on US lakes, rivers and ponds, keeping your personal bests per species, and ranking them against your state.

So this isn't a fight between two versions of the same app. It's a question of which job matters more to you, and whether you need both. We make PB Fishing, which is why we start with the verdict and give Fishing Points its due.

## The 30-second verdict

- **Pick Fishing Points** if you fish saltwater or big water where tides, wind, waves and charts decide the trip, or if you want a private log with no social side.
- **Pick PB Fishing** if you fish inland water, want to know what lives in a lake before you go, and care about your PBs and how they rank in your state.
- **Use both** if you run tides in Fishing Points and log in PB Fishing. Both have free tiers.

## Logging catches

Both apps log catches, but they log for different reasons.

Fishing Points' catch log stores photos, weight and length, and adds weather, solunar and tide data automatically. It's private by design. It's a good log if your question is "what tide stage was I on when the reds were tailing?"

PB Fishing's log starts with a side-profile photo. The app suggests the species and estimates length from the photo, and you confirm or correct it. Date, water, weather and pressure attach on their own. If you didn't have a scale, the [fish weight calculator](/tools/fish-weight-calculator) estimates weight from length. Logging is unlimited on the free tier, and a catch made with no signal waits on your phone and uploads later, which helps on reservoirs where the bars drop to zero in the coves.

## PBs and leaderboards

Fishing Points doesn't do personal bests or leaderboards. That's not what it's built for.

PB Fishing is built around them. Every catch is checked against your best for that species, and the PB updates on its own. Each species has a state board ("Local") and a national board, reset every six months. An entry shows angler name, state, length and weight, never the lake. Each one has a photo behind it, so it's photo-backed, not tournament-judged. Free users see the top 10; Premium shows the full national board.

## Maps

Fishing Points has four map types: satellite, worldwide nautical charts, offline NOAA charts for boating, and layers for seagrass, oyster beds and mangroves. It lists 50,000+ boat ramps worldwide, records trolling paths and trotlines, imports GPX and KMZ files from your GPS, and shows water levels and flows for 35,000+ river stations. Lake depth and contour maps are available in six states: Florida, Minnesota, Nebraska, New Hampshire, North Dakota and Washington.

PB Fishing covers 20,000+ named US waters. Each has a page with species, seasonal activity, community ratings, live conditions and a bite forecast, a trip planner, tackle guidance and links to that state's official license and regulation pages. It's stronger for "what can I catch in this lake in October?" and weaker for "where's the drop-off?" There are no depth charts or nautical charts. At least one inland angler in Fishing Points' App Store reviews said most of their state's small lakes and rivers were missing; if that's your situation, PB Fishing's water pages are worth a look.

## Forecasts

Fishing Points shows hourly feeding times with major and minor periods, daily fish activity, a best-times calendar for bass and other species, hourly wind and gusts, a barometer with hourly pressure, rain radar and severe weather alerts. The free tier shows today; Premium extends fishing times, tides and sun and moon data to 3 months and weather to 7 days.

PB Fishing gives each water a free daily bite forecast. Premium adds an hourly forecast with odds by species and 10-day weather.

Treat any activity score as a reference. Anglers reviewing several forecast apps, Fishing Points included, report slow days on high-activity ratings and good days on poor ones. [Do solunar tables work?](/blog/do-solunar-tables-work) covers what the research says.

## Community

Fishing Points isn't a social app. You can share locations and catch photos with friends and sync across devices, and it says it connects 12 million anglers worldwide, but there's no public feed.

PB Fishing has a feed (For You and Following), 24-hour stories, likes and comments, and the leaderboards. The community is young. If you'd rather not see anyone else's fish, Fishing Points is the quieter app.

## Privacy

- **Fishing Points** keeps your catch log private, and you choose which locations you share and with whom. That's a real strength.
- **PB Fishing** leaderboard entries show your name and state, never the lake or coordinates. Feed posts are separate: watch photo backgrounds and captions.

## Where Fishing Points is better

- **Tides and marine weather.** Tides, currents, waves and sea temperature. PB Fishing has none of these.
- **Charts and navigation.** Offline NOAA nautical charts, 50,000+ boat ramps, distance measuring, compass.
- **Trolling and GPS.** Record trolling paths and trotlines, import GPX and KMZ.
- **Worldwide coverage.** PB Fishing covers US waters only.
- **Privacy by default.** No public feed at all.
- **Web app and track record.** Plan on a computer; on Google Play since 2013 with 10M+ installs. PB Fishing's Android app launched in November 2026.

## Where PB Fishing is better

- **Inland water detail.** 20,000+ named US waters with species and seasonal activity.
- **Automatic PBs per species.**
- **State and national leaderboards** with six-month seasons.
- **Free, unlimited, photo-first logging** that works with no signal.
- **Price.** $29.99 a year for full Premium against $49.99 for Fishing Points' Yearly Premium.

## Bottom line

On the coast, Fishing Points is the better tool, and we'd tell a redfish or snook angler to use it. On lakes, rivers and ponds, where your question is what bites here and whether you beat your PB, PB Fishing on [iPhone](/iphone) or [Android](/android) is the better fit.
