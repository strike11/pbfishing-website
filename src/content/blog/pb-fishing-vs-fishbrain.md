Fishbrain and PB Fishing get compared because both log catches and both have a map and a community. But they're built around different questions. Fishbrain answers "where are people catching fish?" PB Fishing answers "what's the best fish I've ever caught, and how does it stack up?"

We build PB Fishing. That's exactly why this page leads with where Fishbrain is the better app. If it fits you better, use it.

## The 30-second verdict

- **Pick Fishbrain** if you fish lots of unfamiliar water, want depth charts in the same app, use Android, or like a big active community.
- **Pick PB Fishing** if you want a free log with no catch limit, care about your personal bests per species, like the idea of a state leaderboard, and are on an iPhone.
- **Use both** if you scout with Fishbrain's free tier and keep your own record somewhere else.

## Where Fishbrain is better

Let's be clear about these, because they're real.

**Catch data.** This is Fishbrain's moat. Years of catches from a huge community means that on most popular lakes you can see what species people catch, roughly when, and on what. PB Fishing's water pages list species and seasonal activity, but they won't show you a stack of recent catch reports from that pond. If your main question is "what's biting at this lake this month," Fishbrain answers it better.

**Community size.** More anglers means more answers in groups, more reports from small waters, and more people near you. Our community is new. That will take time, and we won't pretend otherwise.

**Depth maps.** Fishbrain Pro includes Garmin Navionics depth charts for the US and Canada. PB Fishing has no depth charts. If you fish offshore structure, ledges or ice, you'll need Navionics or Fishbrain Pro either way.

**Android.** Fishbrain runs on Android. PB Fishing doesn't. If you're on Android, the rest of this page doesn't matter.

**Track record.** Fishbrain has been around for over a decade. Anglers who remember apps shutting down and taking their logs with them have a fair reason to weigh that.

## Where PB Fishing is better

**Price.** $29.99 a year against $79.99, or $3.99 a month against $12.99 to $14.99. The free tier isn't a teaser either: catch logging is unlimited and free.

**Your PBs are the main screen.** Every catch is checked against your best for that species, and the PB updates on its own. You don't have to dig through a logbook to remember your biggest crappie.

**Photo-first logging.** Take a side-profile photo, the app suggests a species and estimates length from the photo, and you confirm or correct it. Date, water, weather and pressure get attached. Weight goes in if you had a scale; if you didn't, our [fish weight calculator](/tools/fish-weight-calculator) gives a solid estimate from length.

**Leaderboards that don't burn your spot.** Each species has a state board and a national board. Seasons reset every six months, so a fish from three years ago doesn't sit on top forever. An entry shows your name, state, length and weight, not the lake or coordinates. Every entry has a photo behind it. That's photo-backed, not tournament-judged; we explain the difference in [fishing leaderboard apps](/blog/fishing-leaderboard-apps).

**No signal, no problem.** Log a catch on a dead-zone reservoir and it uploads when you're back in range.

**A cleaner start.** There's a feed with stories, likes and comments, and some anglers enjoy it. But the log and PBs come first, and you never have to open the feed.

## Privacy: what other people see

This matters more than most comparison pages admit.

- **Fishbrain** says you control how much location info you share, and offers private waypoints in Pro. Its community map is built from catch locations, so check your sharing settings before you post a fish from a small pond.
- **PB Fishing** leaderboard entries show your state, never the lake or coordinates. Posts in the community feed are a separate thing: what's in the photo background or your caption is up to you.

Whichever app you use, the safest habit is the old one: crop out landmarks and don't name small waters.

## Can you run both?

Yes, and lots of anglers do something like it. Use Fishbrain's free tier to see what's being caught on a new water, then log your own fish in the app you prefer. The only bad setup is paying for two apps that overlap.

<p class="callout"><strong>Tip:</strong> before you switch apps, screenshot your top catches and PBs from the old one. Re-entering ten fish takes five minutes and keeps your history intact.</p>

## Bottom line

Fishbrain is the better app for finding fish on unfamiliar water, and it's the one to pick on Android. PB Fishing is the better deal if what you care about is your own catches, your PBs and where they rank in your state, and you're on an iPhone.
