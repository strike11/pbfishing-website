"Good size" depends on where you fish and who you ask. A 3-pounder from a Minnesota lake is a great fish. The same bass at Lake Fork is a nice keeper. So instead of an opinion, start with the yardsticks biologists use, then look at real counts of big bass from the states that track them.

## The short answer: size categories biologists use

Fisheries biologists sort largemouth bass by length into five categories, set by Gabelhouse in 1984 and still used in survey reports across the country. The weights below come from the largemouth standard-weight equation (Henson 1991), which describes a well-fed fish. A thin fish of the same length will weigh less.

| Category | Total length | Standard weight | What anglers call it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stock | 8 in (20 cm) | about 4 oz | Dink |
| Quality | 12 in (30 cm) | about 14 oz | Keeper in many states |
| Preferred | 15 in (38 cm) | about 1 lb 13 oz | Good fish |
| Memorable | 20 in (51 cm) | about 4 lb 11 oz | Hawg, brag fish |
| Trophy | 25 in (63 cm) | about 9 lb 12 oz | Wall-hanger, double-digit territory |

So by the book, a **good size bass is 15 inches and up**, a **really good one is 20 inches and about 4½ to 5 pounds**, and anything near 25 inches is a trophy anywhere in the country. For the full inch-by-inch numbers, see the [largemouth bass weight chart](/blog/largemouth-bass-weight-chart).

## What the average bass actually looks like

Most bass you catch are smaller than the ones you remember. Tennessee's wildlife agency reports an average largemouth catch weight of about 2.5 pounds at Dale Hollow Reservoir, a good lake with a long history of big fish. In plenty of small lakes and ponds, a typical day is 10 to 14 inch fish with a 2-pounder as the kicker.

Two things drive the difference between lakes: how fast bass grow there, and whether the population carries Florida-strain genes.

## Growth rates: south vs north

Bass grow when the water is warm, so the growing season matters more than anything else.

| Where | Age 3 | Age 5 | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Texas ponds (typical) | 15 in | 18 in | Texas A&M AgriLife Extension |
| Lake Wisconsin, south-central Wisconsin | some fish reach 14 in | average just over 14 in | Wisconsin DNR survey, 2023 |

Put weights on those lengths and the gap is obvious. An 18-inch bass is about 3¼ pounds at standard weight; a 14-inch bass is about 1½. And Wisconsin's biologists noted that Lake Wisconsin bass grew *faster* than the state average, so many northern lakes are slower still.

That answers a common question: **how long does it take a bass to grow to 5 pounds?** Even in a Texas pond, a 5-pounder (about 20½ inches) is usually older than five. In the North it can take much longer, and in many lakes it never happens.

### Northern vs Florida bass

Genetics set the ceiling. Texas A&M's pond management guide puts the maximum size of a northern largemouth at about 10 pounds, while a large female Florida largemouth may reach 20 pounds. Texas A&M also notes that Florida bass grow faster in warm water but are more sensitive to cold. That combination is a big part of why the double-digit fish come from southern waters, not northern ones.

A rule of thumb that follows from the numbers:

| Region | Good bass | Brag fish | Fish of a lifetime |
|---|---|---|---|
| Upper Midwest, Northeast (northern strain) | 2–3 lb | 4–5 lb | 6 lb and up |
| Mid-South, Tennessee Valley | 3 lb | 5–6 lb | 8 lb and up |
| Florida, Texas, Deep South (Florida genetics) | 3–4 lb | 6–8 lb | 10 lb and up |

That table is a judgement call, not a statistic. It reflects the growth rates and size ceilings above.

## How rare is a 7-pound bass?

There's no national survey that says "X percent of bass are over 7 pounds," and you should be suspicious of anyone who quotes one. What does exist is good enough to give you a feel for it.

**What a 7-pounder looks like.** Using the standard-weight equation, a well-fed largemouth hits 7 pounds at about 22½ to 23 inches. A thinner fish needs to be closer to 24 inches.

**Texas survey data.** Biologists Steve Miranda and Darren Shoemaker reported in the Wildlife Management Institute's August 2025 bulletin that standard electrofishing surveys in 174 Texas reservoirs from 2018 to 2024 collected more than 31,000 largemouth bass of 8 inches or longer. Only 38 weighed 8 pounds or more. That's roughly **one bass in 800**, in one of the best big-bass states in the country. A 7-pounder is more common than an 8-pounder, but not by a lot.

**Texas ShareLunker.** Texas Parks and Wildlife has collected 13-pound-plus "Legacy Class" bass since 1986. In February 2026 the program logged ShareLunker numbers 686 and 687, a 14.35-pounder from Fort Phantom Hill and a 13.10-pounder from Toledo Bend. That's under 700 fish of 13 pounds or more in 40 seasons, from 79 different public lakes as of late 2025. In 2025 the program took 14 Legacy fish from nine lakes, six of them from O.H. Ivie. Year-round, ShareLunker accepts bass of 8 pounds or 24 inches as its entry level.

**Florida TrophyCatch.** Florida's program counts bass of 8 pounds and heavier that are documented with a photo on a scale and released. The state's current freshwater regulations guide reports more than 17,000 of them, including 176 Hall of Fame fish over 13 pounds, across 13 seasons. The same guide notes that participation among anglers has ranged from 6 to 42 percent, so many big fish never get entered.

Notice where both trophy programs set the entry bar: **8 pounds**, in the two states best known for giant bass. In the North, where bass top out around 10 pounds, a 7-pounder is a fish most anglers will never see.

If you've caught one, it's worth documenting properly. See [how to measure a fish](/blog/how-to-measure-a-fish) for the photo that holds up.

## World record and state record context

The largemouth world record is a tie at **22 lb 4 oz**:

- **George Perry**, Montgomery Lake, Georgia, June 2, 1932.
- **Manabu Kurita**, Lake Biwa, Japan, July 2, 2009. The IGFA announced in January 2010 that Kurita would share the all-tackle record.

For a sense of how far that is from normal, here are some state records:

| State | Record largemouth | Details |
|---|---|---|
| Texas | 18.18 lb | Caught in 1992 (Texas A&M AgriLife) |
| Tennessee | 15 lb 7.5 oz | Nickajack Reservoir, Darren Nunley, February 28, 2026 (TWRA) |

Tennessee's smallmouth record, 11 lb 15 oz from Dale Hollow Reservoir by D. L. Hayes on July 9, 1955, is still on the books, and TWRA's Dale Hollow page describes it as the world record. More state records are on the [Texas](/fishing/texas/state-record-fish), [Florida](/fishing/florida/state-record-fish) and [Tennessee](/fishing/tennessee/state-record-fish) record pages.

**Is a 30-pound largemouth possible?** Nobody has documented one. The standard-weight equation would put a well-fed 30-pounder at about 35 inches, far outside the data the equation was built on, and Texas A&M's estimate of the top end for a big Florida female is about 20 pounds. Treat 30-pound stories the way you'd treat a 30-pound crappie.

## What's a good size bass to eat?

Where harvest is legal, most anglers keep bass in the 12 to 15 inch range and let the big ones go. Those big fish are almost always older females, and in a lake where bass grow slowly, a 20-incher can be many years old. Check your state's minimum length, slot and bag limits, and look up the fish consumption advisory for the specific lake before you keep anything.

## The bottom line

- **Good:** 15 inches and up, or about 2 pounds.
- **Really good:** 20 inches, 4 to 5 pounds.
- **Rare:** 7 pounds and up, which is about 22½ to 23 inches on a healthy fish.
- **Trophy anywhere:** 25 inches, or the 8-pound line the trophy programs use.
