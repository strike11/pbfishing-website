"Best" on the ice means something different than it does in July. The lake you pick decides how you get out there: drive your own truck on a plowed road, ride a tracked shuttle to a rented house, or pull a sled from a public access. So this list ranks waters for what they give a hardwater angler: a reliable bite, a real access system, and rules you can understand before you drill.

Every limit, date and line rule below comes from the **2026 Minnesota Fishing Regulations** (in force March 1, 2026 to February 28, 2027), plus the DNR's winter announcements for Upper Red and Mille Lacs. The open-water view of the same lakes, with sizes and summer patterns, is on our [best fishing lakes in Minnesota](/fishing/minnesota/best-lakes) page.

## 1. Lake of the Woods

This is the default answer to "best ice fishing lake in MN," and the reasons are practical. Minnesota's share of the lake is about 300,000 acres, the walleye and sauger bite holds up all winter, and the access system is the most developed in the state. Resorts along the south shore and the Rainy River plow and maintain their own ice roads. You can pay a road fee and drive out with your own wheelhouse, rent a day house for daylight fishing, or book a sleeper house with bunks and a heater and fish around the clock. Mid and late winter, tracked vans and bombers shuttle anglers to houses miles out. Eelpout (burbot) are a common night catch, and the state's certified burbot record, 19 lb 10 oz, came from this lake in December 2016.

**Winter rules:** this is a Canada border water, so the walleye season runs May 9, 2026 to **April 14, 2027**, well past the inland closer. Keep 6 walleye and sauger combined, no more than 4 walleye, release every walleye from 19.5 to 28 inches, 1 over 28 allowed. Pike: 3, all from 30 to 40 inches released. Border waters normally allow 1 line, but **2 lines are legal when ice fishing**. Shelters must be off by **March 31, 2027**. Burbot limit is 4.

**Access tip:** stay on the marked road. Crews move routes as conditions change, so December's road may not be February's.

## 2. Upper Red Lake

Upper Red has been one of Minnesota's most dependable winter walleye lakes for years, and the DNR's own numbers back that up. Only the eastern 48,000 acres are public. The rest of the lake belongs to the Red Lake Nation and is closed to non-band members, and the boundary is a line on the map, not a fence. Know where it is before you set your GPS waypoint.

**Winter rules:** the DNR set the 2026-27 winter limit at **4 walleye, only 1 over 17 inches**, from Nov. 15, 2026 to Feb. 28, 2027. It's the fourth straight winter under that rule. The DNR's fisheries section manager said the fall assessment showed the population is in excellent shape, and the cap is lower than the 5-fish open-water limit because winter pressure is so heavy. Pike have a permanent rule: all from 30 to 40 inches released, possession 3, only 1 over 40. Shelters off by March 15, 2027.

**Access:** resorts and outfitters around the public shore run plowed roads and rent houses. Before the roads open, it's walk-out fishing, when ice checks matter most.

## 3. Mille Lacs

Mille Lacs is 1.5 to 2 hours from the Twin Cities, and the DNR describes its winter fishery as a fast walleye bite with tullibee and trophy pike through the ice. Being that close to the metro, it draws a crowd on winter weekends.

**Winter rules:** Mille Lacs is an intensive management lake, managed under treaties as well as DNR rules, and its walleye, pike, perch and tullibee rules are set separately. **The winter regulations start Dec. 1.** For 2025-26 the DNR set 3 walleye, each 17 inches or longer with only 1 over 20, from Dec. 1, 2025 to Feb. 22, 2026, and cut the perch limit to 10. The DNR sets the winter numbers after its fall netting, and at the time of writing (Nov. 23, 2026) the 2026-27 winter limits hadn't been published. Check mndnr.gov/millelacslake before your first trip. Culling isn't allowed on Mille Lacs, except for bass. Shelters off by **March 1, 2027**, because the lake sits south of the removal line.

## 4. Rainy River and Four Mile Bay

This is the late-ice bonus most people never use. Inland walleye close Feb. 28, but on the Rainy River (mouth to the International Falls dam) and Four Mile Bay, Lake of the Woods' southeast bay at the river mouth, **walleye and sauger are catch-and-release from March 1 to April 14** every year. Walleye stage in the river system ahead of the spring spawn, so March is a real shot at a personal best while the rest of the state is putting the auger away.

**Rules:** nothing can be kept during that window, so measure, photo and release fast. Watch the ice closely here. Moving water near the river mouth eats ice from below, and March sun does the rest. Shelters off by March 31, 2027 (Canada border waters).

## 5. Lake Winnibigoshish

"Winnie" is a big, shallow Mississippi headwaters reservoir 15 miles northwest of Deer River, with sandy flats and gravel bars. Through the ice it's best known for **yellow perch**, with walleye the bonus. Plan on drilling a lot of holes and moving until the electronics light up.

**Rules:** walleye from 18 to 23 inches must be released, only 1 over 23 in possession; sunfish daily limit 5. The rule covers connected waters such as Cut Foot Sioux. Statewide perch rules apply: 20 a day, 40 in possession. Shelters off by March 15, 2027.

## 6. Leech Lake

Leech's 112,000 acres hold walleye, perch, pike, muskie, crappie, sunfish and eelpout, and its many bays give you somewhere to fish out of the wind. Walker Bay and the Walker area are the usual winter starting point, and resorts around the lake rent houses and maintain access.

**Rules:** walleye possession 4, only 1 over 20 inches; crappie and sunfish daily limits are 5 each, lower than the statewide 10 and 20. Shelters off by March 15, 2027.

## 7. Cass Lake chain

Cass Lake and its connected lakes (Pike Bay, Wolf, Andrusia, Kitchi and others) add up to more than 25,000 acres between Leech and Winnie, which makes a three-lake winter trip easy. Walleye and perch are the usual targets through the ice.

**Rules:** sunfish daily limit 5 across the chain; otherwise statewide limits apply (6 walleye and sauger, only 1 walleye over 20; 10 crappie). Shelters off by March 15, 2027.

## 8. Lake Vermilion

Vermilion is a big, island-studded lake between Tower and Cook, with deep rock basins and shallow weedy bays. It gets less winter attention than the lakes above, but the DNR's species list (walleye, pike, crappie, bluegill, perch and bass) gives you options all season.

**Rules:** all walleye from 20 to 26 inches released, possession 4, only 1 over 26. Northeast zone pike rules apply: 2, all 30 to 40 inches released, only 1 over 40. Shelters off by March 15, 2027. Narrows between islands can carry current, so check ice as you go.

## 9. Kabetogama and Rainy Lake (Voyageurs National Park)

At Voyageurs, the federal government plows the ice road for you. The National Park Service maintains the **Rainy Lake Ice Road** from the Rainy Lake Visitor Center boat launch and the **Kabetogama Lake Ice Road** between the Ash River and Kabetogama Lake visitor centers. They're open to cars and trucks under 7,000 lb gross weight, the speed limit is 30 mph, snowmobiles aren't allowed on the road, and routes change year to year with conditions. Fish houses must sit at least 50 feet from the road and from snowmobile trails.

**Rules:** Kabetogama is an inland lake with a walleye slot (18 to 26 inches released, 1 over 26, walleye and sauger 6 combined, max 4 walleye); shelters off March 15, 2027. Rainy Lake is a Canada border water: 8 walleye and sauger combined, max 4 walleye, 18 to 26 inches released, 2 lines through the ice, shelters off March 31, 2027.

## 10. Lake Minnetonka

If you live in the metro, Minnetonka is the lake you can fish after work. It's a maze of connected bays about 16 miles west of Minneapolis, and you can catch crappie, sunfish, pike, bass and the occasional walleye without a long drive. Access is mostly from public launches and parks, by foot, sled or ATV once the ice allows.

**Rules:** no special listing in the 2026 booklet, so statewide limits apply: 10 crappie, 20 sunfish, 6 walleye and sauger, and north-central zone pike (10, all from 22 to 26 inches released, no more than 2 over 26). Shelters off by **March 1, 2027**. Minnetonka has channels and bridges between bays, and moving water under them keeps the ice thin there.

## 11. Lake Pepin

Pepin is the Mississippi's natural lake below Red Wing, and it's a Wisconsin border water with its own rulebook. Walleye and sauger season is continuous here, which is why it draws winter anglers from both states, along with panfish in the backwaters.

**Rules:** Pools 3 through 8, including Pepin, allow 4 walleye and sauger combined, 15-inch minimum on walleye, only 1 walleye or sauger over 20 inches. You can fish shore to shore with either a Minnesota or Wisconsin license, use 2 lines, and must be within **400 feet** of your tip-ups. With a Minnesota license, shelters on Minnesota-Wisconsin waters come off by March 1, 2027; the shelter rules of the state that issued your license apply. This is river ice with current under it, so it's never uniform.

## The rules that apply on every lake

- **Lines:** 2 through the ice (1 on designated trout lakes and streams). Stay within sight of your lines and within 200 feet of a tip-up.
- **Hook-setters:** new for the 2025-26 season and still legal, non-motorized spring or bent-rod hook-setting devices are allowed. They can't reel in a fish for you.
- **Shelter license:** required for every hard-sided house on inland and Canada border waters. A portable needs one only if it's left unattended between midnight and an hour before sunrise, or you're more than 200 feet away. $15 a year for residents, $37 for nonresidents (2026 booklet). Shelters need your name and address, driver's license number or DNR number in 2-inch letters, reflective material on each side if left overnight, and 10 feet between houses.
- **Litter:** garbage, dead fish and waste can't go on the ice outside your shelter. Bag it and secure it to the shelter or sled.
- **Walleye close:** inland walleye and pike close Feb. 28, 2027. Late-ice walleye after that means border waters.
- **Free ice weekend:** Take a Kid Ice Fishing Weekend, Jan. 16 to 18, 2027. Minnesota residents 16 and older fish without a license when they're with a child under 16.

## How to pick your lake

- **First trip, want walleye and a warm house:** a rental on Lake of the Woods or Upper Red. Someone else drills the holes and knows the road.
- **Weekend from the Twin Cities:** Mille Lacs for walleye and perch, or Minnetonka if you have a few hours.
- **Perch for the fryer:** Winnibigoshish or Leech, and bring a flasher.
- **Late-ice PB attempt:** Rainy River and Four Mile Bay in March, catch-and-release only.

On the big northern lakes, cell signal fades fast once you're a few miles out. If you keep a log, use one that saves offline.
