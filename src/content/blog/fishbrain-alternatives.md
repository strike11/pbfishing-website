People don't leave Fishbrain because it's a bad app. They leave because of one specific thing: the $79.99 yearly Pro price, a feed they never asked for, a honey hole that got crowded after a post, or maps they'd rather get from a dedicated chart app.

So the right alternative depends on why you're leaving. This list is sorted by that reason. One honest note first: no app on this list has as many community catch reports as Fishbrain. If the thing you open Fishbrain for is "what are people catching on this lake," the free version may still be worth keeping next to whatever you pick.

## 1. FishAngler

**If you're leaving because of the price.**

FishAngler is the closest thing to Fishbrain without the paywall up front. The free account covers fishing maps (access points, ramps, piers, reefs), a logbook with 45+ fields per catch, fish ID, angler groups, and built-in IGFA world and state records. Its listing also mentions property-line data, which is handy for bank anglers trying not to trespass. Reddit regulars tend to describe its paywall as unobtrusive.

**Cost:** free. VIP is $6.99 a month or $49.99 a year as of November 2026, and adds credits for its FINN guide and solunar bite windows.

**Trade-off:** it's still a social app with a crowd-sourced catch map, so it has the same feed noise and the same mis-tagged pins. You're changing the price, not the model.

## 2. Fishbox

**If you want forecasts and how-to content more than a community.**

Fishbox focuses on maps (it claims 50,000+ US waters), species-specific bite forecasts, a bait and rig finder, knot guides, live chat with fishing pros and an in-app assistant. Offline map tiles are a plus for remote water.

**Cost:** the US App Store lists Fishbox Premium purchases from $7.99 to $35.99, with several at $27.99. The listing doesn't label which is monthly and which is yearly, so read the paywall screen closely.

**Trade-off:** billing is the complaint you'll see most in its reviews, including anglers who say cancelling a trial was hard. If you subscribe on iPhone, the charge goes through your Apple account, so you can cancel in Settings, your name, Subscriptions. Fishbox doesn't have Fishbrain's catch-report volume either.

## 3. Infinite Livewell

**If you're leaving because of spot burning.**

Infinite Livewell is built around one idea: your data stays yours. It logs catches fast from an iPhone or Apple Watch, builds a private heat map of where you caught fish, and lets approved buddies see what you caught without showing them where, according to the developer's App Store description. It can blur the background of trophy photos so landmarks don't give away the spot, and it logs offline.

**Cost:** free, with no in-app purchases listed on the US App Store as of November 2026. On iPhone, Apple Watch, Mac and Android.

**Trade-off:** it's very new, with only a handful of App Store ratings so far. No community, no forecasts, no maps of public waters. That's the point, but it's a narrow tool.

## 4. Anglers' Log

**If you're leaving because of the feed.**

Anglers' Log is a straight journal: catches with photos, species, bait, gear, spot, water conditions and weather, plus stats on what produces. No feed. App Store reviewers like that it's purpose-built and ad-free. Pro adds automatic weather and tide data, GPS trails, custom fields, cloud backup and CSV export.

**Cost:** free. Pro is $1.49 a month or $11.99 a year as of November 2026, the cheapest paid tier we found.

**Trade-off:** you get a log, nothing more. No catch data from other anglers, no bite forecast.

## 5. MyCatch

**If you want a free log with a bit of friendly competition.**

MyCatch, from Angler's Atlas, logs catches with a fast catch-photo-release flow so the fish is back in the water in under a minute. It also runs fishing events with prizes and shares catch data with university fisheries researchers under agreements that, according to the company, protect anglers' locations. The company's pitch to anglers is that secret spots stay secret.

**Cost:** free, with no in-app purchases listed on the US App Store as of November 2026.

**Trade-off:** it's a log and an events app, nothing more. No forecasts, no depth maps, and its events may not run on your water.

## 6. PB Fishing

**If you want a log built around your personal bests.**

This is our app, so weigh this entry accordingly. PB Fishing puts the log and your PBs first. You photograph the fish side-on, the app suggests the species and estimates length, you confirm, and it saves date, water, weather and pressure. Your best fish per species updates automatically. If you want a little competition, species leaderboards run by state and nationally, reset every six months, and show your name, state, length and weight. They don't show which lake.

**Cost:** unlimited logging is free. Premium is $3.99 a month or $29.99 a year, with a 7-day trial on the yearly plan, billed by Apple.

**Trade-off:** iPhone only (iOS 17.6+), a young community, and no depth charts. There's a feed with likes and comments too, so if you want zero social features, Infinite Livewell or Anglers' Log are cleaner. The head-to-head is in [PB Fishing vs Fishbrain](/blog/pb-fishing-vs-fishbrain).

## 7. Navionics Boating

**If Fishbrain Pro's depth maps were the reason you paid.**

Fishbrain Pro's depth maps come from Garmin. Navionics Boating is Garmin's own chart app: SonarChart HD contours down to 1-foot detail, offline charts, NOAA charts in US waters, markers and tracks. For contour fishing and ice fishing it remains the standard.

**Cost:** $49.99 a year for US & Canada as of November 2026, $30 less than Fishbrain Pro's yearly list price.

**Trade-off:** no catch data and no community. Its App Store rating is 2.9 stars, and the complaints are mostly about price and features changing.

## 8. onX Fish

**If you fish the Midwest and want agency data instead of crowd pins.**

onX Fish pulls state survey data (species, size and catch-per-effort numbers) into lake pages with contour maps and GPS navigation. Instead of trusting a stranger's pin, you see what the state's biologists found in their nets. Reviewers like that there's no social clutter.

**Cost:** free version with Lake Finder, GPS navigation and offline maps. The Midwest region is $34.99 a year with a 7-day trial, as of November 2026.

**Trade-off:** Midwest only. If you're in Minnesota, Wisconsin or Michigan, it's excellent. If you're in Tennessee or Texas, it's not for you yet.

## 9. BassForecast, Fishing Points or Deep Dive

**If you mostly opened Fishbrain for BiteTime.**

- **BassForecast** gives bass anglers a daily rating built from pressure, wind, water temperature and solunar timing, plus lure picks by season and cover. $5.99 a month or $29.95 a year. Also on Android and the web.
- **Fishing Points** is the pick for tides, wind and marine weather, with offline NOAA charts. Yearly Premium is $49.99; a Basic Premium tier is listed at $29.99.
- **Deep Dive** is the serious bass option: tournament patterns, satellite water-clarity maps for 170+ lakes, lake level and flow. Pro is $79.99 a year (monthly listed from $9.99 to $19.99); a Plus tier is $4.99 a month or $39.99 a year.

**Trade-off:** a forecast is a summary of conditions, not a promise. Some of the best days on the water get rated poorly. Our piece on [whether solunar tables work](/blog/do-solunar-tables-work) explains what's behind the scores.

## How to switch without losing your history

Before you cancel anything, save what you've already logged.

1. **Screenshot or export your catch list** while you still have access. Pay special attention to PBs, dates and the lures that worked.
2. **Note your private waypoints** by hand or in your new app. Coordinates are the hardest thing to rebuild.
3. **Cancel the subscription**, not just the app. On iPhone: Settings, your name, Subscriptions.
4. **Re-enter your top 10 catches** in the new app so your PBs carry over.

<p class="callout"><strong>Tip:</strong> whatever app you land on, the most useful record is your own: date, water temperature, wind, depth and what bit. See <a href="/blog/fishing-log-what-to-record">what to record in a fishing log</a>.</p>

## Which one should you pick?

- **Leaving because of price:** FishAngler.
- **Leaving because of spot burning:** Infinite Livewell or Anglers' Log.
- **Leaving because of the feed but want your PBs front and center:** PB Fishing (iPhone) or Anglers' Log (iPhone and Android).
- **Leaving but need depth charts:** Navionics, or onX Fish in the Midwest.
- **Not sure Pro is the problem:** read [Is Fishbrain worth it?](/blog/is-fishbrain-worth-it) first. The free version might be enough.
