BassForecast and PB Fishing cost almost exactly the same per year, $29.95 against $29.99, so price won't settle it. What settles it is what you want your phone to do on a bass trip. BassForecast answers the question before you leave: is today a good day, and what should I tie on? PB Fishing answers the question after: what did I catch, is it a new PB, and where does it rank in my state?

An r/bassfishing angler described BassForecast as useful "for guys like me who haven't memorized all the patterns yet." That's exactly who it serves well. We make PB Fishing, so here's the plain verdict first and then where BassForecast is better.

## The 30-second verdict

- **Pick BassForecast** if you fish mostly bass, want a daily rating and a 10-day outlook, and like being told which baits match the season and the cover.
- **Pick PB Fishing** if you want a free log for every species, PBs that update on their own, and a state leaderboard to chase.
- **Use both** if you plan with BassForecast and log with PB Fishing. Both have free tiers, and paying for both is $59.94 a year.

## Logging catches

BassForecast has a catch log: location, weather, ratings and photos, so you can see which conditions produced your best fish. It's a solid add-on to a forecast app.

PB Fishing is a log first. Snap a side-profile photo; the app suggests the species and estimates length from the photo, and you confirm or correct it. Date, water, weather and pressure attach automatically. If you didn't have a scale, the [largemouth bass weight chart](/blog/largemouth-bass-weight-chart) or the [fish weight calculator](/tools/fish-weight-calculator) estimates weight from length. Logging is free and unlimited, any species, and catches made with no signal upload later.

## PBs and leaderboards

This is the clearest split. BassForecast's listing doesn't mention personal bests or leaderboards.

PB Fishing checks every fish against your best for that species and updates the PB automatically. Each species has a state board ("Local") and a national board, reset every six months. An entry shows angler name, state, length and weight, with a photo behind it. It never shows the lake. That's photo-backed, not tournament-judged. Free users see the top 10; Premium shows the full national board. If you're chasing a bigger bass, [how to catch a personal best bass](/blog/how-to-catch-a-personal-best-bass) is the companion read.

## Maps

BassForecast has interactive lake maps across the US to help you spot structure and cover before you launch, and you can search any lake, river or pond for its forecast.

PB Fishing covers 20,000+ named US waters. Each water page lists species, seasonal activity, community ratings, live conditions and a bite forecast, with a trip planner, tackle guidance and links to the state's license and regulation pages. Neither app replaces a depth chart; for contours you'll want Navionics or a sonar map card.

## Forecasts

BassForecast wins this section. Its Bass Forecast Rating rolls weather, barometric pressure trends, water temperature, GPS-based solunar timing and bass behavior into a daily score for your spot, and flags GOOD and EPIC days. PRO opens the full 10-day outlook. The bait recommendations go deeper than most apps: 22 bait categories across 9 bass seasons, matched to conditions and to cover type (grass, wood or rock). A topwater walker at dawn, a football jig on offshore ledges, a hollow-body frog over matted grass: that's the level it works at.

PB Fishing gives every water a free daily bite forecast for all species. Premium adds an hourly forecast with odds by species and 10-day weather. It doesn't do bass-specific bait picks by cover type.

Either way, no rating guarantees fish. A front can flip a good day, and some of the best bites come on days the apps call tough. [Fishing after rain, wind and a cold front](/blog/fishing-after-rain-wind-cold-front) explains why.

## Community

BassForecast isn't a social app. It says 1.6 million anglers use it, and Google Play shows 500K+ installs, but there's no public feed on its listing.

PB Fishing has a feed (For You and Following), 24-hour stories, likes and comments, plus the leaderboards. The community is young, and you never have to open the feed to use the log.

## Privacy

- **BassForecast's** listing describes the catch log as your personal database and mentions no public catch sharing.
- **PB Fishing** leaderboard entries show your state, never the lake or coordinates. Feed posts are separate, so mind your photo backgrounds and captions on small waters.

## Where BassForecast is better

- **Bass-specific planning.** A daily rating and a 10-day outlook built for bass.
- **Bait recommendations by season and cover.** PB Fishing's tackle guidance is per water, not this detailed.
- **Web app and Mac.** Plan at your desk.
- **Partner perks** in PRO.
- **Track record.** On the App Store and Google Play since 2016. PB Fishing is newer, and its Android app launched in November 2026.
- **Price per year**, by four cents.

## Where PB Fishing is better

- **Every species**, not just bass.
- **Automatic PBs** and **state and national leaderboards** that show your state, not your lake.
- **Fast photo logging**, free and unlimited, that works with no signal.
- **Cheaper monthly**: $3.99 against $5.99.
