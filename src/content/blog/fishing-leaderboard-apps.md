Every angler has heard the joke that all fishermen are liars. It sticks because fish stories are hard to check. Online leaderboards exist to fix that: put the fish on a board, take the photo, let someone else read the number.

There are two kinds of online fishing competition, and they solve different problems. **Tournament apps** run judged events with entry fees, prizes and strict rules. **Season leaderboards** run all year, cost nothing to join, and are about bragging rights and beating your own best. This guide covers how both work, how cheating gets caught, and which apps to look at.

## How an online catch-photo-release tournament works

Kayak fishing made online tournaments popular because you can't bring a livewell full of bass to a weigh-in from a kayak. So the fish gets measured and photographed on the water, then released. The format is often called CPR: catch, photo, release.

A typical event runs like this:

1. **Register** in the tournament app and pay the entry fee.
2. **Get the event identifier**: an ID card or code that's only valid for that event.
3. **Catch a fish** and lay it on an approved measuring board (a bump board) with the jaw against the fence.
4. **Photograph it** with the identifier in the frame, following the photo rules.
5. **Submit** the photo through the app, often right from the water.
6. **A judge reviews it**, reads the length, and accepts or denies it.
7. **Standings update** live. Many events score a limit of your longest fish, often five.

TourneyX, for example, automatically culls your smallest fish when you submit a bigger one, so you don't have to track your limit by hand.

## How cheating gets caught

Online tournaments run partly on the honor system, and anglers know it. The rules exist to make cheating hard and easy to spot. Kayak Bass Fishing (KBF), one of the best-known kayak trails, publishes some of the most detailed rules in the sport. Here's what they check, as of November 2026.

### Approved measuring boards

KBF accepts only specific boards: the aluminum Ketch board, the Ketch Karbonate and the Ketch X. A board can only be shortened by cutting inches off the end opposite the fence, never the middle, and deliberately bending it is prohibited. That rule exists for a reason. In April 2016, an angler at a Kayak Bass Series event on Dale Hollow Reservoir, on the Kentucky and Tennessee line, was removed from competition after allegedly cutting sections out of his boards that the fish's body hid, making each fish read longer than it was, according to Louisiana Sportsman.

### Event identifiers

Every photo needs the official ID card for that event, placed next to the fish, not on it. KBF doesn't accept hand-drawn codes or codes written on your hand. No card, the wrong card or a fake card, and the fish is denied. The identifier proves the photo was taken during that event, not last spring.

### Photo rules

KBF's photo rules, in short:

- Fish facing left, whole fish from mouth to tail in frame.
- Jaw touching the fence, and the fence visible.
- **Mouth closed.** An open mouth costs half an inch, and a wide-open one can get the fish denied.
- Camera directly above the fish, not at an angle.
- No fingers over the eye or inside the gill plate.
- Your kayak visible in the background.
- First-generation photos only, no screenshots or photos of photos.

### Judges and rounding

A judge reads every fish. KBF scores to the quarter inch and rounds down: if the tail falls between two marks, you get the lower one. A blurry photo gets the highest mark the judge can read with no doubt, or gets denied.

### The heavy tools

Submitting the same fish twice on purpose gets you banned. KBF can ask any competitor to take a polygraph, at random or after an event, and refusing is grounds for disqualification. Cheating findings mean a permanent ban from KBF events. Other apps add their own layers: FishDonkey lists date- and time-stamped photos and video, boat check-ins and livewell checks among its anti-cheating tools.

The newest worry is generated images. As one r/bassfishing commenter put it: "We're now at the point where people will use AI to fake their catches." Identifier cards and live submission are a big part of why tournament photos are still trusted.

## Tournament apps worth knowing

Prices and details below are from each app's US App Store listing, November 2026.

### TourneyX PRO

TourneyX says hundreds of tournament directors around the country use its system. Directors build events and pay TourneyX a flat fee or a fee based on the number of anglers, with no monthly contract. Anglers register, submit fish, and can enter several tournaments at once with one submission. Judging is manual: the director accepts or denies every fish. The app itself is free; you pay each event's entry fee.

### FishDonkey

FishDonkey lets anyone create a tournament for free, with anglers paying a small fee when they join. It supports length or weight, brackets, multi-day events, points races, divisions (kids, kayak, boat, co-angler) and ice and shore events. It lists date- and time-stamped photos, anti-cheating tools and offline submission. Its listing names local leagues and high school tournament trails among its uses.

### Krooka

A newer tournament app with very few US ratings so far. Organizers buy a package by event size: $24.99 for up to 30 participants, $44.99 for up to 75, or $79.99 for unlimited, with Premium plans listed separately.

### MyCatch events

MyCatch, from Angler's Atlas, runs catch-photo-release fishing events with prizes inside its free logging app. Its catch data also feeds university fisheries research.

## Season leaderboards: a different idea

A tournament lasts a day or a weekend. A season leaderboard runs for months and is open to anyone who logs a fish. No director, no entry fee, no check at the end. What you get instead is a running answer to "how does my fish compare to everyone else's in my state?"

That's the format PB Fishing uses. Here's exactly how it works:

- **Boards by species**, for each state ("Local") and nationally.
- **Seasons reset every six months**, for example "Season H1 2026," so the board stays winnable and a monster from years ago doesn't sit on top forever.
- **Every entry is photo-backed.** You log the fish with a side-profile photo; the app helps estimate length from it, and you enter weight if you weighed it.
- **An entry shows your name, state, length and weight.** It doesn't show the lake or coordinates, so climbing the board doesn't mean giving up your honey hole.
- **Free accounts see the top 10**; Premium ($29.99 a year) shows the full national board.

What it isn't: a judged tournament. There's no event code and no director reading a bump board to the quarter inch. Photo-backed means there's a photo behind every number, and that's a real step up from "trust me." But if prize money is on the line, you want a tournament app with judges.

## Tournament app vs season leaderboard

| | Tournament app (TourneyX, FishDonkey) | Season leaderboard (PB Fishing) |
|---|---|---|
| Cost to enter | Entry fee per event | Free to log and rank; full national board in Premium |
| Length | A day to a few weeks | Six-month seasons |
| Measuring | Approved bump board required | Photo-backed length, weight if you weighed it |
| Proof | Event ID card in every photo | Catch photo with every entry |
| Review | Judge accepts or denies each fish | No tournament judge |
| What's public | Standings and photos per event rules | Name, state, length, weight; no lake |
| Prizes | Usually | None advertised |
| Best for | Competitive anglers, clubs, trails | Personal goals, state bragging rights, year-round motivation |

## How to make your catch photo hold up anywhere

Whether you fish a $50 tournament or just want your PB to stand, the same habits help:

1. **Use a real measuring board**, not a soft tape laid over the fish's curve. Soft tapes add length.
2. **Mouth closed, jaw against the fence**, fish lying flat on its side.
3. **Shoot straight down** from above the middle of the fish.
4. **Get the whole fish and the tail** in the frame, with the numbers readable.
5. **Be quick.** Have the board wet and ready so the fish is out of the water for seconds, not minutes.

Our guide on [how to measure a fish](/blog/how-to-measure-a-fish) goes into total length, pinching the tail and which boards tournaments accept. No scale on the boat? The [fish weight calculator](/tools/fish-weight-calculator) turns length into a solid weight estimate.

<p class="callout"><strong>Tip:</strong> if you plan to fish tournaments, practice the photo routine at home with a dummy fish or a ruler. Most denied fish come down to an open mouth, a missing code or a jaw off the fence.</p>

## Which one is for you?

If you want prize money, real competition and a judge, sign up for a TourneyX or FishDonkey event in your area. If you want a reason to keep chasing a bigger fish all year, see where you stand in your state, and keep your spots to yourself, a season leaderboard does that without the entry fee.
