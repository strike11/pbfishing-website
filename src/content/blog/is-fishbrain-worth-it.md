Fishbrain is the biggest fishing app in the US by a wide margin, and the question people type into Google is not "is it good" but "is it worth paying for." That's fair. At $79.99 a year, Pro costs more than a decent rod.

The short answer: Fishbrain Pro pays off if you regularly fish water you don't know and would use the depth maps. If you fish the same handful of lakes and mostly want a record of what you caught, the free version (or a different app) does the job.

Here's the long answer.

## What Fishbrain Pro costs in 2026

These are the in-app purchases shown on Fishbrain's US App Store listing as of November 2026:

| Plan | Listed price | Per month |
|---|---|---|
| Annual Power Fishing Package | $79.99 | about $6.67 |
| Quarter Power Fishing Package | $41.99 | about $14.00 |
| Monthly Power Fishing Package | $12.99 or $14.99 | $12.99 to $14.99 |
| Fishbrain Premium (listed separately) | $14.99 | n/a |

Fishbrain's own Pro page offers a **3-day free trial**. Three days is short. If you start it on a Thursday night, it renews before you're off the water on Sunday.

Anglers on r/FishingForBeginners talk about only buying during discount promotions, and you'll sometimes see a lower first-year price inside the app. We couldn't confirm a current promotion, so plan around the list price. Whatever the in-app screen says, the Apple confirmation sheet shows the real amount and renewal date before you double-click to pay.

For context, a $79.99 year is more than Navionics Boating's US and Canada charts ($49.99 a year) and more than double the yearly price of most log-focused apps.

## Free vs Pro: what you actually get

The app is free to download and use. Fishbrain lists these features on its App Store page and Pro page:

| Feature | Free | Pro |
|---|---|---|
| Log catches with weather and time | Yes | Yes |
| Community feed, groups, comments | Yes | Yes |
| Fishing map with waters and recent catch photos | Yes, without exact catch positions | Yes |
| Exact catch locations | No | Yes |
| Garmin HD depth maps (US and Canada) | No | Yes |
| Bait and lure recommendations | No | Yes |
| Local regulations | No | Yes |
| Private waypoints | No | Yes |

The app description also lists BiteTime forecasts, tides, pressure, moon phases and a fish ID tool for 300+ species, without spelling out which parts are free. The two Pro features that carry the price are **exact catch locations** and **depth maps**. Everything else is nice to have.

## What anglers praise

Read enough reviews and Reddit threads and the same four things come up.

**It tells you what lives in a water.** This is the reason people keep Fishbrain installed. Moved to a new city, staring at a map full of ponds? Fishbrain shows which ones produce bass, which ones are full of bluegill, and how hard the popular ones get hit. No other app has that much catch data.

**Automatic conditions in the log.** Every catch gets a time stamp, weather and pressure. People who use it as a digital fish journal like being able to search past catches by species or location.

**Depth maps without a second subscription.** If you'd otherwise pay $49.99 for Navionics, getting Garmin charts inside Fishbrain makes the $79.99 easier to swallow.

**The PB moment.** Some reviewers credit the app with helping them land a personal best. Logging every fish makes you pay attention to what worked.

## What anglers complain about

The negative side is just as consistent.

**The paywall moved.** Long-time users say catch locations used to be free and now sit behind Pro. When the community supplies the data and then pays to see it, people get annoyed.

**Catch pins aren't always honest.** Some anglers tag the wrong lake on purpose to protect their spot. Others pin a catch to the nearest named water miles away. One r/FishingForBeginners commenter summed it up: "A lot of people purposely mistag places not to give their spot away." So a hot pin on a map is a hint, not proof.

**Spot burning.** The flip side of a big catch map: post a good fish from a small pond and the pond gets crowded. Fishbrain says you control how much location info you share, and it has privacy settings. Check them before you post, not after.

**It's a social network.** Some anglers want a tool, not a feed full of other people's kids holding bluegill. You can ignore the feed, but it's the first thing you see.

**Upsells and billing.** Pop-ups to subscribe, trials that roll into a yearly charge, and Fish ID calls that miss. None of that is unique to Fishbrain, but it's where most 1-star reviews in this category land.

## Who should pay for Fishbrain Pro

- **You fish lots of new water.** Traveling anglers, people who just moved, kayakers who hit a different lake every weekend. Exact catch locations save scouting time.
- **You'd buy depth maps anyway.** If you were about to pay for Navionics, Pro bundles Garmin charts with the catch data.
- **You like the community.** If the feed and groups are fun for you, that's real value too.
- **You'll actually open it weekly.** At $6.67 a month on the annual plan, it's cheap if you use it. It's expensive if you open it six times a year.

## Who shouldn't

- **You fish the same few waters.** You already know what lives there. Your own log will beat anyone's pins.
- **You mainly want a record of your catches.** Free Fishbrain logs catches, and so do several apps that don't push a feed. Our [best fishing log apps](/blog/best-fishing-log-apps) list covers them.
- **You don't want your spots anywhere near a public map.** Use a private log and keep sharing off.
- **You're a beginner on a budget.** Spend the $80 on a license, line and a few [lures that work by season](/blog/best-bass-lures-by-season).

If the log and your personal bests are what you care about, that's the job PB Fishing was built for: unlimited free logging, an automatic PB per species, and Premium at $29.99 a year. It's iPhone only and its community is much smaller than Fishbrain's, so it won't replace Fishbrain's catch data. We lay that trade-off out in [PB Fishing vs Fishbrain](/blog/pb-fishing-vs-fishbrain).

## How to get the most out of the 3-day trial

If you're on the fence, use the trial with a plan instead of letting it run out by accident.

1. **Start it the night before a scouting day**, not a random Tuesday.
2. **Set a phone reminder for 48 hours later.** Apple charges within 24 hours before the renewal time.
3. **Pick three waters you're curious about** and check exact catch locations, species mix and depth maps for each.
4. **Write down what you learned** in your own notes. That information stays useful after the trial ends.
5. **Decide on day two.** If you didn't open it twice, you won't open it for a year.

<p class="callout warn"><strong>Check first:</strong> deleting the app does not cancel a subscription. On iPhone go to Settings, tap your name, then Subscriptions. On Android use Google Play, Payments and subscriptions.</p>

## How to cancel Fishbrain Pro

**On iPhone or iPad:** open Settings, tap your name, tap Subscriptions, choose Fishbrain and tap Cancel Subscription. If there's no cancel button, it's already cancelled. You keep Pro until the end of the period you paid for.

**On Android:** open the Google Play Store, tap your profile icon, then Payments and subscriptions, Subscriptions, Fishbrain, Cancel.

If you were charged for a renewal you didn't want, request a refund through Apple (reportaproblem.apple.com) or Google Play. Fishbrain can't refund App Store charges directly.

## The verdict

Fishbrain is a good app with a big, real advantage: more catch data than anyone else. Pro is worth $79.99 a year if you'll use exact catch locations and depth maps on water you don't know. For everyone else, the free version plus a dedicated log, or a cheaper app built around the one job you need, is the better deal.
