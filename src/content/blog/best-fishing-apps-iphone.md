Most "best fishing app" lists are written as if every phone is the same. On iPhone, three things decide whether an app fits you before you ever look at features: does it run on your iOS version, does it do anything useful on your Apple Watch or in CarPlay, and how easy is it to cancel when the trial ends.

We read the US App Store listing for every app below on November 16, 2026, including the compatibility section and the in-app purchase list. Prices change and sales come and go, and Apple shows the current price on the confirmation sheet before you pay, so check it there too.

We build PB Fishing and we rank it #1. Below is why, along with its limits and an honest "best for" for every other app.

## 1. PB Fishing

**Best for:** logging every catch free and watching your personal best per species go up.

We put PB Fishing first because it does the job most anglers actually want from a phone on the water, and doesn't charge for it. Unlimited catch logging is free forever. You take a side-profile photo, the app suggests the species and estimates length from the photo, and you confirm or fix it. Date, water, weather and barometric pressure are saved with the catch. No signal? The catch is stored on your iPhone and uploads later. Your PB for each species updates on its own.

The leaderboards are the other reason. Each species has a state ("Local") board and a national one, seasons reset every six months, and an entry shows your name, state, length and weight. It never shows the lake, so you can rank in your state without giving up your honey hole. There's also a map of 20,000+ named US waters with live conditions, a bite forecast and links to each state's official license pages.

**Price:** free tier includes unlimited logging, 5 photo IDs, a top-10 leaderboard preview, a daily bite forecast and 2 planned trips. Premium is $3.99 a month or $29.99 a year (7-day trial on yearly, about $2.50 a month), billed by Apple.

**Where it falls short:** it's an iPhone app, not an iPad one, and needs iOS 17.6 or newer. No Apple Watch app. The community is young, so on many waters Fishbrain has far more catch reports, and there are no depth charts. More on the app at [PB Fishing for iPhone](/iphone).

## 2. Fishbrain

**Best for:** scouting new water by seeing what other anglers catch there.

With 77K ratings at 4.7 stars, Fishbrain is the most-reviewed app on this list. Its listing covers depth charts from Garmin Navionics and C-Map, BiteTime forecasts, a fish ID tool, private waypoints and regulations for 30+ US states. On popular lakes, the catch reports tell you which species and baits are working.

**Price:** free to download. The Power Fishing Package is $12.99 or $14.99 a month, $41.99 a quarter or $79.99 a year. Fishbrain's Pro page offers a 3-day trial.

**Where it falls short:** it requires iOS 18, and there's no Apple Watch app. The yearly price is steep for weekend use, and crowd-sourced pins are often off on purpose. See [Is Fishbrain worth it?](/blog/is-fishbrain-worth-it)

## 3. Navionics Boating

**Best for:** depth contours and navigation on a boat or kayak.

SonarChart HD bathymetry with 1-foot contours, NOAA charts, offline use, tracks and markers. If you're hunting a hump or a creek channel swing, this is still the reference. It runs well on an iPad, which makes a decent big-screen chart on a small boat.

**Price:** $49.99 a year for US & Canada after a short trial.

**Where it falls short:** 2.9 stars from 2.4K ratings, with complaints about price and changing features. Needs iOS 18.

## 4. FishAngler

**Best for:** a generous free account with a community catch map.

The free account gives you maps with access points, ramps and piers, a 45-field logbook, fish ID, groups and built-in IGFA and state records. It holds 4.8 stars from 14K ratings, one of the best scores in the category.

**Price:** free. VIP is $6.99 a month or $49.99 a year and adds bite windows, exact catch locations, Garmin Navionics charts and no ads.

**Where it falls short:** it's social first, so the feed and notifications are busy.

## 5. Fishing Points

**Best for:** tides, wind and marine weather, especially inshore.

Tides, solunar periods, hourly wind and gusts, pressure, wave forecasts, offline NOAA charts and 50,000+ boat ramps, with 4.7 stars from 31K ratings. It still supports iOS 16, so it runs on an iPhone 8 or X.

**Price:** free basic version. Yearly Premium is $49.99, and a Basic Premium tier is $29.99.

**Where it falls short:** freshwater depth maps only in six states, and no Apple Watch app on the current listing.

## 6. Fishbox

**Best for:** iPhone anglers who want a forecast on their wrist and Lock Screen.

Fishbox has the most Apple-specific features on this list: an Apple Watch app, Lock Screen Live Activities for live fishing info, and an iMessage extension. It also offers maps of 50,000+ US waters, species bite forecasts, bait and rig guides, offline map tiles and live chat with fishing pros.

**Price:** the US App Store lists Fishbox Premium purchases from $7.99 to $35.99, without labeling which is monthly or yearly.

**Where it falls short:** reviews often complain about cancelling trials. Check the renewal date on Apple's confirmation sheet before you start one.

## 7. BassForecast

**Best for:** bass anglers who want a daily rating and bait picks.

A 10-day bass rating built from weather, pressure, water temperature and solunar timing, bait picks from 22 categories across 9 bass seasons, a cover selector for grass, wood or rock, and a catch log. Your account syncs with the web and Android.

**Price:** $5.99 a month or $29.95 a year.

**Where it falls short:** bass only, and good forecast days still fish badly sometimes. More picks for bass anglers are in [best bass fishing apps](/blog/best-bass-fishing-apps).

## 8. onX Fish

**Best for:** Midwest anglers who drive to new lakes.

State survey data (species, size distribution, catch per effort, stocking), contour maps, private land lines and offline maps. On iPhone it connects to Apple CarPlay, so you can navigate to an access site or waypoint from the dash.

**Price:** free version with the Lake Finder, GPS and offline maps. The Midwest region is $34.99 a year with a 7-day trial.

**Where it falls short:** Midwest and Montana only, and it needs iOS 18.

## 9. ANGLR

**Best for:** logging the whole trip, including route, waypoints and lure changes.

ANGLR records your GPS route, 15+ waypoint types, catches with conditions, and a tackle box. Its Apple Watch app uses three core screens so your phone can stay in your pocket. It supports iOS 15.5, the oldest requirement on this list.

**Price:** free core app. Purchases listed at $2.99, $29.99 (Member) and $39.99 (Pro).

**Where it falls short:** 4.2 stars from 636 ratings, and reviewers report crashes during trips.

## 10. Infinite Livewell

**Best for:** logging from your Apple Watch and keeping every spot private.

Log a catch with a swipe on the Watch or iPhone, build a private heat map, and let approved buddies see what you caught but not where. Photos stay on your device unless you share them, and there's a background blur for trophy shots.

**Price:** free, no in-app purchases listed.

**Where it falls short:** very new, with 2 App Store ratings, and the Watch app needs watchOS 26.2. No forecasts or public maps.

## iPhone-specific things to check

- **Your iOS version.** PB Fishing needs iOS 17.6; Fishbrain, Navionics and onX Fish need iOS 18. iPhone 8 and iPhone X stop at iOS 16, so on those phones look at Fishing Points, BassForecast, ANGLR, Anglers' Log, MyCatch or Deep Dive.
- **Watch support.** Only Fishbox, ANGLR and Infinite Livewell list an Apple Watch app. The rest are phone only on the wrist side.
- **Widgets.** Fishbox's listing mentions Lock Screen Live Activities. None of the other descriptions we read mention a Home Screen widget, so don't buy expecting one.
- **Mac and iPad.** FishAngler, Fishing Points and BassForecast can run on Apple silicon Macs; handy for planning at a desk.

<p class="callout warn"><strong>Check first:</strong> the App Store confirmation sheet shows the renewal price and date before any trial starts. Screenshot it. To cancel: Settings, your name, Subscriptions.</p>

## Also worth a spot on your iPhone

Anglers' Log ($11.99 a year for Pro) is the cleanest ad-free journal. Fish Rules shows saltwater limits offline. And your state agency app, like Fish|Hunt FL or Texas Outdoor Annual, is the only place to carry an official license, though both sit around 3.4 to 3.6 stars. Screenshot your license before you lose signal, and see [what to record in a fishing log](/blog/fishing-log-what-to-record) if you're starting one.
