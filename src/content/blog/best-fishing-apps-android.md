Search "fishing app" on an Android phone and Google Play hands you a wall of look-alike icons, half of them with a paywall on the first screen. The good news: every app worth using in 2026 is on Android. The catch: the Android versions don't always match what iPhone reviews describe. Ratings differ, some carry an ads label, and car integration runs through Android Auto instead of CarPlay.

This list is built for Android. For each app you get what's free, what works without signal, its Google Play rating, and what it costs. We opened every Google Play listing on November 16, 2026. Google Play doesn't print subscription prices on the store page, so the dollar figures below come from the US App Store listing for the same app. Treat them as a reference and read the Play paywall before you pay.

We make PB Fishing, and it's #1 here. We explain why below, and we're just as plain about its limits as we are about everyone else's.

## 1. PB Fishing

**Best for:** logging every catch for free and seeing your personal best per species climb, with a state leaderboard if you want a little competition.

PB Fishing landed on Google Play in November 2026 (version 1.0.0), with the same Premium plan as the iPhone app. We put it first on Android for four reasons. The core job, a photo-backed catch log, is free with no catch limit. Your PB for each species updates on its own, so "what's my biggest walleye?" is one tap, not a scroll through 300 photos. The leaderboards run by state and nationally, reset every six months, and an entry shows your name, state, length and weight, never the lake. And Premium costs $29.99 a year, less than half of Fishbrain Pro's $79.99.

How it works: you take a side-profile photo, the app suggests the species and estimates length from the photo, and you confirm or fix it. Date, water, weather and barometric pressure are saved with the catch. If you're on a farm pond with no bars, the catch is stored on the phone and uploads when you get signal back. There's also a map of 20,000+ named US waters with species lists, live conditions, a bite forecast and links to each state's official license pages.

**Free on Android:** unlimited catch logging, 5 photo IDs, the top-10 leaderboard preview, a daily bite forecast and the trip planner for up to 2 trips.

**Price:** Premium is $3.99 a month or $29.99 a year in the US (7-day free trial on yearly), billed by Google Play. It adds unlimited photo IDs, the hourly forecast with species odds, the full national leaderboard, catch insights, advanced stats and 10-day weather.

**Where it falls short:** it's brand new on Android, with no Google Play ratings yet, and the community is young, so many waters have few catch reports compared with Fishbrain. No depth charts. If you fish structure, pair it with Navionics.

## 2. Fishbrain

**Best for:** seeing what other anglers are catching on a lake you've never fished.

On Google Play, Fishbrain has 10M+ downloads and a 4.5-star rating from 67.8K reviews. Its value is volume: on busy waters you can see which species are biting, which baits show up in catch posts, and how hard a spot gets fished. The Android listing mentions depth maps from Garmin Navionics and C-Map, BiteTime forecasts, a fish ID tool and license information for 30+ states.

**Price:** free to download. The US App Store lists Pro at $79.99 a year, $41.99 a quarter, or $12.99 to $14.99 a month. Fishbrain's Pro page offers a 3-day trial. Pro adds exact catch locations, bait recommendations, Garmin HD depth maps, regulations and private waypoints.

**Where it falls short:** the Android listing carries the "Contains ads" label. The price is high for weekend use, and crowd-sourced pins are only as honest as the people dropping them. Our breakdown is in [Is Fishbrain worth it?](/blog/is-fishbrain-worth-it)

## 3. Fishing Points

**Best for:** saltwater and big-water anglers who plan around tides, wind and marine weather.

Fishing Points is huge on Android: 10M+ downloads and 221K Google Play reviews at 4.4 stars, about seven times its iPhone review count. It puts tides, solunar periods, hourly wind and gusts, pressure, wave forecasts and water levels for 35,000+ river stations in one app, with offline NOAA nautical charts and 50,000+ boat ramps.

**Price:** free basic version. On the US App Store, yearly Premium is $49.99 and a Basic Premium tier is $29.99. The Play listing says it contains ads.

**Where it falls short:** freshwater lake depth maps cover only Florida, Minnesota, Nebraska, New Hampshire, North Dakota and Washington, per its own listing. Some reviewers call the free tier thin.

## 4. FishAngler

**Best for:** a big free tier with a community catch map, access points and property lines.

FishAngler's free account covers fishing maps with access points and ramps, a logbook with 45+ fields per catch, fish ID, angler groups and state records. Property-line data helps bank anglers stay legal. Its Play listing says your catch log and location stay private by default unless you share them.

**Price:** free. VIP is $6.99 a month or $49.99 a year on the US App Store, and adds bite windows, exact catch locations, Garmin Navionics charts, private waypoints and no ads.

**Where it falls short:** the free version shows ads, and its Google Play rating (4.2) runs below its iPhone rating (4.8). It's social first, so expect a busy feed.

## 5. Navionics Boating

**Best for:** depth contours, navigation and finding structure from a boat or kayak.

Garmin's chart app gives you SonarChart HD bathymetry with 1-foot contour detail, NOAA charts, tracks, markers, GPX import and export, and offline charts.

**Price:** $49.99 a year for US & Canada on the US App Store, after a short free trial.

**Where it falls short:** 2.9 stars from 48.9K Google Play reviews, the same score it has on iPhone. Complaints center on price and features changing. People still pay because no other app matches its lake coverage. The Play listing also carries the ads label.

## 6. BassForecast

**Best for:** bass anglers who want a daily rating and lure suggestions.

BassForecast's rating rolls weather, pressure trend, water data and moon timing into a 10-day forecast with "Epic" and "Good" days, plus a catch log and up to 10 favorite spots to compare. One account works on Android, iPhone and the web, so you can plan on a laptop and check at the ramp.

**Price:** $5.99 a month or $29.95 a year on the US App Store. Lure recommendations are a Pro feature.

**Where it falls short:** bass only, and a forecast is a summary, not a promise. Our piece on [barometric pressure and fishing](/blog/barometric-pressure-and-fishing) explains what the numbers mean.

## 7. onX Fish

**Best for:** Midwest anglers who want state survey data instead of crowd pins.

onX Fish covers Minnesota, Wisconsin, Michigan, North Dakota, Indiana, Ohio, Illinois, South Dakota, Iowa, Missouri and Montana. Lake pages show species, size distribution, catch-per-effort survey numbers and stocking history, with 3D maps and private land boundaries. On Android it connects to Android Auto for hands-free navigation to access sites.

**Price:** the free version includes the Lake Finder, GPS navigation, offline maps and aerial imagery. The Midwest region is $34.99 a year with a 7-day trial.

**Where it falls short:** nothing south of Missouri. If you fish Texas or Tennessee, skip it for now.

## 8. Anglers' Log

**Best for:** a private, ad-free journal and nothing else.

Anglers' Log logs catches with photos, species, bait, gear, spot and conditions, pins spots on a map and shows which baits and times produce. It has 100K+ Android downloads and 4.6 stars. Pro adds automatic weather and tide data, GPS trails, cloud backup and CSV export.

**Price:** free. Pro is $1.49 a month or $11.99 a year on the US App Store.

**Where it falls short:** no community, no forecast, no leaderboards. For many users, that's the appeal. More logs are compared in [best fishing log apps](/blog/best-fishing-log-apps).

## 9. Fishbox

**Best for:** anglers who want forecasts, rig guides and live chat with fishing pros in one app.

Fishbox lists maps of 50,000+ US waters, species bite forecasts, a bait finder, knot tutorials and offline map tiles. 1M+ Android downloads, 4.6 stars.

**Price:** US App Store purchases run from $7.99 to $35.99, and the listing doesn't label which is monthly or yearly. Read the paywall carefully.

**Where it falls short:** billing and trial cancellation are the top complaints in its reviews.

## 10. Fish Rules

**Best for:** saltwater bag limits, size limits and seasons.

Fish Rules covers state and federal saltwater rules from Maine to Texas, plus California, Hawaii and the Caribbean. It uses GPS to show the rules where you are and works offline.

**Price:** free with a $29.99 ProStaff purchase on the US App Store. Contains ads on Android.

**Where it falls short:** 3.2 stars on Google Play, saltwater only, and its own disclaimer says the rules shown have no legal force. Your state agency is the final word.

## Android-only things to know

- **Cancelling.** Android subscriptions bill through Google Play. Open the Play Store, tap your profile, then Payments & subscriptions, then Subscriptions. Uninstalling the app does not cancel it.
- **The ads label.** Google Play marks apps that show ads. Fishbrain, FishAngler, Fishing Points, Navionics and Fish Rules carry it as of November 2026. PB Fishing, Anglers' Log, onX Fish, BassForecast, Fishbox and MyCatch don't.
- **Ratings differ by platform.** FishAngler is 4.8 on iPhone and 4.2 on Android. Fishing Points has far more Android reviews than iPhone ones. Read the Play reviews, not an iPhone roundup.
- **Install from Google Play only.** Skip "free premium" APKs from other sites. They're a common malware route.
- **Download maps on Wi-Fi.** Offline charts in Navionics, onX and Fishbox can take a lot of storage. Grab them at home, then test in airplane mode.

<p class="callout"><strong>Tip:</strong> before a trip into dead zones, turn on airplane mode at home and open each app you rely on. Whatever doesn't load there won't load at the lake either.</p>

## Also on Android

MyCatch (free, 10K+ downloads) logs catches fast for catch-photo-release and runs fishing events. Deep Dive brings tournament bass patterns to Android too, at about $80 a year for Pro. Infinite Livewell, a privacy-first log, is on Google Play too, though it shows just 100+ installs so far. And almost every state agency has a free Android app for carrying your license; check [do you need a fishing license for catch and release](/blog/do-you-need-a-fishing-license-for-catch-and-release) if you're unsure what you need.

## Which Android setup makes sense

- **Bank angler, $0 budget:** PB Fishing or Anglers' Log for your log, FishAngler's free map to scout, your state's license app.
- **Weekend bass angler:** PB Fishing for PBs, Navionics for contours, BassForecast if you want a daily read.
- **Inshore saltwater:** Fishing Points for tides and wind, Fish Rules for limits, a log for your own patterns.
- **Midwest lakes:** onX Fish's free version first, then decide if the $34.99 region is worth it.
