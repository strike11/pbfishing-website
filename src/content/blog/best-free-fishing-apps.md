In the low-star fishing app reviews we read on the App Store, more than half were about money. A trial that rolled into a $79.99 yearly charge. An app that let you log three fish and then asked for a card. A community map that was free until the data got good, then went behind a subscription. Anglers on r/FishingForBeginners put it bluntly: "everything else seems to be hidden behind the pro version."

So this list does one thing other roundups don't: for each app, it spells out what you get at $0 and what's paywalled. We read every in-app purchase list on the US App Store and checked each Google Play listing for the "Contains ads" label on November 16, 2026.

We make PB Fishing, and it's #1 here because unlimited catch logging is free forever. The rest of the list is ranked on how much real fishing you can do without paying.

## 1. PB Fishing

**Free forever:** unlimited catch logging, automatic personal bests per species, 5 photo identifications, the top-10 leaderboard preview, a daily bite forecast and the trip planner for up to 2 trips.

That's not a trial. You can log your 500th bluegill on the free tier. Snap a side-profile photo, the app suggests the species and estimates length, you confirm, and it saves the date, water, weather and barometric pressure with the catch. Logged with no signal? It's stored on the phone and uploads later. Your PB per species updates by itself, and you can see where your fish sits on the top 10 of your state and national species boards. Entries show name, state, length and weight, not the lake.

**What Premium adds:** unlimited photo IDs, an hourly bite forecast with species odds, the full national leaderboard, catch insights and trends, advanced stats, predictive hotspots, unlimited trips, and 10-day weather and catch history. $3.99 a month or $29.99 a year, with a 7-day trial on yearly.

**Honest limits:** after 5 photo IDs you pick the species yourself on the free tier. The community is young, so you won't find Fishbrain's volume of catch reports, and there are no depth charts. On iPhone and Android.

## 2. FishAngler

**Free:** the most generous free community tier we found. Maps with access points, boat ramps, piers and kayak launches, a logbook with 45+ fields per catch, fish ID for 860+ species, angler groups, property-line data, IGFA and state records, and a 7-day forecast with tides and moon phases.

**Paywalled (VIP):** FINN credits, solunar bite windows, bait and lure intel, exact GPS catch locations, 3D lake maps and Garmin Navionics charts, a 14-day forecast, private waypoints and an ad-free app. $6.99 a month or $49.99 a year.

**Honest limits:** the free version shows ads, and the catch map has the same honesty problem as every crowd map. Reddit regulars still describe its paywall as unobtrusive.

## 3. MyCatch

**Free:** all of it. MyCatch, from Angler's Atlas, lists no in-app purchases on the US App Store. You get quick catch-photo-release logging, stats like catch rate and species counts, and fishing events with prizes. Catch data can help fisheries researchers, and the founder's stated promise is that secret spots stay secret.

**Honest limits:** no forecasts, no depth maps, and events may not run near you. 4.8 stars from 251 App Store ratings.

## 4. Anglers' Log

**Free:** a full journal with photos, species, bait, gear, spot and conditions, map pins and stats on what produces. No catch limit, no feed, no ads label on Google Play.

**Paywalled (Pro):** automatic weather, atmosphere and tide data, live GPS trails, custom fields, cloud backup and CSV export. Pro is $1.49 a month or $11.99 a year, the cheapest paid tier in the category.

**Honest limits:** on the free tier you type the weather in yourself.

## 5. Infinite Livewell

**Free:** all of it, with no in-app purchases listed. Swipe-fast logging on iPhone or Apple Watch, a private heat map filtered by species and season, buddy alerts that show what you caught but not where, and trophy photos with background blur. Photos stay on your device unless you share them.

**Honest limits:** it's very new (2 App Store ratings) and does one job. No forecasts, no public maps.

## 6. Fishbrain (free tier)

**Free:** a logbook with personal stats, the community feed with local catch reports, and the basic map. For a lot of anglers that's enough.

**Paywalled (Pro):** per Fishbrain's own Pro page, exact catch locations, bait and lure recommendations, Garmin HD depth maps, local regulations and private waypoints. Pro is $79.99 a year, $41.99 a quarter or $12.99 to $14.99 a month, with a 3-day trial.

**Honest limits:** the free app carries Google Play's ads label, and the best part, where fish are being caught, is the part behind Pro. Our full take: [Is Fishbrain worth it?](/blog/is-fishbrain-worth-it)

## 7. onX Fish (free version)

**Free:** the Lake Finder, GPS navigation, offline maps, map layers and aerial imagery, with no subscription. That alone is useful for finding access points in the Midwest and Montana.

**Paywalled:** the Midwest region's survey data (size distribution, catch per effort, stocking history, trophy-potential filters) for $34.99 a year, with a 7-day trial.

**Honest limits:** if you don't fish the Midwest or Montana, there's nothing here for you yet.

## 8. Fish Rules

**Free:** saltwater bag limits, size limits, seasons and gear rules for state and federal waters from Maine to Texas, plus California, Hawaii and the Caribbean. It finds your rules by GPS and works offline. App Store reviewers credit it with saving them from fines.

**Paywalled:** a ProStaff purchase at $29.99. The listing doesn't spell out exactly what it adds, so look before you buy.

**Honest limits:** saltwater only, 3.2 stars on Google Play, and its disclaimer says the rules have no legal force.

## 9. Fishing Points (free basic)

**Free:** a basic forecast and the map.

**Paywalled:** the App Store lists Premium as covering nautical charts, forecasts, tides and moon data. Basic Premium is $29.99 and yearly Premium $49.99.

**Honest limits:** some reviewers call the free tier thin. If you want tides and marine forecasts, plan on paying. It's still the best paid pick for saltwater.

## 10. Your state agency app

**Free:** your license on your phone and the official regulations. Fish|Hunt FL and Texas Outdoor Annual are two examples; almost every state has one.

**Honest limits:** ratings in the 3s and spotty offline support. Screenshot your license before you leave signal. Not sure what you need? Start with [do you need a fishing license for catch and release](/blog/do-you-need-a-fishing-license-for-catch-and-release).

## Free to download, not free to use

These are good apps, but don't install them expecting a free tier you'll live on:

- **Navionics Boating:** a limited free trial, then $49.99 a year for US & Canada.
- **Deep Dive:** a free 1-week trial, then Pro at $79.99 a year or Plus at $39.99 a year.
- **BassForecast:** you can use it free, but the full 10-day forecast and all bait recommendations are in Pro at $29.95 a year.
- **Fishbox:** purchases from $7.99 to $35.99, and the paywall comes early.

<p class="callout warn"><strong>Check first:</strong> before tapping any free trial, read the renewal price and date on the Apple or Google Play confirmation sheet. On iPhone, cancel in Settings, your name, Subscriptions. On Android, Play Store, profile, Payments & subscriptions, Subscriptions.</p>

## A $0 setup that covers most anglers

- **Your log and PBs:** PB Fishing, or Anglers' Log if you want zero social features.
- **Scouting new water:** FishAngler's free map, or onX Fish's free version in the Midwest.
- **Rules:** your state agency app, plus Fish Rules on the coast.

That's three free apps and no subscription. If you later find you open one every trip, then pay for that one. And whatever you use, the best free data you'll ever get is your own record. Our guide on [what to record in a fishing log](/blog/fishing-log-what-to-record) shows how to make it pay off.
